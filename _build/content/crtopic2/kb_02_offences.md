## 4.2 Force, criminal force and assault — ss. 349 to 358

1. **Force** (s. 349) — causing motion, change of motion or cessation of motion to another, or to any substance so as to affect his body or feelings.
2. **Criminal force** (s. 350) — intentionally using force on a person **without consent**, in order to commit an offence or intending to cause **injury, fear or annoyance**.
3. **Assault** (s. 351) — a **gesture or preparation** intended or known likely to cause a person present to **apprehend** that criminal force is about to be used against him. **Mere words do not amount to assault**, but words may give a gesture that meaning (*illus.*: shaking a fist; unloosing the muzzle of a ferocious dog).
4. **s. 354** — assault or criminal force to a **woman** with intent to **outrage her modesty** — up to 2 years.
5. **s. 354A** — assault or criminal force to a woman and **stripping her of her clothes** and exposing her to public view — **imprisonment for life** and fine (the death penalty was **removed in 2025**).

# Chapter 5 · Rape, gang rape and sexual abuse

## 5.1 Rape — s. 375 (as substituted in 2016 and 2021)

"A person **A** is said to commit rape if A —

(a) **penetrates his penis**, to any extent, into the vagina, mouth, urethra or anus of another person **B**, or makes B do so with A or any other person; or
(b) **inserts**, to any extent, **any object or a part of the body**, not being the penis, into the vagina, urethra or anus of B, or makes B do so; or
(c) **manipulates** any part of the body of B so as to cause penetration; or
(d) **applies his mouth** to the vagina, anus, urethra or penis of B, or makes B do so,

under the circumstances falling under any of the following **seven descriptions**:

1. **against B's will**;
2. **without B's consent**;
3. with consent obtained by putting B or a person in whom B is interested in **fear of death or hurt**;
4. with consent, when A knows he is **not B's husband** and B consents believing A is another man to whom B is or believes herself to be lawfully married;
5. with consent given when, by reason of **unsoundness of mind, intoxication** or the administration of a **stupefying or unwholesome substance**, B is unable to understand the nature and consequences;
6. **with or without consent when B is under sixteen years** of age; or
7. when B is **unable to communicate consent**."

The definition is **gender-neutral as to the victim** and covers far more than penile-vaginal intercourse.

## 5.2 Gang rape — s. 375A

Where a person is raped by one or more persons **constituting a group** or **acting in furtherance of a common intention**, each of them is guilty of **gang rape** — **death**, or imprisonment for the **remainder of natural life**, or **imprisonment for life**, and fine.

# Chapter 6 · Kidnapping and abduction (ss. 359 to 374)

## 6.1 Definitions

1. **Kidnapping** is of two kinds (s. 359): **from Pakistan** and **from lawful guardianship**.
2. **Kidnapping from Pakistan** (s. 360) — conveying any person **beyond the limits of Pakistan** without his consent or that of a person legally authorised to consent for him.
3. **Kidnapping from lawful guardianship** (s. 361) — **taking or enticing** any **minor under fourteen** (male) or **under sixteen** (female), or any person of **unsound mind**, **out of the keeping of the lawful guardian**, **without the guardian's consent**. "Lawful guardian" includes any person lawfully entrusted with the care or custody. *Exception:* a person who in good faith believes himself to be the **father of an illegitimate child**, or entitled to its lawful custody — unless for an immoral or unlawful purpose.
4. **Abduction** (s. 362) — whoever **by force compels**, or by any **deceitful means induces**, any person to **go from any place**.

# Chapter 7 · Offences against property

![Offences against property — the family tree](property_offences.png)

## 7.1 Theft — s. 378 (LHC 2023)

> "378. Whoever, **intending to take dishonestly** any **moveable property** out of the **possession of any person** **without that person's consent**, **moves that property** in order to such taking, is said to commit theft."

### Ingredients

1. **Dishonest intention** (s. 24 — to cause wrongful gain or wrongful loss) — **at the time of taking**;
2. **Movable property** — including things severed from the earth (Expl. 1: a tree becomes movable as soon as severed);
3. taken **out of the possession** of another — possession, not ownership, is protected; **an owner** can commit theft of his own property from a person in lawful possession (e.g., from a pledgee — *illus. (j)*);
4. **without consent** — express or implied (Expl. 5); consent obtained by deceit is no consent;
5. **moving** the property in order to such taking — moving includes removing an obstacle, separating it, or moving an animal (Expl. 2–4).

*Illustrations:* A cuts down a tree on Z's land intending to take it — theft as soon as it is severed; A entices Z's dog to follow him; A drives Z's bullock carrying treasure — theft of the treasure as soon as the bullock moves; a servant who runs away with his master's plate commits **theft** (the plate was in the master's possession), but a warehouse-keeper who sells goods entrusted to him commits **criminal breach of trust**, not theft.

**Punishment:** theft — up to **3 years** (s. 379); theft in a **dwelling house** or building for custody of property — up to **7 years** (s. 380); by a **clerk or servant** — up to 7 years (s. 381); theft of a **car or other motor vehicle** (including a motorcycle, tractor, a tube-well motor or transformer) — up to **7 years** and fine up to the vehicle's value (s. 381A); theft after **preparation** for causing death, hurt or restraint — up to **10 years** (s. 382). (Theft **liable to hadd** — Topic 4.)

## 7.2 Extortion — s. 383

"Whoever **intentionally puts any person in fear of any injury** to that person or any other, and thereby **dishonestly induces** the person so put in fear **to deliver** to any person any property or valuable security, or anything signed or sealed which may be converted into a valuable security." Punishment — up to **3 years** (s. 384); in fear of **death or grievous hurt** — up to **10 years** (s. 386).

{w:22,39,39}
|Point|Theft (s. 378)|Extortion (s. 383)|
|Property|**Movable** only|**Any** property, including **immovable**, and valuable securities|
|How taken|**Without consent**|With **consent** obtained by **putting in fear**|
|Delivery|The offender **takes**|The victim **delivers**|
|Force / fear|Not an element|**Fear of injury** is the essence|

## 7.3 Robbery and dacoity — ss. 390, 391

1. **Robbery** (s. 390) — in all robbery there is **either theft or extortion**. **Theft is robbery** if, in order to commit it, or in committing it, or carrying away or attempting to carry away the property, the offender **voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death, hurt or restraint**. **Extortion is robbery** if the offender, at the time, is **in the presence** of the person put in fear, and commits extortion by putting him in fear of **instant** death, hurt or wrongful restraint, and so induces him to deliver **then and there**.
2. **Dacoity** (s. 391) — when **five or more persons conjointly** commit or attempt robbery, or the whole number committing, attempting and **present and aiding** amounts to five or more.

|Point|Robbery (s. 390)|Dacoity (s. 391)|
|Number|One or more|**Five or more**, conjointly|
|Punishment|**3 to 10 years** RI and fine; on the **highway** up to **14 years** (s. 392)|**Life** or **4 to 10 years** RI and fine (s. 395)|
|With murder|Qatl and robbery punished separately|**Every** dacoit: **death**, life, or 4–10 years (s. 396)|

**Aggravations:** voluntarily causing hurt in robbery — life or 4–10 years for all jointly concerned (s. 394); **deadly weapon**, grievous hurt or attempt to cause death — **not less than 7 years** (ss. 397, 398); belonging to a **gang of dacoits** — life or up to 10 years (s. 400). (Haraabah — Topic 4.)

## 7.4 Criminal misappropriation — ss. 403, 404

**Dishonestly misappropriating** or converting to one's own use any movable property — up to **2 years** (s. 403). The property comes into the offender's possession **innocently** (finding, mistake) and the dishonesty arises **later**. A **finder** who appropriates property knowing or having means of discovering the owner, or before using reasonable means to find him, commits this offence (Expl. 2). Property of a **deceased** person — up to 3 years; by a clerk or servant of the deceased — up to 7 years (s. 404).

## 7.5 Criminal breach of trust — s. 405

"Whoever, being in any manner **entrusted with property**, or with any **dominion over property**, **dishonestly misappropriates** or converts to his own use that property, or dishonestly **uses or disposes** of it in violation of any **direction of law** prescribing the mode in which the trust is to be discharged, or of any **legal contract**, express or implied, … commits criminal breach of trust." An **employer** who deducts the employee's contribution to a provident or pension fund and does not deposit it is deemed to have committed it (Explanations).

**Punishment:** up to **7 years** (s. 406); by a **carrier, wharfinger or warehouse-keeper** — 7 years (s. 407); by a **clerk or servant** — 7 years (s. 408); by a **public servant, banker, merchant, factor, broker, attorney or agent** — **imprisonment for life** or up to 10 years (s. 409).

## 7.6 Receiving stolen property — ss. 410 to 414

"Stolen property" is property whose possession has been transferred by **theft, extortion, robbery**, criminal misappropriation or criminal breach of trust (s. 410). Dishonestly **receiving or retaining** it, **knowing or having reason to believe** it to be stolen — up to **3 years** (s. 411); property stolen in a **dacoity** — life or up to 10 years (s. 412); **habitually** dealing — life or up to 10 years (s. 413).

## 7.7 Cheating — s. 415

"Whoever, by **deceiving** any person, **fraudulently or dishonestly induces** the person so deceived **to deliver any property** to any person, or to consent that any person shall retain any property, or **intentionally induces** the person so deceived to **do or omit** to do anything which he would not do or omit if he were not so deceived, and which act or omission causes or is likely to cause **damage or harm** to that person in body, mind, reputation or property, is said to cheat." Dishonest concealment of facts is deception (Explanation).

**Punishment:** cheating — up to **1 year** (s. 417); cheating a person whose interest the offender was bound to protect — 3 years (s. 418); **cheating by personation** (s. 416) — up to **7 years** (s. 419); **cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property** or making or destroying a valuable security — up to **7 years** and fine (s. 420).

|Point|Criminal breach of trust (s. 405)|Cheating (s. 415)|
|How property comes|**Lawfully entrusted** — the initial possession is **innocent**|Obtained by **deception** — **fraud from the start**|
|When dishonesty arises|**After** entrustment (subsequent misappropriation)|At the **inception** of the transaction|
|Victim's act|Entrusts for a purpose|Is **induced** to deliver or act|

## 7.8 Mischief — ss. 425 to 440

"Whoever, with **intent to cause, or knowing that he is likely to cause, wrongful loss or damage** to the public or any person, causes the **destruction** of any property, or any such **change** in any property or in its situation as **destroys or diminishes its value or utility**, or affects it injuriously, commits mischief." The offender need not intend loss to the **owner** (Expl. 1), and a person can commit mischief of his own property (Expl. 2). **Punishment** varies with the damage and the means — e.g., causing damage of Rs. 50 or more — up to 2 years (s. 427); **by fire or explosive** — 2 to 7 years (s. 435); by fire to destroy a **house, place of worship or dwelling** — life or 3 to 10 years (s. 436).

## 7.9 Criminal trespass and house-breaking — ss. 441 to 462 (ASJ 2025)

![Criminal trespass — from simple to aggravated](trespass_ladder.png)

1. **Criminal trespass** (s. 441) — **entering** into or upon property in the **possession of another** with **intent to commit an offence**, or to **intimidate, insult or annoy** any person in possession; **or**, having **lawfully entered**, **unlawfully remaining** there with such intent. Punishment — up to **3 months** (s. 447).
2. **House-trespass** (s. 442) — criminal trespass by entering into or remaining in any **building, tent or vessel used as a human dwelling**, or any building used as a **place for worship** or for the **custody of property**. Introducing **any part of the body** is entering (Expl.). Punishment — up to **1 year** (s. 448); to commit an offence punishable with **death** — life or up to 10 years (s. 449); with **life** — up to 10 years (s. 450); after **preparation for hurt** or assault — up to 7 years (s. 452).
3. **Lurking house-trespass** (s. 443) — house-trespass **having taken precautions to conceal** it from a person who has a right to exclude the trespasser.
4. **Lurking house-trespass by night** (s. 444) — the same **after sunset and before sunrise**.
5. **House-breaking** (s. 445) — house-trespass in which the offender effects his **entrance or departure** in any of **six ways**: (1) through a passage **made by himself**; (2) through a passage **not intended for human entrance** (e.g., a window, a chimney) that he reaches by climbing; (3) through a passage he has **opened** which was not intended to be opened; (4) by **opening a lock**; (5) by **using or threatening criminal force** or assault; (6) through a passage he knows to have been **fastened against entrance** and which he has **unfastened**. Opening a door latched from inside is house-breaking.
6. **House-breaking by night** (s. 446) — after sunset and before sunrise.

**Punishments:** lurking house-trespass or house-breaking — up to 2 years (s. 453); to commit an offence punishable with imprisonment — up to 3 years, and if the offence is **theft**, up to **10 years** (s. 454); **by night** — up to 5 years, and for **theft** up to **14 years** (s. 457); by night **after preparation for hurt** — up to **14 years** (s. 458); persons **jointly concerned** where one causes qatl or hurt by night — life or up to 10 years (s. 460).

|Term|Key element|
|Criminal trespass|Entry or remaining on **property** in another's possession with intent to offend, intimidate, insult or annoy|
|House-trespass|The same, into a **dwelling, worship place or store-building**|
|Lurking house-trespass|House-trespass + **concealment**|
|House-breaking|House-trespass + **one of six forced or unusual modes of entry or exit**|
|"By night"|Between **sunset and sunrise** — more severe punishment; private defence of property extends to **death** against **house-breaking by night** (s. 103)|

## 7.10 Dishonestly issuing a cheque — s. 489F (SHC 2022)

> "Whoever **dishonestly issues a cheque** towards **re-payment of a loan or fulfilment of an obligation** which is **dishonoured on presentation**, shall be punishable with imprisonment which may extend to **three years**, or with fine, or with both, **unless he can establish**, for which the **burden of proof shall rest on him**, that he had **made arrangements with his bank** to ensure that the cheque would be honoured and that the **bank was at fault** in not honouring the cheque."

**Ingredients:** (1) **issuance** of a cheque; (2) **dishonestly** (s. 24); (3) towards **repayment of a loan** or **fulfilment of an obligation** — a cheque given as a gift or as a mere **security** may not qualify; (4) **dishonour** on presentation. **Defence:** arrangements with the bank and the bank's fault — the burden is on the accused.

**Bail:** the offence is **non-bailable** but punishable with only up to **3 years**, so it falls **outside the prohibitory clause** of CrPC s. 497(1) (death, life or ten years). In such offences **grant of bail is the rule and refusal an exception** (*Tariq Bashir v. The State*, PLD 1995 SC 34; for s. 489F see *Muhammad Sultan v. The State*, 2010 SCMR 806); refusal needs exceptional grounds (likelihood of absconding, tampering, repetition). A **dispute** about the quality of goods (as in the SHC 2022 problem — the drawer stopped payment because the goods were defective) shows the matter may be **civil** and raises a case of **further inquiry** under s. 497(2) — bail should ordinarily be **granted**, often against a surety.

# Chapter 9 · Defamation (ss. 499 to 502) — KPHC 2026

## 9.1 Definition — s. 499

> "Whoever by **words** either spoken or intended to be read, or by **signs** or by **visible representations**, **makes or publishes any imputation** concerning any person **intending to harm**, or **knowing or having reason to believe** that such imputation **will harm, the reputation** of such person, is said, except in the cases hereinafter excepted, to defame that person."

### Ingredients

1. **Making or publishing an imputation** concerning a person — publication means communication to **at least one person other than** the person defamed;
2. by **words** (spoken — slander; written — libel), **signs** or **visible representations** (cartoons, effigies, videos);
3. with **intention** to harm, or **knowledge / reason to believe** that it will harm, his **reputation**.

### Explanations

1. **Deceased person** — an imputation may be defamation if it would harm his reputation if living and is intended to hurt his family or near relatives.
2. **Company or association** — an imputation concerning a company or a collection of persons may be defamation.
3. **Alternative or ironical** imputations may amount to defamation.
4. **Harm to reputation** — no imputation harms a person's reputation unless it, directly or indirectly, in the estimation of others, **lowers his moral or intellectual character**, or his character in respect of **caste or calling**, or his **credit**, or causes it to be believed that his body is in a **loathsome** or disgraceful state.

## 9.2 The ten exceptions

{w:8,30,62}
|1|**Truth for the public good**|Imputing anything **true**, if it is for the **public good** that it be made or published — truth alone is **not** enough (unlike English civil law); whether it is for the public good is a **question of fact**|
|2|**Public conduct of public servants**|An opinion expressed in **good faith** on the conduct of a public servant in the discharge of his public functions, or his character so far as it appears in that conduct|
|3|**Conduct of any person touching a public question**|Good-faith opinion on the conduct of a person touching a **public question** and his character as it appears in that conduct (politicians, public campaigners)|
|4|**Reports of court proceedings**|Publishing a **substantially true report** of the proceedings of a court of justice, or of the result|
|5|**Merits of cases decided in court; conduct of witnesses**|Good-faith opinion on the merits of a case **decided** by a court, or the conduct of a party, witness or agent in it, as shown in the case|
|6|**Merits of public performance**|Good-faith opinion on the merits of a performance **submitted to the judgment of the public** — a book, speech, film, painting — and the author's character as it appears in it|
|7|**Censure by a person having lawful authority**|Good-faith censure by one having authority over another (a parent, teacher, master, superior officer, judge) in matters to which the authority relates|
|8|**Accusation preferred in good faith to an authorised person**|A good-faith complaint to a person having **lawful authority** over the accused (e.g., to a Magistrate, the police, an employer)|
|9|**Imputation for protection of interests**|An imputation made in good faith for the **protection of the interests** of the person making it, or of any other, or for the **public good** (*illus.*: a shopkeeper warns his manager not to give credit to Z)|
|10|**Caution intended for the good of the recipient or the public**|Conveying a **caution** in good faith to one person against another, for the good of the recipient, a person in whom he is interested, or the public|

## 9.3 Punishment and procedure

1. **s. 500** — **simple imprisonment** up to **2 years**, or fine, or both; **proviso (2004):** the **originator** of the defamatory imputation — imprisonment of either description up to **5 years**, or fine **not less than Rs. 100,000**, or both.
2. **s. 501** — printing or engraving matter known to be defamatory — up to 2 years; **s. 502** — selling such matter — up to 2 years.
3. **Cognizance only on a complaint** by the **person aggrieved** (CrPC s. 198); the offence is **non-cognizable**, **bailable** and **compoundable** by the person defamed.
4. **Civil remedy** — damages under the **Defamation Ordinance 2002**; **online defamation** — the **Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016** (s. 20, offences against the dignity of a natural person).

## 9.4 Why is conviction for defamation difficult?

1. **Complaint-only** — no police investigation; the complainant must collect and present all evidence himself (CrPC s. 198), and the trial follows the slow **complaint-case** route.
2. **Ten broad exceptions**, most turning on **good faith** and **public good** — questions of fact that give the accused wide room; the accused needs only a **preponderance of probability** (Art. 121, QSO).
3. **Proof of publication** and of the **exact words** — oral slander is hard to prove; witnesses are usually the complainant's friends.
4. **Proof of harm to reputation** in the estimation of others (Explanation 4).
5. **Truth for the public good** — the complainant may face embarrassing disclosures; many withdraw.
6. **Compoundable** and **bailable**, with **light** punishment — little deterrence; frequent compromises.
7. **Delay** — the trial outlasts the dispute; complainants lose interest.
8. **Freedom of speech** (Art. 19 of the Constitution) — courts construe criminal defamation strictly.
9. **Social-media** imputations — anonymous accounts, foreign platforms, and jurisdictional problems.

## 9.5 Suggested amendments

1. **Time-bound trial** (e.g., within six months, as for s. 42 SRA suits), with day-to-day hearings.
2. **Decriminalise ordinary defamation** and strengthen the civil remedy (quick damages under the Defamation Ordinance 2002, with a specialised tribunal), keeping criminal liability only for **malicious and serious** cases.
3. **Mandatory preliminary inquiry** by the Magistrate to weed out frivolous complaints (CrPC s. 202), coupled with **costs** against false complainants.
4. A **right of reply and correction** and court-ordered **apology** as alternative sentences.
5. **Clear rules for digital publication** — duty of platforms to preserve and disclose data on court order; coordination between PECA and the PPC.
6. **Restorative** options: mediation before trial; community service.

# Chapter 10 · Criminal intimidation and other offences

## 10.1 Criminal intimidation — ss. 503 to 508

"Whoever **threatens** another with any **injury to his person, reputation or property**, or to the person or reputation of anyone in whom that person is interested, with intent to cause **alarm**, or to cause him to **do** any act he is not legally bound to do or **omit** an act he is legally entitled to do, as the means of avoiding the threat, commits criminal intimidation" (s. 503). **Punishment** — up to **2 years**; if the threat is to cause **death or grievous hurt**, destruction of property by **fire**, an offence punishable with death or life, or to **impute unchastity** to a woman — up to **7 years** (s. 506); by an **anonymous communication** — up to 2 years more (s. 507).

## 10.2 Offences against public tranquillity — ss. 141 to 160

1. **Unlawful assembly** (s. 141) — five or more persons with one of the five common objects (Topic 1); being a member — up to 6 months (s. 143); armed with a **deadly weapon** — up to 2 years (s. 144); continuing after a **command to disperse** — up to 2 years (s. 145).
2. **Rioting** (s. 146) — **force or violence** used by an unlawful assembly or any member in prosecution of the common object — up to **2 years** (s. 147); armed with a deadly weapon — up to **3 years** (s. 148).
3. **s. 149** — every member guilty of an offence committed in prosecution of the common object (Topic 1).
4. **Affray** (s. 159) — **two or more** persons **fighting in a public place** and disturbing the public peace — up to 1 month (s. 160).

## 10.3 Offences against public justice — selected

1. **False evidence** (s. 191) — a person bound by oath or law to state the truth makes a statement which is false and which he knows or believes to be false or does not believe to be true; **fabricating** false evidence (s. 192). **Punishment** — in a **judicial proceeding** up to **7 years**; otherwise up to 3 years (s. 193). Prosecution only on the **complaint of the court** concerned (CrPC s. 195(1)(b)).
2. **False information** to a public servant (s. 182) — up to 6 months (on the complaint of the public servant — CrPC s. 195(1)(a)).
3. **False charge** with intent to injure (s. 211) — up to 2 years; if for an offence punishable with death, life or 7 years or more — up to 7 years.
4. **Harbouring an offender** (s. 212) — Topic 1.
