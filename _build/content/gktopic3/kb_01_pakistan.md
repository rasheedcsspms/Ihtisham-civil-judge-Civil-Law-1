
# Chapter 1 · Pakistan — Constitution, judiciary and office holders

![The 26th and 27th Amendments at a glance](amendments.png)

## 1.1 The 26th Constitutional Amendment (October 2024)

1. Passed **20–21 October 2024**; assented by President Zardari on **21 October 2024**.
2. **Chief Justice of Pakistan** nominated by a **12-member Special Parliamentary Committee** from the **three senior-most** Supreme Court judges, for a **fixed three-year term**; **Justice Yahya Afridi** — first CJP under the new system (oath **26 October 2024**).
3. **Judicial Commission of Pakistan** (Art. 175A) reconstituted with parliamentarians.
4. **Constitutional benches** in the Supreme Court and High Courts (**Art. 191A**) — overtaken in the Supreme Court by the FCC in 2025.
5. **Art. 9A** — right to a **clean and healthy environment**.
6. **Riba** to be eliminated "as far as practicable" by **1 January 2028** (Art. 38(f)).
7. **Suo motu** jurisdiction under Art. 184(3) curtailed (constitutional benches only).

## 1.2 The 27th Constitutional Amendment (November 2025)

{w:30,70}
|Point|Detail|
|Passage|Senate **10 November 2025** (64–0, opposition boycott); National Assembly passed it with changes, re-passed by the Senate; **assent 13 November 2025**|
|**Federal Constitutional Court**|New **Art. 175C** — takes over the Supreme Court's **original jurisdiction** in federal–provincial disputes and fundamental-rights matters (former Art. 184) and **appeals on constitutional questions**; its decisions **bind** the Supreme Court (but not vice versa)|
|First Chief Justice of the FCC|**Justice Aminuddin Khan** — sworn in **14 November 2025**; seven judges at the start|
|Resignations|Supreme Court Justices **Syed Mansoor Ali Shah** and **Athar Minallah** resigned on **13 November 2025** in protest|
|**Chief of Defence Forces** (Art. 243)|New post held by the **Chief of Army Staff**, commanding all three services — **Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir**, COAS and CDF for **five years** (notified **December 2025**)|
|CJCSC abolished|The post of **Chairman Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee** lapsed on **27 November 2025** — the last CJCSC was **Gen Sahir Shamshad Mirza**|
|**Commander National Strategic Command**|New four-star post over strategic (nuclear) forces, appointed by the PM on the CDF's recommendation for **three years** — first holder **Gen Syed Aamer Raza** (July 2026)|
|Field Marshal|Rank (and its naval and air equivalents) made **lifelong**; can be withdrawn only by **Parliament**|
|Presidential immunity|**Art. 248** — the President enjoys **lifetime immunity** from criminal proceedings (lost if he later holds public office)|
|Transfer of judges|**Art. 200** — transfer of High Court judges through the **JCP**; a judge who refuses is deemed retired|
|Follow-up laws|**Defence Forces Act 2026** and **National Command Authority (Amendment) Act 2026** (August 2026)|

:::case The 28th Amendment — not yet passed
As of mid-October 2026 the "28th Amendment" is only a **proposal** (reported ideas: changes to the NFC share, return of some devolved subjects, local-government powers, delinking elections from the census). The PPP has reservations; critics see it as rolling back the 18th Amendment.
:::

:::maxim The constitutional law on emergencies — likely question
**Art. 232** — the President may proclaim an emergency if the security of Pakistan or any part is threatened by war, external aggression or **internal disturbance beyond the power of a provincial government**; for an internal disturbance a **resolution of the Provincial Assembly** is needed, but if the President acts on his own the proclamation must be **placed before both Houses within ten days**. **Art. 234** — on the **failure of constitutional machinery** in a province, the President may assume the functions of the provincial government (exercised through the **Governor**); a proclamation lasts **two months**, extendable by a **joint sitting** for two months at a time, but **not beyond six months** in total. **Art. 245** — the armed forces act **in aid of civil power** when called upon by the Federal Government. Fundamental rights may be suspended under **Art. 233** during an emergency.
:::

## 4.2 The Indus Waters Treaty (1960) since 2025

1. **India's position** — the treaty is "in abeyance"; India boycotts the **Court of Arbitration**, calling it "illegally constituted".
2. **Court of Arbitration** (Permanent Court of Arbitration, case 2023-01) — **Award on general interpretation (8 August 2025)**; supplemental award on pondage (**15 May 2026**); **Award on the Status of the Treaty (31 August 2026)** — India cannot unilaterally hold the treaty in abeyance; the treaty remains in force; interim measures on the **Ratle** project.
3. **Neutral Expert** (Michel Lino) — **Kishenganga** and **Ratle** proceedings continue (decision expected 2027).
4. Exam angle — treaty law: no unilateral suspension (Art. XII(4) — the treaty continues until terminated by a ratified treaty agreed by **both** parties); Vienna Convention Art. 60–62; the **World Bank's** role.
