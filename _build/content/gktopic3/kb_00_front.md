# How to use this knowledge base

## What the examiner wants

The syllabus asks for "**current world affairs** and broad facts of historical, political, geographical and economic importance". In practice this means (a) **MCQs and short notes** on office holders, events, summits, awards and sports of the last 12–18 months, and (b) an **essay** on a live national or international issue — for 2026 the strongest candidates are the **27th Amendment and judicial independence**, **Pakistan's role in the Iran war mediation**, **Pakistan–Afghanistan relations**, **Pakistan–India after May 2025 and the Indus Waters Treaty**, the **economy and the IMF**, **climate and floods**, and **governor's rule in KP**.

:::trap Facts that move
Office holders, policy rates and vote counts change. Where reports differed, this volume gives the most widely reported figure and marks it **(verify)**. Facts are current to **17 October 2026** — glance at a newspaper the week before the paper for anything that changes later (UN Secretary-General choice, Brazil's runoff on 25 Oct, Israel's election on 27 Oct, the SBP decision on 26 Oct).
:::

:::tip Using current affairs in the essays
Every Pakistan Studies or Islamic Studies essay improves with **one or two recent facts** — e.g., Census 2023 figures in a population essay; the 2025 floods in a climate essay; the 27th Amendment in a federalism or judiciary essay; the Islamabad Talks in a foreign-policy essay.
:::
