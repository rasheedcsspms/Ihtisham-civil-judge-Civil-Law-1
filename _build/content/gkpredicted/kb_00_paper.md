## Part I — Objective (10 × 1 = 10)

1. The verse "when you judge between people, judge with justice" is in: (a) Al-Baqarah (b) An-Nisa 4:58 (c) Al-Ma'idah (d) Al-Hujurat

3. Hazrat Umar appointed as Qazi of Kufa: (a) Shuraih (b) Abu Darda (c) Zaid bin Thabit (d) Ka'b bin Sur
4. The office that enforced public morality and fair dealing in markets: (a) Mazalim (b) Hisbah (c) Ifta' (d) Diwan
5. The court of grievances against officials was called: (a) Diwan al-Kharaj (b) Diwan al-Mazalim (c) Bait-ul-Mal (d) Shura
6. "The burden of proof is on the claimant and the oath on the one who denies" is a principle of: (a) Qisas (b) Evidence (bayyinah) (c) Inheritance (d) Zakat
7. The Prophet ﷺ asked a companion how he would decide cases before sending him to Yemen; the companion was: (a) Ali (b) Mu'adh bin Jabal (c) Khalid bin Walid (d) Bilal
8. Inquiry into the credibility of witnesses is called: (a) Tahkim (b) Tazkiyah al-shuhood (c) Ta'zir (d) Istihsan
9. Punishment for qazf (false accusation of zina): (a) 100 lashes (b) 80 lashes (c) 40 lashes (d) Rajm
10. The Federal Shariat Court is established under Article: (a) 2A (b) 203C (c) 227 (d) 230

## Part II — Subjective (attempt any TWO; 20 marks each)

:::case Q1 ★★★ The Islamic system of administration of justice
"Describe the salient features of the Islamic system of administration of justice. How far can they guide the judiciary of Pakistan today?"
**Outline** — definition of *qada'* and *'adl* (4:58, 4:135, 5:8) → justice as an attribute of Allah and the purpose of prophethood (57:25) → the Prophet ﷺ as judge; Mu'adh's hadith (Quran, Sunnah, ijtihad) → **features**: independence of the qazi (Hazrat Ali v. the Jew over the coat of armour before Shuraih), equality before the law (the Makhzumi woman — "even if Fatimah …"), speedy and inexpensive justice, open court, qualifications of the qazi, rules of evidence (bayyinah, oath, tazkiyah), the presumption of innocence and benefit of doubt (*idra'u al-hudud bi al-shubuhat*), separation of the judiciary under Hazrat Umar, accountability of rulers (mazalim) → institutions — qada', mazalim, hisbah, ifta', tahkim → relevance to Pakistan — Arts. 2A, 31, 227, 203C, 37(d), 175(3); delay, cost, corruption; ADR (tahkim / sulh) → conclusion. *(KB Topic 1, Chapters 7–8)*
:::

:::case Q2 ★★★ Hazrat Umar's letter to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari
"Hazrat Umar's letter to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari is the charter of judicial conduct. Discuss its principles and their relevance for a modern judge."
**Outline** — background and authenticity (recorded by al-Mawardi, Ibn Qayyim) → principles: *qada'* is a firm obligation; understand the case; equality of parties in seating, attention and justice; burden of proof on the claimant, oath on the denier; compromise (sulh) permitted except one that makes the lawful unlawful; time for evidence; review of one's own earlier decision (returning to the truth is better than persisting in error); reasoning by analogy (qiyas); all Muslims are competent witnesses except those convicted of qazf or known for false testimony; avoid anger, irritation and impatience → comparison — the Code of Conduct for judges (Supreme Judicial Council), Bangalore Principles; CPC O.X (compromise), Art. 117 QSO (burden of proof) → conclusion. *(KB Topic 1, Chapter 8)*
:::

:::case Q3 ★★ Qualifications and conduct of a Qazi / independence of the judiciary in Islam
Qualifications (Muslim, adult, sane, free, just, knowledgeable — mujtahid, sound hearing and sight); conduct (no gifts, no deciding in anger, equal treatment, no private hearing of one party); examples of independence (Ali before Shuraih; Abu Yusuf; Imam Abu Hanifa refusing the post); accountability of the Caliph before the court. *(KB Topic 1, Chapter 8)*
:::

:::case Q4 ★★ Islamic concept of justice / human rights in Islam
'Adl, qist, ihsan; justice to enemies (5:8), to parents and self (4:135); Farewell Sermon as a charter of human rights (life, property, honour, equality, women's rights); Madinah Charter (rights of minorities); comparison with the UDHR 1948; Pakistan's Constitution, Chapter 1 of Part II. *(KB Topic 1, Chapters 3, 7, 10)*
:::

:::case Q5 ★★ Evidence and women as witnesses / sources of Islamic law and ijtihad
**Option (a)** — modes of proof (iqrar, shahadah, qasamah, documentary, circumstantial); 2:282 and the reason for two women; hudood v. other cases; QSO Art. 17 → **Option (b)** — Quran, Sunnah, ijma', qiyas; secondary sources (istihsan, maslahah mursalah, 'urf); ijtihad — conditions; the four schools; role of the CII and FSC in modern ijtihad. *(KB Topic 1, Chapters 6, 8)*
:::

:::eg Short-note alternatives ★★
Hisbah · Diwan al-Mazalim · Tahkim (arbitration) · Qisas and diyat · Madinah Charter · Farewell Sermon · Hilf-ul-Fudul · Ijtihad · Objects of punishment (deterrence, retribution, reform) in Islam.
:::

## Part II — Subjective (attempt any TWO; 20 marks each)

:::case Q1 ★★★ The two-nation theory and the demand for Pakistan
"The two-nation theory was the basis of the creation of Pakistan. Discuss the factors that led the Muslims to demand a separate homeland."
**Outline** — definition; Sir Syed (Urdu–Hindi 1867), Iqbal (Allahabad 1930), Jinnah (Lahore 1940: "Muslims are a nation by any definition") → factors: religious and cultural identity, Hindu majoritarianism, Nehru Report, Congress ministries 1937–39 (Pirpur Report; Day of Deliverance), economic and educational backwardness, Shuddhi / Sangathan, failure of constitutional formulas, 1945–46 elections → conclusion — relevance today (Objectives Resolution, Art. 2A). *(KB Topic 2, Chapter 1.3)*

:::case Q2 ★★★ Constitutional development / why constitution-making was delayed

:::

:::case Q3 ★★★ Federalism and provincial autonomy

:::case Q4 ★★ A national issue — water, population, corruption or climate
