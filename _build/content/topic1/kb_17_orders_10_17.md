# Chapter 17 · Before the trial: examination of parties, discovery, admissions and documents (Orders X to XIII)

These Orders help the court **narrow the dispute** before evidence begins, so that the trial focuses only on what is really contested.

## 17.1 Examination of parties by the court — Order X

- **r. 1:** at the **first hearing**, the court shall ascertain from each party or his pleader whether he **admits or denies** the allegations of fact in the plaint or written statement (if not expressly or impliedly admitted or denied). Admissions and denials are **recorded**.
- **r. 2:** at the first or any subsequent hearing, the court **may examine orally** any party appearing in person or present in court, or any person accompanying the party or his pleader who can answer material questions.
- **r. 3:** the substance of the examination is **reduced to writing** by the judge and forms part of the record.
- **r. 4:** if a pleader is **unable or refuses to answer** material questions, the court may postpone the hearing and direct the party to appear in person; if the party fails without lawful excuse, the court may **pronounce judgment against him** or make other orders (appealable: O. XLIII r. 1(e)).

:::why Purpose of Order X
To find out, **at the outset**, what is really in dispute. It is not for recording evidence; statements under O. X are not evidence on oath, but admissions made can be acted upon. *T. Arivandandam* recommends using O. X to expose **clever drafting** that creates an illusory cause of action.
:::

## 17.2 Discovery and inspection — Order XI

**Discovery** means compelling the opposite party to **disclose** facts or documents relevant to the case.

1. **Interrogatories (rr. 1–11, 22):** with the **leave of the court**, a party may deliver written questions to the other party, to be answered on **affidavit** within ten days (or as fixed). Leave is granted only for interrogatories **necessary** for disposing fairly of the suit or saving costs. Interrogatories that are scandalous, irrelevant, or **fishing** (to find out the other side's evidence in general) are refused or struck out (r. 7). Answers may be used at the trial (r. 22). Costs follow if interrogatories were unreasonable (r. 3).
2. **Discovery of documents (rr. 12–13):** any party may apply for an order directing the other to disclose, **on affidavit**, the documents in his possession or power relating to the matter in question.
3. **Production and inspection (rr. 14–19):** the court may order production of documents; a party may by notice require the other to produce documents referred to in his pleadings or affidavits for **inspection**; the court may order inspection.
4. **Non-compliance (r. 21):** if a party fails to comply with an order for interrogatories, discovery or inspection, the **plaintiff's suit may be dismissed for want of prosecution**, or the **defendant's defence struck out** and he is placed as if he had not defended (appealable: O. XLIII r. 1(f)).
5. **r. 23:** the Order applies to **minors** (through next friend / guardian).

:::maxim Fishing expeditions are not allowed
Discovery is to obtain **admissions** to support one's own case or destroy the opponent's on **specific** matters in issue — not to search for some unknown case. Courts refuse "roving" or "fishing" interrogatories.
:::

## 17.3 Admissions — Order XII

- **r. 1:** a party may, by pleading or in writing, **admit the truth of the whole or part** of the other party's case.
- **r. 2:** a party may call on the other to **admit documents**; unreasonable refusal → costs.
- **r. 4:** a party may, by notice, call on the other to **admit facts** (not later than nine days before the hearing).
- **r. 6 — Judgment on admissions:**

> "6. Any party may, at any stage of a suit, where admissions of fact have been made, either on the pleadings, or otherwise, apply to the Court for such judgment or order as upon such admissions he may be entitled to, without waiting for the determination of any other question between the parties: and the Court may upon such application make such order, or give such judgment, as the Court may think just."

**Principles of r. 6:** (1) the admission must be **clear, unequivocal and unconditional**; (2) it may be in the pleadings, in a statement under O. X, in a document, or "otherwise"; (3) the power is **discretionary** ("may") — the court will not give judgment where the admission is ambiguous or the defence raises a serious question of law; (4) the court can pass a **partial decree** for the admitted part and try the rest.

:::eg Judgment on admission (LHC 2020 short note)
Imran sues Shahid for Rs. 1.5 million (loan). Shahid's written statement says: "The defendant did borrow Rs. 1,000,000 from the plaintiff but repaid Rs. 500,000; the claim beyond Rs. 500,000 is denied." Shahid admits borrowing Rs. 1 million and admits Rs. 500,000 is unpaid (by necessary implication). Imran may apply under O. XII r. 6: the court may pass a **decree for Rs. 500,000** at once and try the dispute over the remaining Rs. 1 million.
:::

## 17.4 Documents — Order XIII

- **r. 1:** the parties shall produce **at the first hearing** all documentary evidence in their possession or power on which they intend to rely (not already filed), with an accurate **list**; under r. 1(3) the court may call on the parties to **admit or deny** the documents produced and record it.
- **r. 2 — Effect of non-production:** documents that should have been produced under r. 1 but were not "shall not be received at any subsequent stage … unless **good cause** is shown"; the court must **record reasons** for receiving them.
- **r. 3:** the court may at any stage **reject** irrelevant or inadmissible documents, recording the ground.
- **r. 4:** endorsements on documents **admitted in evidence** (number, title of suit, by whom produced, date, signature of judge).
- **r. 6:** endorsement "rejected" on inadmissible documents.
- **r. 8:** the court may **impound** any document (e.g., a forged or insufficiently stamped document).
- **r. 9:** return of admitted documents after the suit / appeal period, on certified copies.
- **r. 10:** the court may send for papers from its own records or from other courts.

:::tip Admissibility of documents — link to Qanun-e-Shahadat
The IHC syllabus says the examiner may ask you to "discuss the admissibility of evidence". When a document is tendered, think: (1) Was it **listed / produced** in time (O. VII r. 14, r. 18; O. XIII r. 2)? (2) Is it **relevant** (Arts. 18–39 QSO)? (3) Is it proved by **primary or secondary** evidence (Arts. 72–76 QSO)? (4) Does it need **registration** (Registration Act s. 17, 49) or **stamping** (Stamp Act s. 35)? (5) Has the opponent **admitted** it under O. XII / O. XIII r. 1(3)? Mark "objected" documents carefully; decide admissibility at the end if the objection depends on evidence.
:::

:::recap Chapter 17 in five lines
- **O. X:** court ascertains admissions / denials at first hearing; may examine parties orally; refusal → judgment against party.
- **O. XI:** interrogatories with **leave** (no fishing), discovery and inspection of documents on affidavit; non-compliance → suit dismissed / defence struck out (r. 21).
- **O. XII:** notices to admit; **r. 6 judgment on admissions** — clear, unequivocal admission; discretionary; part decree possible.
- **O. XIII:** produce documents at first hearing with list; late documents only for **good cause** with reasons (r. 2); reject irrelevant / inadmissible (r. 3); endorsements; impounding.
- These steps **narrow the dispute** before issues are framed.
:::

# Chapter 18 · Issues — the heart of a civil trial (Orders XIV and XV)

## 18.1 What an issue is

> "1. (1) Issues arise when a material proposition of fact or law is affirmed by the one party and denied by the other. (2) Material propositions are those propositions of law or fact which a plaintiff must allege in order to show a right to sue or a defendant must allege in order to constitute his defence. (3) Each material proposition affirmed by one party and denied by the other shall form the subject of a distinct issue. (4) Issues are of two kinds: (a) issues of fact, (b) issues of law. (5) At the first hearing of the suit the Court shall, after reading the plaint and the written statements if any, and after such examination of the parties as may appear necessary, ascertain upon what material propositions of fact or of law the parties are at variance, and shall thereupon proceed to frame and record the issues on which the right decision of the case appears to depend. (6) Nothing in this rule requires the Court to frame and record issues where the defendant at the first hearing of the suit makes no defence." (O. XIV r. 1)

**In plain words:** an issue is a **question** that the court must answer to decide the suit. It arises only where one side says "yes" and the other says "no" on a **material** point — a point the plaintiff must establish to win, or the defendant must establish to defend.

:::why Why are issues so important?
1. They **define the trial**: evidence is led only on the issues; the judgment answers each issue.
2. They tell each party **what he must prove** (burden of proof — "onus").
3. A wrongly framed or missing issue can cause a **mistrial** and remand, unless the parties knew the case and led evidence on it (no prejudice).
4. The **IHC syllabus** expressly says candidates will be given the facts of a typical case and asked to **frame issues**. This is a guaranteed skill.
:::

:::case Makhan Lal Bangal v. Manas Bhunia ((2001) 2 SCC 652)
__Held.__ The framing of issues is a very important stage in a civil trial. It is the **duty of the court** to frame proper issues; issues should be framed with care after examining the pleadings so that the real controversy is identified. A trial without proper issues tends to wander, and parties are taken by surprise. Issues are framed on **material propositions**, not on every allegation.
:::

## 18.2 Sources and timing — rr. 3 to 6

- **r. 3 — materials:** issues may be framed from (a) **allegations on oath** by parties or persons on their behalf, or statements of their pleaders; (b) allegations in the **pleadings** or answers to interrogatories; (c) **contents of documents** produced by either party.
- **r. 4:** if the court cannot frame issues correctly without examining a person or document not before it, it may **adjourn** and compel attendance or production.
- **r. 5 — amendment:** the court may **at any time before passing a decree** amend issues or frame **additional** issues, and must do so where necessary to determine the matters in controversy; it may also **strike out** issues wrongly framed.
- **r. 6–7 — agreed issues:** parties may agree on questions of fact or law to be stated as issues, and the court may decide accordingly.

## 18.3 Issues of law first — r. 2

> "2. Where issues both of law and of fact arise in the same suit, and the Court is of opinion that the case or any part thereof may be disposed of on the issues of law only, it **shall** try those issues first, and for that purpose may, if it thinks fit, postpone the settlement of the issues of fact until after the issues of law have been determined."

The Pakistani text uses "**shall**". If a legal issue (e.g., **jurisdiction**, a **bar by law**, **limitation** on admitted dates, **res judicata** on admitted pleadings) can dispose of the whole suit **without evidence**, the court must decide it first. But if the legal issue depends on **disputed facts** (e.g., when the plaintiff got knowledge, for limitation), it is a **mixed** issue and must be decided with the rest after evidence.

## 18.4 Disposal at the first hearing — Order XV

- **r. 1:** if the parties are **not at issue** on any question of law or fact, the court may **at once pronounce judgment**.
- **r. 2:** if one of several defendants is not at issue, judgment may be pronounced for or against him and the suit proceeds against the others.
- **r. 3:** if parties are at issue, but the court is satisfied that no further argument or evidence than the parties can **at once** adduce is required on issues sufficient to decide the suit, and no injustice will result, it may decide those issues and pronounce judgment (if the summons was for issues only, the parties must be present and not object).
- **r. 4:** where the summons was for **final disposal** and a party fails without sufficient cause to produce his evidence, the court may at once pronounce judgment or, after framing issues, adjourn for evidence.

## 18.5 How to frame issues — a step-by-step method

![How to frame issues — the seven-step method](issue_steps.png)

1. **Read the plaint** and list every **material fact** the plaintiff must prove to get each relief (e.g., the agreement, payment, breach, loss).
2. **Read the written statement** and mark each fact **denied** and each **new fact** pleaded (fraud, limitation, payment, non-joinder, jurisdiction, valuation, s. 80 notice, res judicata).
3. **Drop the admitted facts** — no issue on admitted facts (O. VIII r. 5).
4. **Frame one issue for each disputed material proposition**, in the form "**Whether …?**".
5. **Place the onus** at the end of each issue: **OPP** (onus probandi on the plaintiff) or **OPD** (on the defendant). The burden is on the person who **asserts** the fact and who would **fail** if no evidence were given (Arts. 117–118, Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984).
6. **Order:** customarily, legal objections (jurisdiction, maintainability, limitation, valuation) first or last — courts do both — but be consistent; the main factual issues on the plaintiff's case come before the defendant's special defences.
7. Always end with the **relief** issue: "**Relief.**"

:::tip Rules of good drafting for issues
- One issue = **one** material proposition. Don't combine two questions ("Whether the agreement was executed and the plaintiff paid the price?") unless they are inseparable.
- Use the **parties' own pleaded words** (amount, date, document) so the issue is precise.
- Do not frame issues on **admitted** facts, on **law alone** without facts in dispute, or on **evidence**.
- Do not frame an issue on **costs** or **special costs** as if a cause of action (but an issue on s. 35B averments is acceptable where the defendant pleads it).
- The onus should follow the **pleading**: the party who pleads a fact proves it.
:::

## 18.6 Worked example 1 — the LHC 2017 question

:::eg The facts (LHC Civil Judge exam 2017, Civil Law-II, Q. 3)
Ahmed supplied certain goods worth **Rs. 2.00 million**, received by **Zahid on behalf of his employer, Babar**. On non-payment within the stipulated period, Ahmed filed a **recovery suit against Zahid**. In the written statement, Zahid controverted the plaint and asserted that the amount was outstanding against **Babar**. He also raised preliminary objections regarding **maintainability**, **limitation**, **deficient court fee**, **non-joinder** of parties, **false and vexatious claim** and **exemplary costs**, and prayed for dismissal. *Frame the appropriate issues.*
:::

:::issues Issues framed (Suit for recovery of Rs. 2,000,000 — Ahmed v. Zahid)
1. Whether the suit is not maintainable in its present form? **OPD**
2. Whether the suit is barred by limitation? **OPD**
3. Whether the suit has not been properly valued for the purposes of court fee and jurisdiction, and the court fee paid is deficient? **OPD**
4. Whether the suit is bad for non-joinder of Babar, a necessary party? **OPD**
5. Whether the plaintiff supplied goods worth Rs. 2,000,000 which were received by the defendant, and the said amount is outstanding and recoverable from the defendant? **OPP**
6. Whether the defendant received the goods only as an employee / agent on behalf of his disclosed employer Babar, and is therefore not personally liable for the price? **OPD**
7. Whether the suit is false and vexatious to the knowledge of the plaintiff, and the defendant is entitled to special costs under section 35B CPC? **OPD**
8. Relief.
:::

**Why these issues?** Issue 5 is the plaintiff's whole case (supply, receipt, non-payment) — onus on him. Issue 6 is the defendant's special plea of agency; under **s. 230 of the Contract Act**, an agent is not personally liable on contracts entered on behalf of a **disclosed** principal (in the absence of a contract to the contrary) — this links Topic 1 with Topic 4. Issue 4 flows from the same plea: if Babar is the real buyer, he is a **necessary party**. Issues 1–3 are the legal objections; issue 2 (limitation) can be decided under O. XIV r. 2 first if the dates are admitted — the Limitation Act gives **three years** for the price of goods sold and delivered (First Schedule, Art. 52). "Exemplary costs" is not a separate issue; it is covered by Issue 7 and the court's power under s. 35B.

## 18.7 Worked example 2 — a suit for specific performance

:::eg The facts
Tariq sues Nasir for **specific performance** of an agreement to sell House No. 7, Street 3, G-13/2, Islamabad, dated 10.01.2025, for Rs. 30 million, alleging payment of **Rs. 5 million earnest money** and his readiness to pay the balance. Nasir admits the agreement but says (i) it was a **sham** to secure a loan of Rs. 5 million, (ii) Tariq failed to pay the balance by the agreed date of 10.04.2025 and **time was of the essence**, so the earnest money stands forfeited, and (iii) the suit is barred because Tariq did not serve any legal notice.
:::

:::issues Issues
1. Whether the agreement to sell dated 10.01.2025 was a sham transaction executed only as security for a loan of Rs. 5,000,000? **OPD**
2. Whether time was of the essence of the agreement and the plaintiff failed to perform his part by 10.04.2025, entitling the defendant to forfeit the earnest money? **OPD**
3. Whether the plaintiff has always been, and is, ready and willing to perform his part of the agreement? **OPP**
4. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree for specific performance of the agreement dated 10.01.2025? **OPP**
5. Relief.
:::

*Note:* No issue on the **execution** of the agreement (admitted). The plea about "no legal notice" raises **no issue** unless a law requires notice as a condition precedent — none does in a private specific performance suit; the court can simply note this in the judgment. (Specific performance and "ready and willing" — Specific Relief Act, Civil Law-II.)

:::trap Three classic mistakes in framing issues
1. Framing an issue on an **admitted** fact (e.g., "Whether the agreement was executed?" when the defendant admits it).
2. Putting the onus on the wrong party — e.g., "Whether the plaintiff did not pay? OPP". The party who **asserts** the fact must prove it.
3. Missing the defendant's **special pleas** (fraud, sham, limitation, non-joinder, res judicata). Each special plea in the written statement usually needs its own issue.
:::

:::recap Chapter 18 in six lines
- Issue = material proposition of fact or law **affirmed by one, denied by the other** (O. XIV r. 1); each a distinct issue; framed at the first hearing.
- Sources: pleadings, statements on oath, interrogatories, documents (r. 3); amend / add / strike out before decree (r. 5).
- **Issues of law first** where they can dispose of the case — "shall" (r. 2), but not if they depend on disputed facts.
- O. XV: judgment at once if parties not at issue; quick decision if no further evidence needed.
- Method: material facts → denials / new pleas → drop admissions → "Whether …?" → **OPP / OPD** → order → "Relief".
- Duty of the court to frame proper issues (*Makhan Lal Bangal*).
:::

# Chapter 19 · Witnesses and adjournments (Orders XVI and XVII)

## 19.1 Summoning witnesses — Order XVI

> "1. (1) Not later than seven days after the settlement of issues, the parties shall present in Court a certificate of readiness to produce evidence, along with a list of witnesses whom they propose to call or produce either to give evidence or to produce documents. (2) A party shall not be permitted to call or produce witnesses other than those contained in the said list, except with the permission of the Court and after showing good cause for the omission of the said witnesses from the list; and the Court grants such permission, it shall record reasons for so doing. (3) … Provided that no summons shall be issued for service on a person under rule 8 unless an application in that behalf is made not later than fourteen days prior to the date fixed for the hearing of the suit and the necessary expenses for the summoning of such person are deposited."

**Key points:**

- **List of witnesses + certificate of readiness** within **seven days** after issues (Pakistani rule, 1972 / 1994).
- Witnesses **outside the list** only with the court's **permission for good cause**, with recorded reasons.
- Application for summons at least **fourteen days** before the hearing, with **expenses** deposited (r. 2: travelling and one day's attendance; experts may be paid reasonable remuneration).
- **r. 6:** a person may be summoned only to **produce a document** (without giving evidence).
- **r. 7:** any person **present in court** may be required to give evidence or produce a document.
- **r. 10–13:** a witness who fails to attend despite service may be issued a **proclamation**, **warrant** (with or without bail), **attachment** of property, and a **fine** (up to the limit in s. 32).
- **r. 14:** the court may of its own accord summon a **stranger** as a witness if it thinks necessary.
- **r. 20:** if a **party** present in court refuses to give evidence or produce a document when required by the court, the court may **pronounce judgment against him** (appealable: O. XLIII r. 1(h)).

## 19.2 Adjournments — Order XVII

> "1. (1) The Court may, if sufficient cause is shown, at any stage of the suit grant time to the parties or to any of them, and may from time to time adjourn the hearing of the suit.
> 2. In every such case the Court shall fix a day for the further hearing of the suit, and may make such order as it thinks fit with respect to the costs occasioned by the adjournment: Provided that, when the hearing of evidence has once begun, the hearing of the suit shall be continued from day to day until all the witnesses in attendance have been examined, unless the Court finds the adjournment of the hearing beyond the following day to be necessary for reasons to be recorded."

- Adjournment only for **sufficient cause**; costs — and in Islamabad, **minimum Rs. 5,000 per adjournment** under **s. 35A** unless unavoidable reasons are recorded.
- Once evidence begins, **day-to-day** hearing of witnesses in attendance.
- **Second r. 2 — failure to appear on an adjourned date:** the court may dispose of the suit in one of the modes directed by **Order IX**, or make such other order as it thinks fit.
- **rr. 4–5 (Pakistani):** if the date is a **holiday**, parties appear the next working day; if the **presiding officer is absent**, parties still appear and the reader gives them slips with the next date.

## 19.3 Decision despite default — O. XVII r. 3

> "3. Where any party to a suit to whom time has been granted fails to produce his evidence, or to cause the attendance of his witnesses, or to perform any other act necessary to the further progress of the suit, for which time has been allowed, the Court may, notwithstanding such default, proceed to decide the suit forthwith."

This is one of the most litigated rules in Pakistan. The settled principles:

1. **Time must have been granted** to the party, at his request or by the court, for a **specific act** (produce evidence, summon witnesses, deposit expenses), and the party **defaulted**.
2. The power is **penal** and must be exercised **strictly** — usually after a clear **last opportunity** with a warning.
3. The court must decide **"forthwith"** — on the same day — and **on the merits**, on the material available on the record (pleadings, documents, evidence recorded so far). It is a **decision on merits**, i.e., a **decree** appealable under s. 96 — not a dismissal for default.
4. If the defaulting party is **also absent**, the court may choose between **O. XVII r. 2** (Order IX modes) and **r. 3**; where the conditions of r. 3 exist, it may proceed under r. 3.
5. The other party is **not** required to lead evidence after closing the defaulting party's evidence; the court decides on burden of proof — the party on whom the onus lay and who failed to prove it loses that issue.

:::why Why O. XVII r. 3?
Litigants sometimes keep a case alive for years by asking for "one more date" to produce witnesses. Rule 3 gives the court a sharp tool to stop this, consistent with s. 1(4) (Islamabad) and *vigilantibus non dormientibus jura subveniunt*. But because it shuts out evidence, it is used only after clear default and fair warning.
:::

:::eg O. XVII r. 3 — order format
*"The plaintiff's evidence was due today. On 05.06.2026, 11.07.2026 and 15.08.2026 adjournments were granted at the plaintiff's request to produce his witnesses; on the last date a final opportunity was given subject to costs of Rs. 5,000 with the warning that no further adjournment would be granted. Today again no witness is present and no summons was even applied for. The plaintiff has failed to perform the act for which time was granted. Resultantly, the plaintiff's evidence is **closed under Order XVII rule 3 CPC**. As the burden of issues No. 1 and 3 lay on the plaintiff and no evidence has been produced, those issues are decided against him. Consequently, the suit is **dismissed** with costs. Decree sheet be drawn. File be consigned to the record room after completion."*
:::

:::recap Chapter 19 in five lines
- O. XVI r. 1: **list of witnesses + certificate of readiness within 7 days** of issues; others only with leave for good cause; summons applied 14 days ahead with expenses.
- Witness defaults → proclamation, warrant, attachment, fine; court may summon strangers; party refusing evidence → judgment (r. 20).
- O. XVII rr. 1–2: adjournment only for sufficient cause, with costs (s. 35A: **≥ Rs. 5,000**); day-to-day once evidence begins; default on adjourned date → Order IX modes.
- **O. XVII r. 3:** party given time defaults → court may decide **forthwith on merits** (decree; appeal s. 96); strict, penal, after last opportunity.
- rr. 4–5: holiday → next day; presiding officer absent → appear and take next date.
:::

|Territorial vs inherent jurisdiction defect|Waivable; s. 21; decree not void|Not waivable; decree a nullity|
|s. 22 vs s. 24 transfer|Defendant's application; courts of concurrent jurisdiction; before issues|High Court / District Court; any stage; on application or *suo motu*|
|s. 12(2) vs appeal|Fraud, misrepresentation, want of jurisdiction; to court of final order|Any error of fact or law; to higher court|
|Legal representative vs intermeddler|Heir / executor / administrator|Person who meddles with estate without authority — included in s. 2(11)|

# Chapter 22 · Exam technique: problem answers, issues and short orders

## 22.1 The IRAC method for problem questions

Most marks in the IHC paper go to **problem questions**. Use **IRAC**:

1. **Issue** — identify every legal question raised by the facts. Number them.
2. **Rule** — state the relevant section / rule, quoting key words from the bare Act, with the leading case and the principle.
3. **Application** — apply the rule to **these** facts, step by step; deal with the other side's best argument.
4. **Conclusion** — give a clear answer for each issue and the final result (remedy, order to be passed).

:::tip Seven habits that earn marks
1. Start with a **one-line answer** (the bottom line), then explain.
2. **Quote** the bare Act in quotation marks with the section number.
3. Give **one leading case** per major point, with its **ratio** in one line.
4. Use **headings** and **numbered points**; examiners scan.
5. Mention the **Islamabad-specific** law where it applies (ss. 1(4), 35, 35A, 96(2), 115 proviso, 151 reasons, 159; Ordinance s. 18 (2025)).
6. Use **Pakistani names** and **Islamabad sectors** if you invent illustrations — it shows practical sense.
7. Finish with a **conclusion** that tells the examiner exactly what order you would pass.
:::

## 22.2 Format of a short order (application stage)

:::order Specimen: order on an application under Order VII rule 11 CPC
IN THE COURT OF CIVIL JUDGE 1st CLASS, ISLAMABAD (WEST)

Civil Suit No. ___ of 2026 · Ali Raza v. Capital Development Authority & another

__ORDER__ (dated ___)

1. Through this order I shall dispose of the application filed by defendant No. 1 under Order VII rule 11 CPC for rejection of the plaint.
2. **Brief facts:** (two or three lines on the plaint and the relief claimed).
3. **Grounds of the application:** (e.g., suit barred by s. ___ of the ___ Act; no cause of action).
4. **Reply:** (plaintiff's stance).
5. **Arguments heard; record perused.**
6. **Law:** quote O. VII r. 11; principles — only the plaint and annexed documents are seen, averments assumed true (*Saleem Bhai*); clever drafting not allowed (*T. Arivandandam*).
7. **Discussion:** apply to the plaint paragraph by paragraph.
8. **Conclusion:** "The plaint, read as a whole, discloses / does not disclose a cause of action … The application is **accepted / dismissed**. Costs to follow the event." (If accepted: "The plaint is **rejected** under O. VII r. 11(__) CPC. Decree sheet be prepared.")

Announced: ___ · Civil Judge 1st Class, Islamabad (West) (seal)
:::

## 22.3 Format of a judgment (after trial) — O. XX r. 4(2)

1. Heading: court, case number, title, dates of **institution** and **decision**.
2. **Judgment**: brief facts of the plaint.
3. Brief facts of the written statement.
4. **Issues** framed (reproduce them with onus).
5. Evidence: list of PWs and DWs and exhibits (Ex. P-1, Ex. D-1 etc.).
6. Arguments of both sides (short).
7. **Issue-wise findings** with reasons — legal issues and factual issues — each ending with "Issue No. (x) is decided in favour of (party) and against (party)".
8. **Relief:** final conclusion, decree terms, costs (s. 35 actual costs in Islamabad), decree sheet, consignment.
9. Announcement in open court, date, signature and seal.

## 22.4 A 3-hour plan for the paper

- **10 minutes:** read all questions; choose; mark the parts.
- **Per 20-mark question (about 32–35 minutes):** 3 minutes planning (answer map like the companion volume), 28 minutes writing, 2 minutes checking.
- **Keep 10 minutes** at the end to complete headings, case names and conclusions.
- **Write on both sides** of the page (the roll number slip says no extra sheet will be provided).
