# Answer 1 · Kinds of jurisdiction of civil courts and how they are exercised (KPHC 2026)

:::glance KP HIGH COURT 2026 · CIVIL LAW-I · Q. 5 · 20 MARKS
What are the different types of jurisdictions of civil courts, and how are these exercised?
*(Related: SHC 2022, Q. 4 — "The different facets of the jurisdiction of a civil court".)*
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the model answer below](a1_map.png)

## Decoding the question

"What are the different types" — a **classify-and-explain** question. "How are these exercised" — the examiner wants the **source** of each kind of jurisdiction (CPC section or Civil Courts Ordinance section), **who** exercises it, and the **rules and limits** on its exercise (s. 15 lowest grade; s. 21 objections; nullity). Start with a definition and the basic rule of s. 9, explain each kind with its law and an example, then explain consequences of lack of jurisdiction, and end with the Islamabad position.

## The model answer

### 1. Introduction — the power to decide

#### i) Definition

**Jurisdiction** is the legal authority of a court to hear and decide a cause. It comes from *juris* (law) and *dictio* (saying): the power to declare the law in a case. A court gets jurisdiction only from the **Constitution or a statute**; parties cannot confer it by consent.

#### ii) The argument

A civil court's jurisdiction is not one single power but a bundle: it is limited by the **kind of dispute** (subject matter), by **money value** (pecuniary), by **place** (territorial), and by the **stage** of the case (original, appellate, revisional). The Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC) and the West Pakistan Civil Courts Ordinance 1962 together decide who exercises which power.

### 2. The foundation — s. 9 CPC

#### i) The rule

"The Courts shall (subject to the provisions herein contained) have jurisdiction to try **all suits of a civil nature** excepting suits of which their cognizance is either **expressly or impliedly barred**" (s. 9).

#### ii) Its meaning

Every civil right has a remedy (*ubi jus ibi remedium*). The civil court is the court of **general** jurisdiction; any exclusion must be shown and is strictly construed (*Secretary of State v. Mask & Co.*, AIR 1940 PC 105).

### 3. Subject-matter (inherent) jurisdiction

#### i) The kind

Power over the **nature** of the dispute — suits of a civil nature (property, office, contract, tort, status). Disputes given exclusively to special forums (Family Court, Rent Controller, Banking Court) are outside it.

#### ii) How exercised

The court examines the plaint to see whether the suit is civil and not barred (s. 9). If barred, the plaint is **rejected** (O. VII r. 11(d)). Lack of subject-matter jurisdiction makes the decree a **nullity** and cannot be cured by consent (*Kiran Singh v. Chaman Paswan*, AIR 1954 SC 340).

### 4. Pecuniary jurisdiction

#### i) The kind

Power limited by the **value** of the suit: "nothing herein contained shall operate to give any Court jurisdiction over suits the amount or value of the subject matter of which exceeds the pecuniary limits, if any, of its ordinary jurisdiction" (s. 6 CPC, as substituted by Act XIX of 2023).

#### ii) How exercised

The **District Judge** has jurisdiction "in original civil suits **without limit as regards the value**" (s. 7, Ordinance). The value limit of each **Civil Judge** "shall be determined by the High Court either by including him in a class or otherwise" (s. 9, Ordinance). The plaintiff's valuation in the plaint (O. VII r. 1(i)) ordinarily decides the forum; if undervalued, the court requires correction (O. VII r. 11(b)) and, if beyond its limit, returns the plaint (O. VII r. 10).

### 5. Territorial (local) jurisdiction

#### i) The kind

Power limited to a **geographical area**. The High Court defines a Civil Judge's local limits; otherwise the district is his limit (s. 10, Ordinance).

#### ii) How exercised — ss. 16 to 20 CPC

Suits about **immovable property** lie where the property is situate (s. 16; s. 17 if in several districts); suits for **wrongs to person or movables** lie where the wrong was done or the defendant resides (s. 19); **all other suits** lie where the defendant resides, carries on business or personally works for gain, or where the **cause of action** wholly or partly arose (s. 20).

#### iii) Illustration

Ayesha, living in Islamabad, buys a car in Lahore from Junaid of Karachi; the car proves stolen. She may sue in Lahore (cause of action) or Karachi (residence) — s. 20.

### 6. Original, appellate and revisional jurisdiction

#### i) Original

The power to try a case at **first instance** — suits (s. 26) and original petitions. In Islamabad, Civil Judges and the District Judge exercise it.

#### ii) Appellate

The power to rehear a decided case on facts and law. A first appeal lies from every original decree (s. 96); from **Civil Judges to the District Judge** (s. 18(1) Ordinance, as substituted for Islamabad by Act XV of 2025) and from the **District Judge / ADJ to the High Court** (s. 17); second appeal on law to the High Court (s. 100).

#### iii) Revisional

The supervisory power of the **High Court** (and of the **District Court** within its appellate limits) to correct a subordinate court that has exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it, failed to exercise a vested jurisdiction, or acted illegally or with material irregularity, in a case where **no appeal lies** (s. 115). Islamabad text: application within **ninety days**, to be decided within **six months**.

### 7. Exclusive and concurrent jurisdiction

#### i) Exclusive

Only one forum may decide (e.g., Family Court for dower and maintenance under the Family Courts Act 1964). The civil court's jurisdiction is ousted.

#### ii) Concurrent

Several courts have jurisdiction (e.g., residence and cause of action at different places). The plaintiff, as **dominus litis**, chooses; but he must sue in the **court of the lowest grade competent to try it** (s. 15); the defendant may seek transfer (s. 22) and the High Court / District Court may transfer or withdraw (s. 24).

### 8. Special jurisdictions under the Civil Courts Ordinance

#### i) Suits against the State

Suits in which Pakistan, a Province or a public officer in his official capacity is a party may be entertained **only** by a Civil Judge having jurisdiction **without limit as to value**, at district headquarters (s. 24).

#### ii) Small cause and succession matters

The High Court may confer **Small Cause Court** jurisdiction on Civil Judges (s. 11) and authorise them to hear **Succession Act** proceedings (s. 12).

### 9. Inherent powers — not a separate jurisdiction

"Nothing in this Code shall be deemed to limit or otherwise affect the inherent power of the Court **to be exercised after recording reasons** to make such orders as may be necessary for the ends of justice or to prevent abuse of the process of the Court" (s. 151, Islamabad text after Act VII of 2020). It supplements, but never creates, jurisdiction.

### 10. Objections and consequences of want of jurisdiction

#### i) Inherent defect

A decree without subject-matter jurisdiction is **void**; the objection can be raised at any stage, even in execution (*Kiran Singh*).

#### ii) Territorial and pecuniary defects

They are **waivable**. "No objection as to the place of suing shall be allowed by any appellate or revisional Court unless such objection was taken in the Court of first instance at the earliest possible opportunity … and unless there has been a consequent failure of justice" (s. 21). The same approach applies to valuation (s. 11 Suits Valuation Act 1887). No decree is reversed for an irregularity not affecting the merits or jurisdiction (s. 99).

### 11. Exercise within limits — ouster clauses

Even where a special statute ousts civil courts, the civil court may still examine orders **without jurisdiction, mala fide, coram non judice** or against natural justice (*Abbasia Cooperative Bank v. Hakeem Hafiz Muhammad Ghaus*, PLD 1997 SC 3; *Dhulabhai v. State of M.P.*, AIR 1969 SC 78).

### 12. Conclusion — one court, many powers

A civil court's jurisdiction is **general in subject** (s. 9) but **limited** by value (s. 6; ss. 7, 9 Ordinance), place (ss. 15–20) and stage (original, appellate, revisional). It is exercised by District Judges, ADJs and Civil Judges in the hierarchy laid down by the Civil Courts Ordinance, which s. 3 CPC adopts for Islamabad. The law treats defects of **inherent** jurisdiction as fatal, but defects of **place and value** as curable — balancing the rule of law against the need to end litigation.

## Why this answer scores

- It **defines** jurisdiction and anchors everything in **s. 9**, the master section.
- Each kind is explained with **its source** (CPC or Ordinance section) **and how it is exercised** — the second half of the question that weak answers ignore.
- **Islamabad-specific law** is used: s. 6 (2023), s. 18(1) Ordinance (2025), s. 115 time limits, s. 151 reasons.
- **Leading cases** support each legal point: *Kiran Singh*, *Mask & Co.*, *Dhulabhai*, *Abbasia Cooperative Bank*.
- The **consequences** of lack of jurisdiction (void vs waivable) show depth.

# Answer 2 · Forums of civil appeal under the Civil Courts Ordinance 1962 (KPHC 2026)

:::glance KP HIGH COURT 2026 · CIVIL LAW-I · Q. 6 · 20 MARKS
Explain the concept and forums of different civil appeals briefly as provided in the Civil Courts Ordinance, 1962.
*(Related: LHC 2023 General Law, Q. 10 — "Is it lawful for a Civil Judge to act as District Court for the purposes of appeal(s) preferred against the decree / order passed in any original suit by any Civil Judge?")*
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the model answer below](a2_map.png)

## Decoding the question

Two parts: (1) the **concept** of an appeal — what it is, that it is a creature of statute, and how the CPC and the Ordinance work together; (2) the **forums** — ss. 17 and 18 of the Ordinance, with the Islamabad amendment of 2025 and the special rule in s. 18(3). A first-class answer also explains the **second appeal and revision** that follow, and draws the full appellate map.

## The model answer

### 1. Introduction — appeal as a right given by statute

#### i) Meaning

An **appeal** is the removal of a case from a lower court to a higher court for a **rehearing** on facts and law, so that the higher court may confirm, reverse or vary the decision. A first appeal is a continuation of the suit: the appellate court has "the same powers … as are conferred … on Courts of original jurisdiction" (s. 107(2) CPC).

#### ii) A creature of statute

There is **no inherent right of appeal**. It exists only if a statute gives it. The CPC creates the **right** (s. 96: appeal from every original decree; s. 104 and O. XLIII: appeals from listed orders). The **Civil Courts Ordinance 1962** decides the **forum** — to which court the appeal goes. A right of appeal is a **substantive** right that vests on the date of the suit.

### 2. The courts in the hierarchy

"There shall be the following classes of Civil Courts, namely:— (a) the Court of the District Judge; (b) the Court of the Additional District Judge; and (c) the Court of the Civil Judge" (s. 3, Ordinance). For Islamabad, s. 3 CPC (as substituted by Act VII of 2020) adopts the same hierarchy for the purposes of the Code. All civil courts are subordinate to the High Court, and the District Judge controls the courts in his district (s. 14).

### 3. Appeals from District Judges and Additional District Judges — s. 17

#### i) The rule

"Save as otherwise provided by any enactment for the time being in force, an appeal from a decree or order of a District Judge or Additional District Judge exercising **original** jurisdiction shall lie to the **High Court**" (s. 17(1)).

#### ii) The limit for ADJs

"An appeal shall not lie to the High Court from a decree or order of an Additional District Judge in any case in which, if the decree or order had been made by the District Judge, an appeal would not lie to that Court" (s. 17(2)). The ADJ exercises the District Judge's powers (s. 6(2)); his orders have no wider appealability than the District Judge's.

### 4. Appeals from Civil Judges — s. 18(1) (Islamabad, 2025)

#### i) The rule after Act XV of 2025

"Save as aforesaid, an appeal from a decree or order of a Civil Judge shall lie to the **District Judge**" (s. 18(1), substituted by Act No. XV of 2025 in its application to the Islamabad Capital Territory).

#### ii) What changed

Previously, s. 18(1) split appeals by **value** — high-value appeals from Civil Judges went to the High Court. In Islamabad, **all** appeals from Civil Judges now go to the District Judge, irrespective of value. The IHC accordingly transferred pending appeals of this kind to the District Judges in 2025. The amendment brings appeals closer to the litigant and reduces the High Court's first-appeal workload.

### 5. Appeals to an Additional District Judge — s. 18(2)

"Where the function of receiving any appeals which lie to the District Judge under the last preceding sub-section has been assigned to an Additional District Judge, the appeal may be preferred to the Additional District Judge." In practice, the District Judge distributes appeals among ADJs under s. 15.

### 6. A Civil Judge as appellate court — s. 18(3)

#### i) The rule

"The High Court may, **by notification**, direct that appeals lying to the District Judge from all or any of the decrees or orders passed in any original suit by any Civil Judge shall be referred to such other Civil Judge as may be mentioned in the notification … and the Court of such Civil Judge shall be **deemed to be a District Court** for the purposes of all appeals so preferred."

#### ii) Answer to the LHC 2023 question

It is lawful for a Civil Judge to hear appeals **only** if the High Court has issued a notification under s. 18(3). Without it, a Civil Judge has no appellate jurisdiction; an appeal decided by him would be **coram non judice** and void.

### 7. Appeals from orders

Only orders listed in **s. 104 CPC** and **O. XLIII r. 1** are appealable — e.g., an order returning a plaint (r. 1(a)), refusing to set aside an ex parte decree (r. 1(d)), or granting or refusing a temporary injunction (r. 1(r)). Such appeals go to the court to which an appeal from the decree in that suit would lie (s. 106 CPC) — so, in Islamabad, from a Civil Judge to the District Judge.

### 8. Further remedies — second appeal and revision

#### i) Second appeal — s. 100 CPC

From an appellate decree of the District Judge / ADJ to the **High Court**, on law only: decision contrary to law or usage having the force of law; failure to determine a material issue of law; or a substantial procedural error that may have affected the decision on merits.

#### ii) Revision — s. 115 CPC

Where no appeal lies, the High Court (or the District Court within its appellate limits) may revise jurisdictional errors. The Islamabad text requires the application "within ninety days" of the decision, with copies of pleadings and the order, and disposal "within six months".

### 9. The complete appellate map for Islamabad

![Appeals from civil courts in Islamabad after the 2025 amendment](a2_appeals.png)

### 10. Rationale of the scheme

- **Two levels of factual review, one of legal review:** trial court → first appeal (facts and law) → second appeal (law only). Facts are finally settled at the first appellate stage.
- **Proximity and cost:** appeals from Civil Judges are heard within the district.
- **Uniformity of law:** the High Court's second-appeal and revisional powers keep the law uniform across the ICT.

### 11. Conclusion

Under the Civil Courts Ordinance, the **forum** of a civil appeal follows the **rank** of the court that decided the case: District Judge / ADJ (original) → **High Court** (s. 17); Civil Judge → **District Judge** in every case in Islamabad after Act XV of 2025 (s. 18(1)), or an ADJ to whom appeals are assigned (s. 18(2)), or a Civil Judge notified by the High Court (s. 18(3)). The CPC supplies the right (ss. 96, 104), the further remedies (ss. 100, 115) and the procedure (O. XLI).

## Why this answer scores

- It separates the **right** of appeal (CPC) from the **forum** (Ordinance) — the key conceptual point.
- It states the **2025 Islamabad amendment** of s. 18(1) — few candidates will know it.
- It directly answers the **LHC 2023 question** on s. 18(3) with the consequence (coram non judice).
- The **appellate map** figure shows the whole system at a glance.

# Answer 3 · "Courts to try all civil suits unless barred" — s. 9 CPC (LHC ASJ 2020)

:::glance LHC ASJ EXAM 2020 · CIVIL LAW-II · Q. 2 · 20 MARKS
Courts to try all civil suits unless barred. Discuss all aspects.
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the model answer below](a3_map.png)

## Decoding the question

"Discuss all aspects" means: the **text** of s. 9, its **two conditions** (civil nature; no bar), the **Explanation**, the **presumption** in favour of jurisdiction, **express and implied bars**, the **limits of ouster clauses**, who bears the **burden**, how the court **decides** the question, and the **consequence** of lack of jurisdiction. Use cases from Pakistan, India and the Privy Council.

## The model answer

### 1. Introduction — the charter of civil jurisdiction

"The Courts shall (subject to the provisions herein contained) have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature excepting suits of which their cognizance is either expressly or impliedly barred" (s. 9 CPC). The section expresses the maxim **ubi jus ibi remedium** — where there is a right, there is a remedy — and gives every person a forum for the enforcement of civil rights.

### 2. The two conditions

A civil court has jurisdiction under s. 9 when (i) the suit is **of a civil nature**, and (ii) its cognizance is **not barred**, expressly or impliedly. Both must be satisfied.

### 3. "Suit of a civil nature"

#### i) Meaning

"Civil" refers to **private rights and remedies** of a person, as distinguished from criminal, political or purely religious matters. A suit is of a civil nature if its **principal question** concerns a civil right — property, office, status, contract or tort.

#### ii) Case law

In *Most Rev. P.M.A. Metropolitan v. Moran Mar Marthoma* (AIR 1995 SC 2001) the Supreme Court of India held that the language of s. 9 is mandatory ("shall"); the court is **bound** to try every civil suit unless barred. In *Ganga Bai v. Vijay Kumar* (AIR 1974 SC 1126) it held that every person has an **inherent right** to bring a civil suit; no statutory authority is needed to sue, only the absence of a bar.

#### iii) Illustrations

Civil: right to property, to an office with emoluments (imam, mutawalli), to worship in a public mosque, to salary, to damages. Not civil: a mere question of social precedence, caste dignity or religious honour without any right to property or office.

### 4. The Explanation — religious questions

"A suit in which the right to property or to an office is contested is a suit of a civil nature, notwithstanding that such right may depend entirely on the decision of questions as to religious rites or ceremonies." So a suit about the right to the office of mutawalli remains civil even if the court must decide a religious qualification.

### 5. Express bar

A statute may say in words that civil courts shall not entertain a matter — e.g., the Family Courts Act 1964 (exclusive jurisdiction of Family Courts over matters in its Schedule), or bar clauses in the West Pakistan Land Revenue Act 1967 for matters within revenue officers' jurisdiction. The court must give effect to the bar **within its terms**.

### 6. Implied bar

#### i) When implied

Without words of exclusion, a bar is implied where (a) a statute **creates a new right** unknown to the general law and in the same statute provides a **complete machinery** — forum, appeal and revision — to enforce it; or (b) **public policy** forbids the suit (e.g., a suit for a gambling debt, or about an act of State).

#### ii) Example

The Islamabad Rent Restriction Ordinance 2001 creates the landlord's statutory right to evict on specified grounds and gives exclusive jurisdiction to the Rent Controller. A civil suit for eviction of a tenant is barred.

### 7. Presumption and strict construction

The exclusion of civil court jurisdiction "is not to be readily inferred; such exclusion must be either explicitly expressed or clearly implied" (*Secretary of State v. Mask & Co.*, AIR 1940 PC 105). The **burden** lies on the party who asserts the bar, and ouster provisions are construed strictly, in favour of jurisdiction.

### 8. Limits of ouster clauses

#### i) The Pakistani rule

Even where a statute ousts the civil court, it may still examine whether the special forum acted **without jurisdiction, mala fide, coram non judice**, or in violation of natural justice — because such acts are not acts "under" the statute (*Abbasia Cooperative Bank v. Hakeem Hafiz Muhammad Ghaus*, PLD 1997 SC 3).

#### ii) The Dhulabhai tests

*Dhulabhai v. State of M.P.* (AIR 1969 SC 78) holds that finality clauses exclude civil courts only where the statute provides an **adequate remedy**; civil courts may still examine non-compliance with the Act or with fundamental principles of judicial procedure; challenges to the **vires** of the Act may be brought by suit; and where a statute creates a special right with a special remedy, that remedy is ordinarily exclusive.

### 9. How the court decides the question

Jurisdiction is decided first on the **averments in the plaint** (O. VII r. 11(d) — "barred by any law"). If the bar is apparent from the plaint, the plaint is rejected; if it depends on disputed facts, an issue is framed and, where it is a pure question of law, tried first (O. XIV r. 2).

### 10. Consequences of lack of jurisdiction

A decree passed by a court that lacked subject-matter jurisdiction is a **nullity** and can be challenged at any stage, even in execution (*Kiran Singh v. Chaman Paswan*, AIR 1954 SC 340); in Pakistan, a decree passed without jurisdiction may be challenged under **s. 12(2)** CPC by application to the court that passed it.

### 11. Illustration — applying s. 9

The Capital Housing Authority cancels Bilal's plot allotment without notice; its statute says its orders "shall not be called in question in any court". Bilal sues for a declaration that the cancellation is void. The suit is **maintainable**: the cancellation without hearing violates natural justice and is not an order "under" the statute (*Abbasia Cooperative Bank*). Had the Authority given notice and decided within its powers, the civil court could not reappraise the merits.

### 12. Conclusion

Section 9 makes the civil court the **ordinary guardian of civil rights**: jurisdiction is the rule, exclusion the exception. A bar must be express or clearly implied, is strictly construed, and never protects acts outside the statute, in bad faith or against natural justice. This balance respects the legislature's power to create special forums while preserving the citizen's access to justice under Articles 4 and 10A of the Constitution.

- **Authority at every step**: Privy Council (*Mask*), India (*Metropolitan*, *Ganga Bai*, *Dhulabhai*, *Kiran Singh*) and Pakistan (*Abbasia Cooperative Bank*).

- Constitutional link (Arts. 4, 10A) in the conclusion lifts the answer above a textbook summary.
