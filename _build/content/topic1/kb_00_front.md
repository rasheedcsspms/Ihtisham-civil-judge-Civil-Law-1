# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 1 covers

Civil Law Paper I has four parts: the **Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC)**, the **West Pakistan Civil Courts Ordinance 1962**, the **Contract Act 1872** and the **Sale of Goods Act 1930**. This volume is Topic 1. It covers:

- the **civil courts** of Islamabad — who they are, their powers and where appeals go (the Civil Courts Ordinance 1962, the Islamabad High Court Act 2010 and s. 3 CPC);
- the **foundations of the CPC** — its purpose, its structure and its key definitions (ss. 1–2);
- **jurisdiction** — pecuniary, territorial and subject-matter; the bar of s. 9; the place of suing (ss. 6, 9, 15–21);
- the three great **bars to a second case** — res sub judice (s. 10), res judicata (s. 11) and the bar of rules (s. 12(1)) — plus the special Pakistani remedy of **s. 12(2)** against a decree obtained by fraud;
- **transfer**, **institution**, **summons**, **discovery** and **costs** (ss. 22–35C);
- the **First Schedule, Orders I to XVII** — parties, frame of suit, pleadings, plaint, written statement, appearance and default, examination of parties, discovery, admissions, documents, **issues**, witnesses and adjournments.

Topic 2 will take the story forward: interim orders, judgment and decree, execution, appeals, review, revision and special suits.

## How each chapter is built

Every chapter follows the same pattern so that you can revise fast:

1. **The idea in plain words** — what the provision does and why you should care.
2. **The law** — the key words of the section or rule, quoted from the official text (shown in a blue quotation bar).
3. **Ingredients / conditions** — numbered, so that you can reproduce them in the exam.
4. **Rationale** — *why* the law says this (green box). Examiners reward reasons, not just rules.
5. **Case studies** — facts, decision and **ratio decidendi** (blue box).
6. **Legal maxims** — Latin phrase, meaning and use (purple box).
7. **Illustrations** — worked examples with Pakistani names and rupees (grey box).
8. **Exam tips and common mistakes** (orange and red boxes).
9. **Quick recap** at the end of the chapter (green box).

Boxes marked **Islamabad-specific law** show where the law in the Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) is different from the text in most textbooks, which usually print the Punjab version.

:::ict Why the Islamabad text of the CPC is different
After the 18th Amendment (2010), civil procedure is no longer on a shared federal list. Each province now amends the CPC for itself, and **Parliament amends it for the Islamabad Capital Territory**. So the CPC in force in Islamabad is the **federal text** as amended by Acts of Parliament, for example Act XLVIII of 2016, Act XVII of 2017 (actual costs), Act VII of 2020 and Act XIX of 2023. Punjab, Sindh, KP and Balochistan have their own amendments, which do **not** apply in Islamabad. When you quote a section in the IHC paper, quote the federal text. This knowledge base does that.
:::

## How the Islamabad paper is set — and what that means for you

The IHC syllabus (Appendix-C, para 5) says: *"The object of the examination is to test the practical ability of the candidates rather than the range of their theoretical knowledge. For this purpose the kind of questions that asked shall be to give the facts of a typical case and ask the candidate to frame issues, to write a judgment and to discuss the admissibility of evidence."*

The same Appendix says that **bare copies of the relevant Acts can be consulted** (except for Muslim law, Hudood laws, Islamic jurisprudence and pleadings). This changes how you should prepare:

- **Do not try to memorise section numbers by heart.** You will have the bare Act. Instead, know *where* things are and *how to use* them.
- **Know the reasons, the case law and the tests.** The bare Act will not give you the ratio of *Satyadhyan Ghosal* or the test for "sufficient cause". This knowledge base will.
- **Practise problem answers.** Most marks go to the candidate who spots the issue, states the rule, applies it to the facts and gives a clear conclusion.
- **Practise framing issues and writing short orders.** Chapter 18 (issues) and Chapter 22 (exam technique) show you exactly how.

:::tip How to use your bare Act in the exam hall
Before the paper starts, flag (with small paper slips if allowed, or by memory of page numbers) these places in your CPC: s. 2 (definitions), s. 9, ss. 10–12, ss. 15–21, s. 24, ss. 35–35C, s. 96, s. 115, s. 151, and Orders I, II, VI, VII (rr. 10–11), VIII, IX (rr. 6–13), XIV and XVII (r. 3). In the Civil Courts Ordinance, flag ss. 3, 7, 9, 17, 18 and 24. Quoting the exact words from the bare Act — inside quotation marks, with the section number — looks professional and earns marks.
:::

:::note About the case law in this volume
Case citations are given only where they are well established and reliable. Where the Supreme Court of Pakistan and the High Courts have settled a principle through many judgments, the principle is stated as "settled law" without loading you with doubtful citations — in the exam it is safer to state a correct principle without a citation than to quote a wrong citation. Classic Indian and English cases are included where Pakistani courts follow them, because the CPC is common to both countries and these cases explain the reasoning best.
:::
