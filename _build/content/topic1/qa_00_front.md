# How to use these model answers

## Why these ten questions

No past paper of the Islamabad High Court's civil judge examination is publicly available. The questions below are therefore taken from the papers of courts that set the **same or a similar syllabus**:

- the **Peshawar High Court's 2026 Civil Law-I paper**, whose syllabus is identical to Islamabad's (CPC, Civil Courts Ordinance, Contract Act, Sale of Goods Act);
- the **Sindh High Court's 2022** civil law paper;
- the **Lahore High Court's** 2017, 2020 and 2023 papers (where the CPC is in Civil Law-II and General Law), including its ASJ paper of 2020; and
- one **model question** written in the exact pattern described in the IHC syllabus: *"give the facts of a typical case and ask the candidate to frame issues, to write a judgment"*.

Together they cover every high-yield area of Topic 1: jurisdiction, civil courts and appeals, s. 9, res judicata, s. 12(2), plaint, parties, ex parte proceedings, issues, and a court order.

## The five parts of every answer

1. **At a glance** — the source, the marks and the exact question.
2. **The answer map** — a one-page picture of the answer: each box is one numbered heading of the model answer. In the exam, spend two or three minutes drawing a rough version of this before you write.
3. **Decoding the question** — what the examiner is really asking and how to structure the answer.
4. **The model answer** — written the way a top candidate would write it in the hall: numbered headings, short paragraphs, the bare Act quoted, one leading case per point, and an illustration.
5. **Why this answer scores** — the features that earn marks, so you can copy the technique in any other question.

:::tip Length and timing
A 20-mark answer should take about **30–35 minutes**. The model answers here are slightly longer than you need, so that you have a complete source to learn from. In the exam, keep every **heading** and every **case**, and shorten the explanation under each heading to two or three sentences.
:::
