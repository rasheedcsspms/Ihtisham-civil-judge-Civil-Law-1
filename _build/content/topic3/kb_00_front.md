# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 3 covers

The Contract Act 1872 is the foundation of commercial law in Pakistan. Its first 75 sections state the **general principles** that apply to **every** contract — a loan, a sale of land, a building contract, employment, a bank guarantee. Topic 4 will take the **special contracts** (indemnity, guarantee, bailment, pledge, agency) and the **Sale of Goods Act 1930**. This volume covers:

1. the **nature of a contract** and its essentials (ss. 2, 10);
2. **proposal and acceptance**, communication and revocation (ss. 3–9);
3. **consideration** and privity, and agreements without consideration (ss. 2(d), 25);
4. **capacity** — minors and persons of unsound mind (ss. 11–12, 68);
5. **free consent** — coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation and mistake (ss. 13–22);
6. **lawful consideration and object** (ss. 23–24);
7. **void agreements** — restraint of marriage, trade and legal proceedings, uncertainty and **wagers** (ss. 26–30C);
8. **contingent contracts** (ss. 31–36);
9. **performance** — by whom, when, where; reciprocal promises; time as of the essence; appropriation of payments (ss. 37–61);
10. **discharge** — novation, remission, rescission and **frustration** (ss. 56, 62–67);
11. **quasi-contracts** (ss. 65, 68–72);
12. **remedies for breach** — damages, penalty and liquidated damages (ss. 73–75); and
13. **terms of a contract** — the intention of the parties and the relative importance of terms (conditions, warranties, innominate terms).

The pattern of every chapter is the same as in Topics 1 and 2: the idea in plain words, the law (quoted), ingredients, **rationale**, **case studies** with ratio decidendi, **maxims**, **illustrations**, exam tips and common mistakes, and a quick recap.

:::ict The Contract Act as it applies in Pakistan
The Contract Act is a **federal** law that "extends to the whole of Pakistan" (s. 1). A few Pakistani features differ from the Indian Act, on which many textbooks are based:
1. **Sections 30A–30C** (inserted by Ordinance XLVII of 1960) make **agreements collateral to wagering agreements void**, bar suits for brokerage or commission on them, and deny credit to guardians and executors for such payments. In India, collateral agreements remain valid (*Gherulal Parakh v. Mahadeodas Maiya*, AIR 1959 SC 781).
2. **Section 74** carries **illustrations (d)–(g)** (added by Act VI of 1899) on increased interest and acceleration clauses.
3. **Section 27** has only the **goodwill** exception; the partnership exceptions were moved to the **Partnership Act 1932**.
4. The chapters on **sale of goods** (ss. 76–123) and **partnership** (ss. 239–266) were repealed by the Sale of Goods Act 1930 and the Partnership Act 1932.
5. Contracts are also tested against the **Injunctions of Islam** (Art. 227 of the Constitution); provisions involving **riba** (interest) have been the subject of Federal Shariat Court proceedings — a point to remember when discussing interest under s. 74.
:::

:::tip Use the illustrations in the Act
The Contract Act is full of statutory **illustrations**. They are part of the Act and are excellent authority. In the exam (with the bare Act in hand), quote the illustration that fits your facts — e.g., "This is exactly illustration (c) to s. 25." It shows mastery and saves time.
:::
