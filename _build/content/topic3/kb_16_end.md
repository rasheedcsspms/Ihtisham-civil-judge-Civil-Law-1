# Chapter 15 · The great distinctions of Topic 3

{w:25,37,38}
|Pair|First|Second|
|Agreement vs contract|Every promise or set of promises forming consideration for each other (s. 2(e))|An agreement enforceable by law (s. 2(h)); every contract is an agreement, not vice versa|
|Void agreement vs voidable contract|Never enforceable; no rights for third parties|Valid until avoided; bona fide third-party rights protected (ss. 19, 19A, 55)|
|Void vs illegal|Merely unenforceable; collateral agreements valid (except wagers in Pakistan, s. 30A)|Forbidden; collateral agreements tainted; no restitution (*in pari delicto*)|
|Offer vs invitation to treat|Willingness to be bound on acceptance (s. 2(a))|Invitation to make offers — displays, tenders, catalogues (*Boots*; *Spencer v. Harding*)|
|Counter-offer vs inquiry|Rejects and kills the offer (*Hyde v. Wrench*)|Seeks information; offer remains alive|
|Postal vs instantaneous acceptance|Proposer bound on posting (s. 4)|Complete when and where received / heard (*Entores*; *Bhagwandas Kedia*)|
|Consideration vs object|Price of the promise — what is given|Purpose of the agreement — what is sought|
|Privity of contract vs privity of consideration|Only parties may sue (followed in Pakistan, with exceptions)|Consideration may move from a third person — not required (s. 2(d); *Chinnaya*)|
|s. 25(3) vs s. 19 Limitation Act|New written, signed promise to pay a **time-barred** debt — new contract|Acknowledgment **before** expiry — fresh period for old debt|
|Coercion vs undue influence|PPC act / detention of property; by anyone; no relationship|Domination + unfair advantage; relationship essential; burden shift (s. 16(3))|
|Fraud vs misrepresentation|Knowing / reckless falsehood; damages in deceit|Honest but unwarranted belief; no damages; s. 19 Exception applies|
|Mistake of fact vs mistake of law|Bilateral and essential — void (s. 20)|Pakistani law — no relief; foreign law treated as fact (s. 21)|
|Bilateral vs unilateral mistake|Void (s. 20)|Not voidable (s. 22) — except identity, *non est factum*|
|Wager vs contingent contract|Mutual chances; no interest but the stake; void (s. 30)|Collateral uncertain event; valid (s. 31) — insurance|
|Wager vs insurance|No insurable interest; chance|Insurable interest; indemnity; utmost good faith|
|Frustration vs force majeure|By law (s. 56); contract becomes void|By contract; effect as agreed; governed by s. 32|
|Rescission (s. 62) vs rescission (s. 64)|By mutual agreement — discharge|By the party entitled to avoid a voidable contract — restores benefits|
|Novation vs alteration|New contract replaces old (may change parties)|Terms changed; same parties; same contract|
|Penalty vs liquidated damages|English law: penalty unenforceable; liquidated sum recoverable in full|Pakistani s. 74: no distinction — reasonable compensation up to the named sum (*Fateh Chand*)|
|Earnest money vs part-price|Guarantee of performance — reasonable sum forfeitable|Part of consideration — forfeiture is a penalty (*Fateh Chand*; *Maula Bux*)|
|General vs special damages|Arise naturally (first limb)|From communicated special circumstances (second limb) — *Hadley v. Baxendale*|
|Condition vs warranty|Essential; termination + damages (SGA s. 12(2))|Collateral; damages only (SGA s. 12(3))|
|Contract vs quasi-contract|Created by agreement|Imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment (ss. 68–72)|
|Restitution vs compensation|Return of a benefit received (ss. 64, 65, 72)|Making good a loss suffered (ss. 73–75)|

# Chapter 16 · Exam technique — contract problems

## 16.1 The five-step method for a contract problem

![The five-step method for a contract problem](problem_method.png)

1. **Formation** — Was there an offer? Acceptance? Was it communicated (s. 4)? Was it revoked (ss. 5–6)? Consideration (s. 2(d), s. 25)? Intention to create legal relations?
2. **Validity** — Capacity (ss. 11–12)? Free consent (ss. 14–22)? Lawful object (s. 23)? Expressly void (ss. 24–30A, 56)? Form (writing / registration / attestation — Art. 17 QSO)?
3. **Performance and discharge** — Was it performed or tendered? Is the non-performance excused (s. 56, s. 63, s. 67)? Was time of the essence (s. 55)?
4. **Breach** — Actual or anticipatory? Was the repudiation accepted (s. 39)?
5. **Remedy** — Damages (s. 73: limbs, illustrations, mitigation); penalty (s. 74); rescission (ss. 64, 75); restitution (s. 65, s. 70); specific performance / injunction (SRA).

## 16.2 How to write the answer

1. **Open** with a one-line statement of the issue: "The question raises the rules of acceptance by e-mail, counter-offer and revocation under ss. 2, 4–7 of the Contract Act 1872."
2. **Quote** the decisive words of the section (you have the bare Act).
3. **Cite one case per point**, with its ratio in a sentence.
4. **Use the statutory illustrations** — they are often the exact answer.
5. **Apply** the law to every fact given — examiners put each fact in for a reason.
6. **Show both sides** where the law is unsettled (e.g., restitution against a minor; e-mail acceptance), and give your **reasoned conclusion**.
7. **Add the Pakistani touch** — ss. 30A–30C, s. 74 illustrations (d)–(g), Art. 17 QSO, Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002, procurement rules, the riba position.
8. **Conclude** clearly: who wins, what relief, and why.

## 16.3 A 3-hour plan (reminder)

Ten minutes reading; about 33 minutes per 20-mark question; a quick answer map; quote the Act; one case per point; Pakistani features; clear conclusion; ten minutes for review.
