# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 1 covers

1. **The nature** of the law of evidence, the scheme of the Order and its **Islamic** features.
2. **Definitions** (Art. 2) — the vocabulary every answer must use correctly.
3. **Witnesses** — who may testify, the Islamic qualifications and number of witnesses, **privileged communications**, self-incrimination, the **accomplice**.
4. **Relevancy** — facts in issue and relevant facts (Arts. 18–29), the *res gestae*, motive and conduct, **alibi**, state of mind, similar facts.
5. **Admissions and confessions** (Arts. 30–45).
6. **Hearsay exceptions** — **dying declarations**, business entries, electronic records, former evidence (Arts. 46–53).
7. **Judgments**, **expert opinion** and **character** (Arts. 54–69).

:::ict How the Qanun-e-Shahadat differs from the Evidence Act 1872
The Order (P.O. X of 1984, 28 October 1984) **repealed the Evidence Act 1872** (Art. 166) and **renumbered** its sections; most rules are the same, but it added **Islamic** provisions and later **technology** provisions:
1. Competence and **number of witnesses** follow the **Injunctions of Islam** (Arts. 3 third proviso, 17); a person **convicted of perjury** is incompetent unless he has repented (Art. 3).
2. **Two male, or one male and two female, attesting witnesses** for documents of **financial or future obligations** (Art. 17(2)(a)).
3. An **accomplice** is not a competent witness in **hadd** cases (Art. 16); a confession obtained by deception etc. is not relevant in **Hudood** trials (Art. 42 proviso).
4. **Shahada ala al-shahadah** — a witness who cannot attend may appoint **two witnesses** to depose for him (Art. 71, third proviso), except in Hudood.
5. Presumption of **legitimacy** follows Muslim law (**six lunar months**, **two years**) (Art. 128).
6. **Decision on oath** (Art. 163).
7. **Electronic evidence** — Arts. 2(1)(e)–(f), 30 Explanation, 46A, 59, 73 Explanations 3–4, 78A, 85(6) (Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002), and **modern devices** — video call, WhatsApp, video conference (Art. 164, as substituted in 2023).
8. **Character of a rape victim** can no longer be attacked to impeach her credit (Art. 151(4) **omitted** in 2016).
:::
