
# Chapter 8 · Cognizance and complaints (ss. 190 to 205)

## 8.1 Cognizance by Magistrates — s. 190

All Magistrates of the **first class** (and others specially empowered) may take cognizance of an offence (a) upon receiving a **complaint** of facts; (b) upon a **police report** in writing; (c) upon **information** from any other person, or upon their **own knowledge or suspicion**. A Magistrate who takes cognizance of an offence **triable exclusively by a Court of Session** shall, **without recording evidence**, **send** the case to the Court of Session (s. 190(2)). "Taking cognizance" is the **application of judicial mind** to the facts for the purpose of proceeding — not merely receiving papers.

## 8.2 Bars to cognizance — ss. 195 to 199

|s. 195(1)(a)|Contempt of lawful authority of public servants (PPC ss. 172–188) — only on the **written complaint of the public servant** concerned or his superior|
|s. 195(1)(b)|Offences against **public justice** (false evidence ss. 193–196, 199, 200, 205–211, 228) committed in or in relation to a court proceeding — only on the **written complaint of that court** or its superior (procedure — s. 476)|
|s. 195(1)(c)|**Forgery** (s. 463) or using forged documents (ss. 471, 475, 476) by a **party** in respect of a document **produced in court** — only on the court's complaint|
|s. 196|Offences against the **State** (Chapter VI), ss. 153A, 295A, 505 etc. — only on complaint by order of the **Federal or Provincial Government**|
|s. 197|**Judges, Magistrates** and public servants removable only by Government — for acts in the discharge of official duty — **previous sanction** of the President or Governor|
|s. 198|**Breach of contract, defamation** (Chapter XXI) and **offences against marriage** (ss. 493–496) — only on complaint by the **person aggrieved** (with leave, by another for a woman who does not appear in public, a minor, an idiot, or a sick person)|
|s. 199|(Former) adultery and enticing a married woman — on complaint by the husband|

## 8.3 Complaints to Magistrates — ss. 200 to 205

1. **s. 200** — the Magistrate taking cognizance on a complaint shall **at once examine the complainant on oath**; the substance is reduced to writing and signed. Exceptions — a written complaint by a **court** or a **public servant** in official duty; a case being transferred.
2. **s. 202** — **postponement of process**: for reasons recorded, the court may **inquire itself** or direct an **inquiry or investigation** by a **Justice of the Peace**, a police officer or any other person, to ascertain the truth of the complaint; a Court of Session may direct an inquiry by a subordinate Magistrate.
3. **s. 203** — **dismissal** of the complaint if, after considering the complainant's statement and the inquiry result, there is **no sufficient ground for proceeding** (brief reasons recorded). Dismissal is **not acquittal** — a fresh complaint lies on new material.
4. **s. 203A** — **zina** complaint: only on a **complaint in court**; the court examines the complainant and **at least four Muslim adult male eye-witnesses** (subject to **tazkiyah-al-shuhood**) of the act of **penetration**; summons only if sufficient ground (Topic 4).
5. **s. 203C** — **fornication** (s. 496B PPC): only on a complaint in court; the complainant and **at least two eye-witnesses** are examined on oath; **no security** except a personal bond; no fornication complaint against a person accused of zina or against a **rape complainant or victim**.
6. **s. 204** — **issue of process**: summons or warrant according to **column 4 of Schedule II**; process fees.
7. **s. 205** — the Magistrate may **dispense with the personal attendance** of the accused and permit appearance by a pleader.

# Chapter 9 · The charge (ss. 221 to 240) — KPHC 2026; ASJ 2025; SHC 2022

## 9.1 Purpose

The charge tells the accused **precisely** what he must meet, so that he can prepare his **defence**. It is the **foundation** of the trial; in Sessions trials it is framed after the documents are supplied (s. 265D), and in Magistrate trials after the evidence or documents show a prima facie case (s. 242).

## 9.2 Contents of a charge — ss. 221 to 224

1. **The offence** (s. 221(1)); its **specific name** if the law gives one (s. 221(2)); if not, enough of the definition to give notice (s. 221(3)).
2. **The law and section** (s. 221(4)).
3. The charge is equivalent to a statement that **every legal condition** required to constitute the offence was fulfilled (s. 221(5)).
4. **Language** — English or the language of the court (s. 221(6)).
5. **Previous conviction** — the fact, date and place, where enhanced punishment is sought (s. 221(7)).
6. **Particulars of time, place and person** or thing (s. 222(1)); for **criminal breach of trust or misappropriation of money**, the **gross sum** and the dates between which it was committed — one offence, provided the period does not exceed **one year** (s. 222(2)).
7. **Manner** of committing the offence where ss. 221–222 do not give sufficient notice (s. 223) — needed for **cheating**, **false evidence**, obstruction of a public servant; not for theft or murder.
8. **Words** used in the charge are taken in the sense of the law (s. 224).

## 9.3 Effect of errors, alteration — ss. 225 to 232

1. **s. 225** — no error or omission in the charge is **material** unless the accused was **in fact misled** and it has occasioned a **failure of justice** (*illus.*: wrong name of the deceased and wrong date — immaterial where the accused knew which murder was meant).
2. **s. 227** — any court may **alter or add to** a charge **at any time before judgment** is pronounced; the alteration is **read and explained** to the accused.
3. **ss. 228–229** — the trial proceeds at once if there is **no prejudice**; otherwise a **new trial** or adjournment.
4. **s. 230** — stay where the altered charge needs **previous sanction**.
5. **s. 231** — witnesses may be **recalled** after alteration.
6. **s. 232** — an appellate or revisional court finding that the accused was **misled** by the absence of or an error in the charge shall order a **new trial**; if no valid charge could be framed on the facts, it shall **quash** the conviction.

## 9.4 Joinder of charges — ss. 233 to 240

**The basic rule (s. 233):** for **every distinct offence** there shall be a **separate charge**, and every charge shall be **tried separately** — except in the cases of ss. 234, 235, 236 and 239.

{w:16,84}
|s. 234|**Three offences of the same kind within twelve months** — whether against the same person or not — may be charged and tried together. "Same kind" — punishable with the same amount under the same section; **theft (s. 379) and theft in a dwelling (s. 380)** are the same kind; an offence and its **attempt** are the same kind.|
|s. 235(1)|Offences committed in **one series of acts so connected as to form the same transaction** — e.g., rescuing a prisoner and causing hurt to the constable (ss. 225, 333)|
|s. 235(2)|An act falling within **two definitions** — e.g., rioting and grievous hurt|
|s. 235(3)|Acts which separately constitute offences and **combined** a different offence — e.g., house-breaking with intent to commit adultery and the adultery|
|s. 236|Where it is **doubtful which of several offences** the facts will constitute — charge with all or **in the alternative** (theft, or receiving, or CBT, or cheating; contradictory statements on oath — false evidence)|
|s. 237|Conviction of an offence **not charged** where it could have been charged under s. 236|
|s. 238|Conviction of a **minor (included) offence** — where the offence charged consists of several particulars and some only are proved (e.g., charged with robbery, convicted of theft)|
|s. 239|**Joint trial of several persons** — (a) the same offence in the same transaction; (b) an offence and its **abetment or attempt**; (c) offences of the same kind committed **jointly** within 12 months; (d) **different offences in the same transaction**; (e) theft, extortion or misappropriation and **receiving** the property; (f) receiving property from the same distribution; (g) offences under ss. 231–233 and 241–243 relating to counterfeit coin|
|s. 240|On conviction on one of several charges, the **remaining charges may be withdrawn** with the court's consent — operates as **acquittal** on them|

## 9.5 Misjoinder and non-joinder — impact on the trial

1. **Misjoinder** — joining charges or accused **not permitted** by ss. 234–239 (e.g., four thefts in one trial; unconnected offences of different persons). Earlier view (*Subrahmania Ayyar v. King-Emperor*, 1901, PC) — an **illegality** vitiating the trial. Present view in Pakistan — a misjoinder is an **irregularity curable under s. 537** unless it has **occasioned a failure of justice** (prejudice to the defence, confusion of evidence). The appellate court may order a **retrial**.
2. **Non-joinder** — failure to join charges or accused who **could** have been tried together — **no illegality**, since separate trial is the **rule** (s. 233); at most inconvenience or **inconsistent findings**.
3. **Absence of a charge** where one was required — the conviction is set aside and a **new trial** ordered if the accused was misled (s. 232); or quashed if no valid charge could be framed.

## 9.6 How to frame a charge (model)

:::eg Charge — a robbery on a highway (ASJ 2025 type)
I, (name), Additional Sessions Judge, Islamabad (West), do hereby charge you, (1) **Salman** son of …, and (2) **Noman** son of …, as follows:

That on **12.03.2022** at about **3:45 p.m.**, near **Liaquat Flyover**, within the area of police station …, you both, **armed with 9 mm pistols** and riding a motorcycle, **in furtherance of your common intention**, intercepted the car No. ABC-123 driven by **Adnan** with **Farooq**, put them in fear of **instant death** at gun-point and **robbed** them of (cash Rs. …, mobile phones …), and thereby committed an offence punishable under **section 392 read with section 34 of the Pakistan Penal Code**, which is within the cognizance of this Court.

Secondly, that at the same time and place, while committing the said robbery, you **used deadly weapons** … punishable under **section 397 PPC**. Thirdly (if a shot was fired): that you, with the intention of causing death, **fired** at Adnan, which hit him on the left arm, causing **ghayr-jaifah mudihah**, and thereby committed offences punishable under **sections 324 and 337F(iv) read with section 34 PPC**.

And I hereby direct that you be **tried** by this Court on the said charge.

(Signed and dated) — **Read over and explained** to the accused, who **pleaded not guilty** and **claimed trial**.

# Chapter 10 · Trials

![The course of a Sessions trial](trial_stages.png)

## 10.1 Summary trials — ss. 260 to 265

1. **Who** — a **Magistrate of the first class specially empowered**, or a Bench so empowered (s. 260).
2. **What** — offences **not punishable with death, transportation or imprisonment exceeding six months**; weights and measures (ss. 264–266 PPC); hurt; theft, receiving or misappropriation where the value does not exceed a stated amount; house-trespass to commit such theft; insult with intent to provoke breach of peace; criminal intimidation; abetment and attempt of these.
3. **Procedure** — that of **summons** trials (Chapter XX); **no sentence exceeding three months** (s. 262(2)).
4. **Record** — where **no appeal** lies, no need to record evidence or frame a formal charge — only the particulars of s. 263 (serial number, dates, complainant, accused, offence, plea, finding, sentence); where an appeal lies, the **substance of the evidence** and a **judgment** (s. 264).

## 10.2 Trials before Magistrates — ss. 241 to 250

1. **s. 241A** — **copies** of s. 161 and s. 164 statements and the inspection note supplied **free** to the accused **at least seven days** before trial (police cases, other than summary or six-month cases); in complaint cases, the complainant supplies copies.
2. **s. 242** — the **charge** is framed and read over; the accused is asked whether he pleads guilty.
3. **s. 243** — on a **plea of guilty**, the Magistrate may convict.
4. **s. 244** — otherwise, **prosecution evidence**, then the **defence**.
5. **s. 245** — **acquittal** or **conviction** and sentence (s. 245A — proof of previous conviction after conviction).
6. **s. 247** — **non-appearance of the complainant** in a complaint case — the Magistrate **shall acquit** unless he adjourns — not where the offence is **cognizable or non-compoundable**.
7. **s. 248** — **withdrawal of the complaint** with the Magistrate's permission — **acquittal**.
8. **s. 249** — stopping proceedings in cases not on complaint — release with the effect of **discharge**.
9. **s. 249A** — **acquittal at any stage** if, after hearing the prosecutor and the accused and for reasons recorded, the **charge is groundless** or there is **no probability of conviction**.
10. **s. 250** — **compensation** for **false, frivolous or vexatious** accusation, payable by the complainant or informant.

|s. 245(1)|On the evidence, the accused is **not guilty**|
|s. 247|**Complainant absent** on the date of hearing (non-cognizable, compoundable offences)|
|s. 248|**Withdrawal** of the complaint with permission|
|s. 249A|**Groundless charge** or **no probability** of conviction, at any stage|

## 10.3 Trials before High Courts and Courts of Session — ss. 265A to 265N

1. **s. 265A** — prosecution conducted by the **Public Prosecutor**.
2. **s. 265C** — **copies** to the accused **at least seven days** before trial: (a) the **FIR**, (b) the **police report**, (c) statements under **ss. 161 and 164**, (d) the **inspection note** and **recovery notes**; in complaint cases, the complaint, documents and statements under ss. 200, 202.
3. **s. 265D** — if there is ground for proceeding, the court **frames a charge** in writing.
4. **s. 265E** — the charge is read and explained; the accused is asked to **plead**; a plea of **guilty** is recorded and the court **may** convict (it is not bound to, especially in capital cases).
5. **s. 265F** — **prosecution evidence**; refusal to summon a witness called for vexation (reasons recorded); the accused's **defence evidence**; written statement filed.
6. **s. 265G** — **summing up** — the prosecutor and then the defence address the court.
7. **s. 265H** — **acquittal** or **conviction**; **s. 265I** — previous conviction proved after conviction.
8. **s. 265K** — the court may **acquit at any stage** if there is **no probability** of conviction, after hearing both sides and recording reasons.
9. **s. 265L** — the **Public Prosecutor may withdraw** a case under s. 494.

## 10.4 Examination of the accused — s. 342 (LHC 2023)

1. **Purpose** — to enable the accused to **explain any circumstance** appearing in the evidence against him (*audi alteram partem*).
2. **When** — the court **may** question him at **any stage** without warning, and **shall** question him **generally** on the case **after the prosecution witnesses** have been examined and **before he is called on for his defence**.
3. **Every incriminating piece of evidence** must be put to him; a circumstance **not put** cannot be used against him.
4. **No oath** (s. 342(4)), **no punishment** for refusing or for false answers, but the court may draw **inferences** (s. 342(2)); the answers may be used for or against him in the trial.
5. **Statement on oath — s. 340(2):** the accused **may** (if he does not plead guilty) give evidence **on oath** in disproof of the charge; he is then a witness, subject to cross-examination, but protected from questions about **other offences or bad character** except in three situations.

## 10.5 Recording evidence in the absence of the accused — s. 512 (LHC 2023)

1. **Absconding accused (s. 512(1))** — if it is proved that the accused **has absconded** and there is **no immediate prospect of arresting him**, the court competent to try (or send for trial) may **examine the prosecution witnesses** in his absence and record their depositions. On his arrest, the depositions may be **given in evidence** against him **only if** the deponent is **dead**, **incapable** of giving evidence, or his attendance cannot be procured without **unreasonable delay, expense or inconvenience**.
2. **Offender unknown (s. 512(2))** — where an offence punishable with **death or life imprisonment** has been committed by persons **unknown**, the **High Court** may direct a first-class Magistrate to hold an **inquiry** and examine witnesses; the depositions may be used against a person later accused if the deponent is dead, incapable or **beyond Pakistan**.
3. **s. 540A** — where two or more accused are before the court, one **incapable of remaining** may be excused if represented by a pleader.

## 10.6 Death of an accused during trial (LHC 2023)

1. The Code contains **no express provision** for a pending **trial**; but criminal liability is **personal** — on the accused's death, the proceedings **abate** as against him and the case is **consigned** (filed) to the record, with no finding of guilt or innocence.
2. The trial **continues** against **co-accused**.
3. **Appeals** — every appeal (other than against a sentence of **fine**) **abates** on the death of the **appellant**; an appeal against **acquittal** abates on the death of the **accused** (s. 431). An appeal against **fine** survives because the fine is recoverable from his **estate** (PPC s. 70). **Diyat, arsh and daman** are likewise recoverable from the estate (PPC ss. 331(3), 337X(3)).
4. **Property** produced in the case is dealt with under **s. 517**.

## 10.7 Other general provisions

1. **s. 337** — **tender of pardon** to an **accomplice** (offences triable by the Sessions Court, or punishable with up to 10 years, and certain others) on condition of **full and true disclosure**; s. 339 — prosecution if he breaks the condition.
2. **s. 344A** — offences under **ss. 354A, 376, 377 and 377B** PPC must be decided **within three months** of cognizance, failing which the matter is brought to the notice of the **Chief Justice** of the High Court.
3. **s. 345 — compounding:** (1) offences in the first table compoundable **without permission** by the persons named (e.g., hurt-type minor offences, defamation, criminal trespass); (2) offences in the second table compoundable **with the permission of the court** — including **all Qisas and Diyat offences** (by heirs or victim, subject to **s. 311 PPC**); abetment and attempt (s. 345(3)); a guardian for a minor or lunatic (s. 345(4)); while an appeal is pending, only with leave of the appellate court (s. 345(5)); **composition = acquittal** (s. 345(6)); **only as provided** (s. 345(7)).
4. **s. 349** — a Magistrate who finds the accused deserving of greater punishment than he can award submits the case to a **Chief Judicial / s. 30 Magistrate**.
5. **s. 353** — evidence shall be taken **in the presence of the accused** (or his pleader where attendance is dispensed with).
6. **s. 364** — record of the **examination of the accused** in full, in the language used, read over, and signed.
7. **s. 403 — double jeopardy:** a person once **convicted or acquitted** by a competent court shall not be **tried again for the same offence**, nor on the same facts for any other offence for which a different charge might have been made (s. 236) or a conviction recorded (s. 237) — also **Art. 13** of the Constitution.
8. **s. 540** — the court **may** summon any person as a witness at any stage, and **shall** if his evidence is **essential to the just decision** of the case.
9. **s. 544A** — **compensation** to the heirs of a person killed, or to a person hurt, or for **mental anguish**, or for property loss — **ordered on conviction** unless reasons are recorded; recoverable as **arrears of land revenue**; default imprisonment up to six months.

## 10.8 Identification parade (SHC 2022)

A **test identification parade** is an aid to investigation, conducted by a **Magistrate** (usually in jail) where the witness did not know the accused before the occurrence. Safeguards: the accused is kept **muffled** after arrest; **dummies** of similar appearance (at least nine for one accused); the witnesses are kept apart; the Magistrate records **objections**, and the **role** each witness ascribes to the accused; held **promptly**. It is **not substantive evidence** — the substantive evidence is the identification **in court**; the parade **corroborates** it (Art. 22 QSO — facts establishing identity are relevant). A parade with defects, or identification without **specifying the role**, has little value.

## 10.9 Judgment — ss. 366 to 373

1. **s. 366** — pronounced in **open court**, in the court's language or one the accused understands; the accused attends (except where only fined or acquitted and attendance was dispensed with).
2. **s. 367** — written by the presiding officer, containing the **points for determination**, the **decision** and the **reasons**; dated and signed in open court; it must specify the **offence**, the **section** and the **punishment**; in a doubtful case, judgment in the **alternative**; an acquittal directs that the accused be **set at liberty**; where the offence is punishable with **death** and a lesser sentence is passed, the **reasons** why death was not awarded.
3. **ss. 374–376** — a **death sentence** is submitted to the **High Court** for **confirmation**; the High Court may confirm, pass another sentence, convict of another offence, order a new trial, or acquit.
4. **s. 382** — a woman sentenced to death found **pregnant** — the High Court **shall** postpone execution and **may** commute the sentence to imprisonment for life.
5. **s. 382A** — sentences under one year (or s. 476) **not executed** till the appeal period expires if bail is furnished.
6. **s. 382B** — the court **shall** consider the period of **pre-sentence custody** when awarding imprisonment.
7. **s. 382C** — the court may take into account **scandalous or false and frivolous pleas** in sentencing.

# Chapter 11 · Bail (ss. 496 to 498A) — KPHC 2026; ASJ 2025

## 11.1 Meaning and philosophy

**Bail** is the release of an accused from custody on **security** (a bond with or without sureties) that he will **appear** when required. It reconciles the presumption of **innocence** and the right to **liberty** (Art. 9) with the State's interest in securing the accused's attendance and protecting the investigation. Bail is **not** acquittal; the grant of bail is a **tentative** assessment, and the trial court is not bound by it.

![Forms of bail](bail_forms.png)

## 11.2 Bailable offences — s. 496

A person accused of a **bailable** offence, arrested without warrant or appearing before a court, **shall be released on bail** if prepared to give it — bail is his **right**; the officer or court may instead release him on a **personal bond** without sureties.

## 11.3 Non-bailable offences — s. 497

1. **Discretion** — such a person "**may** be released on bail", **but shall not** be released "if there appear **reasonable grounds for believing** that he has been guilty of an offence punishable with **death or imprisonment for life or imprisonment for ten years**" — the **prohibitory clause**.
2. **First proviso — concession** — the court **may** release on bail a person **under sixteen**, **any woman**, or any **sick or infirm** person, even in a prohibitory-clause offence.
3. **Second proviso — statutory bail for delay** — the court **shall** release on bail (unless the delay was caused by the accused or his agents): (a) an accused of an offence **not punishable with death** detained continuously for more than **one year** (a **woman** — **six months**) whose trial has not concluded; (b) an accused of an offence **punishable with death** detained for more than **two years** (a **woman** — **one year**).
4. **Third proviso — exclusions from statutory bail:** a **previously convicted** offender for an offence punishable with death or life, a **hardened, desperate or dangerous** criminal, or a person accused of **terrorism** punishable with death or life.
5. **s. 497(2) — further inquiry:** if at any stage there are **not reasonable grounds** to believe the accused committed a non-bailable offence, but there are **sufficient grounds for further inquiry** into his guilt, he **shall** be released on bail — bail then becomes a **right**.
6. **s. 497(3)** — **reasons** must be recorded when releasing.
7. **s. 497(4)** — after the trial concludes and before judgment, if there are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is **not guilty**, he shall be released on his own bond.
8. **s. 497(5)** — the **High Court or Court of Session** (and the releasing court for its own order) may **cancel** bail and commit the accused to custody.

## 11.4 "Reasonable grounds" and "further inquiry" (ASJ 2025)

1. **Reasonable grounds** — more than mere **allegations** or suspicion; the court looks at the **material collected** (FIR, statements, medical evidence, recoveries, the accused's specific role) **tentatively**, without a deep appraisal of evidence, to see whether there is **prima facie** material connecting him with an offence in the prohibitory clause.
2. **Further inquiry** — where the material leaves **reasonable doubt** about guilt: e.g., **two versions** of the occurrence; a **general or ineffective role** (e.g., *lalkara*, no injury attributed); the applicability of a **lesser section** (whether s. 302 or s. 316 or s. 324 is attracted); **delay** in the FIR unexplained; contradictions with the medical evidence; **co-accused with a similar role** already bailed (**rule of consistency**); a case resting on **documents** already in police possession; **civil nature** of the dispute (cheques, contracts); the **benefit of doubt** principle applies even at the bail stage.
3. **Offences outside the prohibitory clause** — "**grant of bail is a rule and refusal an exception**" (*Tariq Bashir v. The State*, PLD 1995 SC 34); refusal only for **extraordinary** reasons — likelihood of **absconding**, **tampering** with prosecution evidence, **repeating** the offence, or being a previous **convict**.
4. **Rule of consistency (SHC 2022)** — an accused whose role is **identical** to that of a co-accused already granted bail is ordinarily entitled to bail on the same footing; a **distinguishable** role defeats the rule.

## 11.5 Concurrent powers and pre-arrest bail — ss. 498, 498A

1. **s. 498** — the **High Court or Court of Session** may, in any case, whether there is an appeal or not, direct that any person be **admitted to bail**, or that the bail fixed by a police officer or Magistrate be **reduced**. **Pre-arrest bail** (bail before arrest, "anticipatory bail") is derived from this section.
2. **s. 498A** — no bail to a person **not in custody, not present in court**, or against whom **no case is registered**; and bail is effective only for the case **registered and specified** in the order. So a pre-arrest bail petitioner must **appear in person**, and a "blanket" bail for future cases cannot be granted.

### Principles of pre-arrest bail

1. It is an **extraordinary relief**, meant to protect **innocent** persons against **harassment and humiliation** by arrest in a case launched with **mala fide** or **ulterior motives** (*Murad Khan v. Fazal-e-Subhan*, PLD 1983 SC 82; *Meeran Bux v. The State*, PLD 1989 SC 347).
2. The petitioner must show (i) **mala fide** of the complainant or police, or that arrest would cause **irreparable humiliation**; and (ii) a **prima facie** case for bail on **merits** (*Rana Muhammad Arshad v. Muhammad Rafique*, PLD 2009 SC 427).
3. Ordinarily the petitioner must **first approach the Court of Session** before the High Court (hierarchy).
4. Pre-arrest bail is **not** a device to avoid **recovery** or interrogation where recovery is to be effected from the accused.
5. **Interim** pre-arrest bail is granted until the final hearing; if the petition is dismissed, the accused is arrested **in court**; if confirmed, he remains on bail.
6. **Proclaimed offenders / absconders** ordinarily cannot claim it (Chapter 4.6).
7. **Protective (transit) bail** — a High Court may grant short protective bail to enable a person to appear before the court of competent jurisdiction in another province or territory.

## 11.6 Bail pending appeal — s. 426

The appellate court may **suspend the sentence** and release the appellant on bail for reasons recorded (s. 426(1)). **Statutory release** (s. 426(1A)) unless the appellant caused the delay: sentence **up to 3 years** — appeal not decided within **six months**; **over 3 and up to 7 years** — within **one year**; **life or over 7 years** — within **two years**; with the same exclusions as s. 497. A court convicting for a **bailable** offence may grant bail to enable an appeal (s. 426(2A)). Time on bail is **excluded** from the sentence (s. 426(3)).

## 11.7 Bonds and sureties — ss. 499 to 502

1. **s. 499** — a **bond** by the accused and, for bail, by one or more **sufficient sureties**, conditioned on attendance.
2. **s. 500** — **release** as soon as the bond is executed (release order to the jail).
3. **s. 501** — if the sureties prove **insufficient**, the court may issue a warrant and require sufficient sureties.
4. **s. 502** — a **surety may apply** to be **discharged**; the court issues a warrant, and on the accused's appearance discharges the bond and calls for other sureties.

## 11.8 Termination of bail (KPHC 2026)

Bail comes to an end:

1. by **cancellation** — s. 497(5) (by the High Court, Sessions Court or the granting court) or under s. 498 — on grounds of **misuse**: **tampering** with evidence, **threatening** witnesses, **absconding**, committing a **fresh offence**, violating conditions, or where the order was **perverse** or passed without jurisdiction (the grounds for cancellation are narrower than for refusal);
2. by **discharge of the bond** at the surety's request (s. 502), or the **death or insolvency** of a surety (s. 514A — fresh security);
3. on **forfeiture** of the bond for non-appearance (s. 514);
4. by a **conviction** and sentence of imprisonment (bail ends unless the sentence is suspended under s. 426);
5. by an **acquittal or discharge**, or the **cancellation** of the case;
6. by the **expiry** of interim bail or the **dismissal** of a pre-arrest bail petition;
7. on the **death** of the accused.

## 11.9 Deposit, forfeiture and appeal — ss. 513 to 516 (LHC 2020)

1. **s. 513 — deposit instead of a bond:** when any person is required to execute a bond, with or without sureties, the court or officer may — **except in the case of a bond for good behaviour** — permit him to **deposit a sum of money or Government promissory notes** **or furnish a bank guarantee** (Act XXXIII of 2022) in lieu of executing the bond.
2. **s. 514 — forfeiture:** when it is **proved** to the satisfaction of the court (or a first-class Magistrate) that the bond has been **forfeited**, the court **records the grounds**, and **calls upon** the person bound to **pay the penalty or show cause**; if sufficient cause is not shown and the penalty is not paid, it may be recovered by **attachment and sale** of his **movable** property (**and, in the ICT, immovable** property — Act XXXVI of 2026) or his estate; if not recoverable — **civil jail** up to **six months**; the court may **remit part** of the penalty (**in the ICT not more than half** of the surety amount); a **surety's estate** is discharged if he **dies** before forfeiture.
3. **s. 514A** — fresh security on the **insolvency or death** of a surety or on forfeiture; **s. 514B** — a **minor** may give a bond through sureties only.
4. **s. 515 — appeal and revision:** all orders passed by any **Magistrate** under s. 514 are **appealable to the Sessions Judge**, or, if no appeal is preferred, may be **revised** by the Sessions Judge (and the High Court under ss. 435, 439).
5. **s. 516** — the High Court or Court of Session may direct a Magistrate to **levy** the amount due on a bond for appearance before it.

## 11.10 Bail in a s. 489F case — model order outline (SHC 2022)

:::eg Order (outline)
"The applicant seeks post-arrest bail in case FIR No. … under s. 489F PPC. The prosecution alleges that he issued a cheque for the price of goods which was dishonoured on presentation. Learned counsel for the applicant contends that the goods supplied were **defective** and that the applicant **stopped payment** for that reason. Heard. Record perused. 1. The offence carries a **maximum of three years** and does **not fall within the prohibitory clause** of s. 497(1) Cr.P.C.; in such cases **grant of bail is the rule and refusal an exception** (*Tariq Bashir v. The State*, PLD 1995 SC 34). 2. Whether the cheque was issued **dishonestly** and towards **an obligation** which subsisted after the goods were found defective is a question requiring **further inquiry** within s. 497(2). 3. The **documentary** evidence is in the possession of the prosecution and there is no likelihood of tampering; the applicant is **not a previous convict** and is **no longer required** for investigation. 4. Keeping him in jail would serve no useful purpose; the civil dispute is pending. Bail is **granted** subject to his furnishing a bail bond of Rs. … with one surety in the like amount to the satisfaction of the trial court. The observations are **tentative** and shall not prejudice the trial."
:::

# Chapter 12 · Appeals, revision and inherent powers

## 12.1 Appeals — the scheme

1. **No appeal except as provided** (s. 404).
2. From a **Magistrate** (s. 30 or otherwise) or an **Assistant Sessions Judge** — to the **Court of Session**; but from an Assistant Sessions Judge's sentence **exceeding four years** — to the **High Court** (s. 408).
3. From a **Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge** — to the **High Court** (s. 410).
4. From the **High Court's original** criminal jurisdiction — to a **Division Bench** (s. 411A).
5. **Against acquittal — s. 417:** (1) the **Government** may direct the Public Prosecutor to appeal to the **High Court** against any acquittal by a court other than the High Court; (2) in a **complaint** case, the **complainant** may appeal with the High Court's **special leave**; (2A) **any person aggrieved** by an acquittal by a court other than the High Court may appeal **within thirty days**.

## 12.2 Cases in which no appeal lies — ss. 412 to 415 (LHC 2020)

{w:16,84}
|s. 412|**Plea of guilty** — where the accused pleaded guilty and was convicted on that plea by a High Court, Court of Session or first-class Magistrate, there is **no appeal except as to the extent or legality of the sentence**|
|s. 413|**Petty cases** — no appeal where (a) the **High Court** passes imprisonment **not exceeding six months** only or fine **not exceeding Rs. 200** only; (b) a **Court of Session** passes imprisonment **not exceeding one month** only; (c) a Court of Session or **first-class Magistrate** passes a fine **not exceeding Rs. 50** only. *Explanation:* imprisonment in **default** of fine alone is not appealable|
|s. 414|**Summary convictions** — no appeal where a Magistrate empowered under s. 260 passes a sentence of **fine not exceeding Rs. 2,000** only|
|s. 415|**Proviso to ss. 413 and 414** — an appeal **lies** against such a sentence where it is **combined with any other punishment**; but a sentence is **not** appealable merely because the convict is also ordered to give **security to keep the peace**; and imprisonment in default of fine is **not** a combination of punishments|

## 12.3 Procedure in appeal — ss. 419 to 431

1. **Petition of appeal** with a copy of the judgment (s. 419); summary dismissal only after hearing (s. 421).
2. **Powers (s. 423)** — in an appeal from **acquittal**: reverse and order retrial or convict; from **conviction**: reverse and acquit or order retrial, alter the finding, **reduce** the sentence — but **not enhance** it (enhancement only in revision after notice); any amendment or incidental order.
3. **Abatement** (s. 431) — Chapter 10.6.

## 12.4 Revision — ss. 435 to 439A

1. **s. 435** — the **High Court or Sessions Judge** may **call for the record** of any proceeding of an **inferior criminal court** to satisfy itself as to the **correctness, legality or propriety** of any finding, sentence or order and the **regularity** of proceedings, and may **suspend** the sentence or release the accused on bail meanwhile.
2. **s. 439 — High Court's powers:** any power of an appellate court (ss. 423, 426–428) and **enhancement** of sentence after hearing the accused (s. 439(2), (6)); but it **cannot convert an acquittal into a conviction** (s. 439(4)(a)); no revision at the instance of a party who **could have appealed** but did not (s. 439(5)).
3. **s. 439A — Sessions Judge's powers** — the same powers in respect of Magistrates' proceedings; an Additional Sessions Judge exercises them in transferred cases; **no revision to the High Court** against a Sessions Judge's s. 439A order (s. 439(4)(b)) — the remedy is **s. 561A** or Art. 199.

## 12.5 Inherent powers — s. 561A

"Nothing in this Code shall be deemed to limit or affect the **inherent power of the High Court** to make such orders as may be necessary to give effect to any order under this Code, or to **prevent abuse of the process** of any Court, or otherwise to **secure the ends of justice**." It is used to **quash** proceedings that are groundless or an abuse, but it is **not** an alternative to appeal or revision and is exercised **sparingly**; the **Sessions Court has no inherent powers**.

## 12.6 Transfer — ss. 526, 528

The **High Court** may transfer any case from one criminal court to another (s. 526) — where a **fair and impartial trial** cannot be had, for general convenience, or to meet the ends of justice; a **Sessions Judge** may transfer within his division (s. 528). A reasonable **apprehension** of bias is enough — not proof of actual bias.

# Chapter 14 · Juvenile Justice System Act 2018 (in brief)

1. **Definitions (s. 2)** — **child**: under **18**; **juvenile**: a child who may be dealt with for an offence differently from an adult; **minor offence**: maximum imprisonment **up to three years**; **major offence**: **more than three and up to seven years**; **heinous offence**: serious, gruesome, brutal, sensational or shocking, punishable with **death, life or more than seven years**; **guardian**: parent, court-appointed guardian or person with actual care; **best interest of the child**: the basis of decisions to secure his basic rights, identity, social well-being and development; **Juvenile Rehabilitation Centre**, **observation home**, **suitable person**, **Juvenile Justice Committee** (s. 10).
2. **Legal aid** at State expense; informed of rights by a lawyer within **24 hours** (s. 3).
3. **Juvenile Court** — exclusive jurisdiction; decide within **six months** (s. 4).
4. **Arrest (s. 5)** — kept in an **observation home**; guardian and probation officer informed; no preventive detention.
5. **Bail (s. 6)** — in **bailable, minor and major** offences — treated as **bailable**; never kept in a police station or jail; over **16** in a **heinous** offence — may be refused if reasonable grounds; **statutory bail** after **six months**.
6. **Investigation (s. 7)** — by an officer **not below Sub-Inspector** under SP/SDPO supervision, assisted by a **probation officer** (social investigation report).
7. **Age determination (s. 8)** — by the police on the basis of **birth certificate, educational certificates** or other documents; failing them, a **medical examination**; the Magistrate, before remand, **records a finding on age**.
8. **Diversion (s. 9)** — with the consent of the juvenile or guardian and of the **victim**, a case may be disposed of **without formal trial** by the **Juvenile Justice Committee** (s. 10 — a s. 30 Judicial Magistrate as head, the district Public Prosecutor, a lawyer of 7 years' standing and a probation or social welfare officer) — by **restitution, reparation, apology, community service, fine and costs, placement in a rehabilitation centre, or reprimand**; available for **minor** offences, and for **major** offences where the juvenile is **not more than 16**; **not** for **heinous** offences; all minor and major offences are **compoundable** for this purpose.
9. **Joint trial (s. 12)** — a juvenile is **not** tried with an adult **unless** the Juvenile Court finds it in the **interests of justice**; presence may be dispensed with and joined by video link.
10. **Probation officer's report (s. 14)**, **orders of release (s. 15)** — release on pardon by the victim, community service, fine, compensation, counselling, **probation** under a guardian or suitable person, or a **rehabilitation centre** till 18.
11. **Orders that shall not be passed (s. 16)** — **no death sentence**; no prison, **labour, fetters, handcuffs** or **corporal punishment** (exception: handcuffs or 24-hour solitary confinement for a juvenile over 16 in a heinous offence with risk of escape).
12. **Identity** not to be disclosed (s. 13); **appeal** under the CrPC, by the guardian; appeal against acquittal in **30 days** (s. 18); **no disqualification** attaches to the conviction (s. 19).
