
# Chapter 1 · The scheme of the Code and key definitions

## 1.1 Nature of the Code

The CrPC is a **procedural** law: it lays down the **machinery** for investigating offences, bringing offenders to court, trying them, and executing sentences. All PPC offences are dealt with under it (s. 5(1)); offences under other laws too, **subject to** any special procedure in those laws (s. 5(2)) — e.g., the Anti-Terrorism Act 1997, the CNSA 1997, the Juvenile Justice System Act 2018. Procedure is the **handmaid of justice**: an irregularity does not vitiate proceedings unless it has caused a **failure of justice** (s. 537).

## 1.2 The core definitions — s. 4

{w:24,76}
|Term|Meaning|
|**Bailable / non-bailable offence** (s. 4(1)(b))|An offence shown as **bailable in the Second Schedule** or made bailable by another law; every other offence is non-bailable|
|**Cognizable offence / case** (s. 4(1)(f))|An offence for which, and a **case** in which, a police officer **may arrest without warrant** under the Second Schedule or any other law|
|**Non-cognizable offence / case** (s. 4(1)(n))|One in which the police **may not arrest without warrant**|
|**Complaint** (s. 4(1)(h))|An **allegation**, oral or written, made **to a Magistrate** with a view to his taking action, that some person (known or unknown) has committed an offence — but **not a police report**|
|**Inquiry** (s. 4(1)(k))|Every inquiry **other than a trial** conducted by a Magistrate or Court (e.g., ss. 107, 145, 176, 202, 476)|
|**Investigation** (s. 4(1)(l))|All proceedings for the **collection of evidence** by a **police officer** or a person (other than a Magistrate) authorised by a Magistrate|
|**Offence** (s. 4(1)(o))|Any act or omission made punishable by any law|
|**Officer in charge of a police station** (s. 4(1)(p))|Includes the next senior officer above constable present when the SHO is absent|
|**Police report**|The report forwarded under **s. 173** (challan)|

## 1.3 Classic distinctions (LHC 2023)

{w:24,38,38}
|Pair|First|Second|
|**Cognizable offence / cognizable case**|The **offence** itself, as classified in Schedule II (e.g., theft, s. 379)|The **case** as a whole — a case is cognizable if the police may arrest without warrant in it; a case containing **both** cognizable and non-cognizable offences is treated as **cognizable** for investigation (s. 155(4) principle)|
|**Bailable / non-bailable offence**|Bail is a **right** — the officer or court **shall** release (s. 496)|Bail is a **discretion** — "may" release, but not where reasonable grounds exist of an offence in the prohibitory clause (s. 497)|
|**Complaint case / State case**|Begun by a **complaint** to a Magistrate (s. 200); complainant leads evidence; may be **withdrawn** (s. 248) or dismissed for non-appearance (s. 247)|Begun on a **police report** (s. 173) after an FIR; the **State** prosecutes through the Public Prosecutor; withdrawal only under s. 494|
|**Inquiry / investigation / trial**|Inquiry — by a Magistrate or court, short of a trial|Investigation — by police, to collect evidence; **trial** — the judicial determination of guilt, beginning with the **charge**|
|**Discharge / acquittal**|Release **before** the charge (e.g., s. 494(a)); no bar to a fresh case|Finding of **not guilty** after the charge; bars a second trial (s. 403)|

# Chapter 2 · Criminal courts and their powers

## 2.1 Classes of criminal courts — s. 6 (LHC 2023)

Besides the **High Court** and courts under special laws (Anti-Terrorism Courts, Special Courts, Juvenile Courts, Banking Courts), there are **two classes** of criminal courts:

1. **Courts of Session** — the **Sessions Judge**, **Additional Sessions Judges** and **Assistant Sessions Judges** (s. 9);
2. **Courts of Magistrates** — Magistrates of the **first, second and third class** (s. 6(2)), with **s. 30 Magistrates** (first-class Magistrates specially empowered to try all offences **not punishable with death**).

## 2.2 Sentences which courts may pass — ss. 31 to 35 (LHC 2023)

{w:30,70}
|Court|Sentence it may pass|
|**High Court** (s. 31(1))|Any sentence authorised by law|
|**Sessions Judge / Additional Sessions Judge** (s. 31(2))|Any sentence authorised by law — **death** subject to **confirmation by the High Court** (s. 374)|
|**Assistant Sessions Judge** (s. 31(3))|Any sentence except **death** or imprisonment **exceeding seven years**|
|**Section 30 Magistrate** (s. 34)|Any sentence except **death** or imprisonment **exceeding seven years**|
|**Magistrate of the first class** (s. 32(1)(a))|Imprisonment up to **three years** (including solitary confinement allowed by law); fine up to **Rs. 45,000**; **arsh; daman**; whipping|
|**Magistrate of the second class** (s. 32(1)(b))|Imprisonment up to **one year**; fine up to **Rs. 15,000**|
|**Magistrate of the third class** (s. 32(1)(c))|Imprisonment up to **one month**; fine up to **Rs. 3,000**|

**Default imprisonment** (s. 33) — within the Magistrate's powers, and where substantive imprisonment is awarded, not more than **one-fourth** of his maximum; it may be **in addition** to the maximum substantive sentence. **Several offences at one trial** (s. 35) — consecutive unless ordered concurrent; total not more than **14 years**, and for a Magistrate not more than **twice** his ordinary powers.

## 2.3 Justices of the Peace — ss. 22A, 22B, 25

1. **Powers (s. 22A):** to **arrest** as a police officer (ss. 54, 55) and hand the person to the SHO, who **re-arrests**; to call on police on duty to aid in arresting offenders and preventing crime; to issue **identity certificates**, **verify** and **attest** documents.
2. **Ex-officio Justices of the Peace (s. 25):** the **Sessions Judges** and, on nomination by them, the **Additional Sessions Judges**, for the whole district.
3. **Directions to the police — s. 22A(6):** an ex-officio Justice of the Peace may issue **appropriate directions** to the police on a complaint regarding (i) **non-registration of a criminal case**; (ii) **transfer of investigation** from one police officer to another; and (iii) **neglect, failure or excess** committed by a police authority in its functions and duties.
4. **Duties (s. 22B):** inquire into and report breaches of the peace and offences to the nearest Magistrate and SHO; preserve the **scene** of a cognizable offence; assist an investigating officer on written request, and record a **dying declaration**.

:::case Khizar Hayat v. Inspector-General of Police (PLD 2005 Lahore 470) and Younas Abbas v. Additional Sessions Judge (PLD 2016 SC 581)
The functions of the ex-officio Justice of the Peace under s. 22A(6) are **quasi-judicial**, not executive or ministerial — so they are **not** an investigating or supervisory agency. *Younas Abbas* (larger bench) confirmed that these functions are **quasi-judicial**, and the Justice of the Peace must hear both sides and pass a **reasoned** order. On a petition for registration of an FIR, he examines whether the information discloses a **cognizable offence**; if so, the SHO is **bound** to register it (s. 154) — the police cannot hold a "preliminary inquiry" first.
:::

# Chapter 3 · Arrest (ss. 46 to 67)

## 3.1 How an arrest is made — ss. 46 to 53

1. **s. 46** — by **touching or confining** the body, unless the person **submits** by word or action; if he resists or tries to evade, **all means necessary** may be used — but this does **not** give a right to cause the **death** of a person not accused of an offence punishable with **death or imprisonment for life**.
2. **s. 47** — the police may **search a place** entered by the person to be arrested; if the place is an apartment in the actual occupancy of a **woman** (not the person to be arrested) who by custom does not appear in public, she must be given **notice** and every facility to **withdraw** before entry.
3. **s. 48** — break open outer or inner doors or windows if refused admittance.
4. **ss. 49, 50** — **no more restraint** than necessary to prevent escape.
5. **s. 51** — search of the arrested person; seized articles listed.
6. **s. 52** — "Whenever it is necessary to cause a **woman** to be searched, the search shall be made by **another woman**, with strict regard to **decency**."
7. **s. 53** — seizure of offensive weapons.

## 3.2 Arrest without warrant by the police — s. 54

A police officer may arrest without warrant (nine heads): (1) a person **concerned** in a cognizable offence, or against whom a **reasonable complaint**, **credible information** or **reasonable suspicion** exists; (2) one possessing a **house-breaking implement** without lawful excuse; (3) a **proclaimed offender**; (4) one in possession of property reasonably suspected **stolen**; (5) one who **obstructs** a police officer or **escapes** from lawful custody; (6) a suspected **deserter** from the armed forces; (7) one concerned in an act **abroad** which would be an offence here (extradition); (8) a released convict breaking a rule under s. 565(3); (9) on **requisition** from another police officer. Also **s. 55** (vagabonds, habitual robbers), **s. 56** (written order to a subordinate), **s. 57** (refusal to give name and residence in a non-cognizable case).

## 3.3 Arrest by a private person — s. 59 (LHC 2023)

1. **Who may be arrested:** any person who **in his view** commits a **non-bailable and cognizable** offence, or any **proclaimed offender**.
2. **Procedure:** without **unnecessary delay**, make him over to a **police officer**, or, in the absence of one, take him to the nearest **police station**.
3. If he falls under s. 54, the police **re-arrest** him; if he has committed a **non-cognizable** offence and refuses his name and residence, he is dealt with under s. 57; if there is no reason to believe he committed any offence, he is **released at once**.

## 3.4 Production within 24 hours — ss. 60, 61 (LHC 2023)

1. **s. 60** — a police officer arresting without warrant shall, **without unnecessary delay**, take the person before a **Magistrate** having jurisdiction or the officer in charge of a police station.
2. **s. 61** — no police officer shall detain a person arrested without warrant for longer than is **reasonable**, and the period shall **not exceed twenty-four hours**, **exclusive of the time necessary for the journey** from the place of arrest to the Magistrate's court, in the absence of a **special order of a Magistrate under s. 167**.
3. **Constitutional guarantee — Art. 10:** an arrested person must be **informed of the grounds**, has the right to **consult and be defended** by a legal practitioner of his choice, and must be **produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours**.

**Consequences of failure:** the detention becomes **illegal**; the arrested person may seek release through a **habeas corpus** petition (Art. 199; CrPC s. 491) or a **search warrant** under s. 100 to recover him from illegal confinement; the officers are liable for **wrongful confinement** (PPC ss. 342–348) and departmental action (Police Order 2002); evidence obtained by torture is excluded (Art. 14(2) — no torture for extracting evidence; QSO Art. 38–39 for confessions to police).

## 3.5 Other rules

1. **s. 62** — the SHO reports arrests without warrant to the district police and authorities.
2. **s. 63** — an arrested person is discharged only on his **bond, bail** or a **Magistrate's special order**.
3. **ss. 64, 65** — a Magistrate may arrest, or order the arrest of, an offender committing an offence in his presence.
4. **s. 66** — a person escaping may be pursued and re-arrested anywhere in Pakistan.

# Chapter 4 · Processes to compel appearance (ss. 68 to 93) — KPHC 2026; LHC 2023

![Processes to compel appearance — the escalation](processes.png)

## 4.1 Summons — ss. 68 to 74

1. **Form** (s. 68) — in **writing**, in duplicate, **signed** by the presiding officer and bearing the court's **seal**; served by a police officer or court officer.
2. **Personal service** (s. 69) — by delivering or tendering **one duplicate**; the person served signs a receipt on the other.
3. **Service on an absent person** (s. 70) — by leaving a duplicate with some **adult male member of his family**.
4. **Substituted service** (s. 71) — if service cannot be effected under ss. 69–70, by **affixing** a duplicate on a conspicuous part of the house where he ordinarily resides.
5. **Service on a Government servant** (s. 72) — through the **head of his office**; outside jurisdiction (s. 73); proof by affidavit (s. 74).

## 4.2 Warrant of arrest — ss. 75 to 86

1. **Form** (s. 75) — in **writing**, signed, sealed; **remains in force until cancelled** or executed.
2. **Bailable warrant** (s. 76) — the court may **endorse** the warrant directing that, if the person executes a **bond with sureties**, he shall be **released** (stating the number of sureties, the amount, and the time to attend).
3. **To whom directed** (ss. 77–79) — ordinarily to a **police officer**; may be directed to any person (s. 78).
4. **Execution** (ss. 80–82) — the officer shall **notify the substance** and, if required, **show the warrant**; bring the person **before the court without delay** (s. 81); may be executed **anywhere in Pakistan** (s. 82).
5. **Outside jurisdiction** (ss. 83–86) — forwarded to a Magistrate or SP there; endorsement; the arrested person taken before the local Magistrate.
6. **Warrant in lieu of or in addition to summons — s. 90:** after **recording reasons**, (a) if the court has reason to believe that the person **has absconded or will not obey** the summons, or (b) if he **fails to appear** after due service and offers no reasonable excuse.

## 4.3 Proclamation for a person absconding — s. 87 (LHC 2020, 2023; ASJ 2025)

1. **Condition** — the court is **satisfied after taking evidence** that a person against whom **it has issued a warrant** has **absconded or is concealing himself** so that the warrant cannot be executed.
2. **Content** — a written proclamation requiring him to appear at a **specified place and time** **not less than thirty days** from the date of publication.
3. **Publication** — (a) **publicly read** in a conspicuous place of the town or village where he ordinarily resides; (b) **affixed** to a conspicuous part of his house or homestead or a conspicuous place of the town or village; and (c) a copy **affixed to the court-house**.
4. **Proof** — a written statement by the court that it was duly published on a specified day is **conclusive evidence** (s. 87(3)).

**Pre-conditions in short:** a **warrant** first; **evidence** of abscondence (the process server's statement on oath); a full **thirty days**; all **three** modes of publication. Without these the proclamation, and the attachment, are **illegal**.

## 4.4 Attachment of property — s. 88

1. The court issuing the proclamation **may at any time** order **attachment** of any property, **movable or immovable**, of the proclaimed person (within the district; outside it with the endorsement of the **Sessions Judge** of that district).
2. **Movable property** (debts etc.) — by **seizure**, appointment of a **receiver**, a written **prohibitory order** against delivery, or any two.
3. **Immovable property** — land paying revenue, through the **Collector (District Officer (Revenue))**; otherwise by **taking possession**, a **receiver**, a prohibitory order against payment of rent or delivery, or any two.
4. **Perishable** property or livestock may be **sold** at once.
5. **Claims and objections** by **third persons** (s. 88(6A)–(6D)) — within **six months** of attachment, inquired into and allowed or disallowed; a person whose claim is disallowed may sue in a **civil court** within **one year** to establish his right.
6. If the proclaimed person does not appear within the time, the attached property is at the **disposal of the Government**; it may be **sold** after **six months** (s. 88(7)).

## 4.5 Restoration of attached property — s. 89 and the appeal under s. 405

If, **within two years** from the date of attachment, the person whose property is at the Government's disposal **appears voluntarily** or is **apprehended**, and proves to the satisfaction of the court that he **did not abscond or conceal himself** to avoid execution of the warrant, **and** that he had **no such notice of the proclamation** as would have enabled him to attend within the time, the property — or the net sale proceeds, or the proceeds and the residue — shall be **delivered to him**, after deducting the costs of attachment.

**Remedy if restoration is refused — s. 405:** "Any person whose application under section 89 for the delivery of property or the proceeds of the sale thereof has been **rejected** by any Court may **appeal** to the Court to which appeals ordinarily lie from the sentences of the former Court" (from a Magistrate — the Sessions Court; from a Sessions Court — the High Court).

## 4.6 Consequences of being a proclaimed offender (ASJ 2025; SHC 2022)

1. **Attachment and forfeiture** of property (ss. 88–89).
2. Any **private person** may arrest him (s. 59); police may arrest without warrant (s. 54).
3. **Evidence may be recorded in his absence** (s. 512) and used later if the witness is dead or unavailable.
4. **Abscondence** is a relevant circumstance that may **corroborate** other evidence against him — but it is **not** by itself proof of guilt (an innocent person may also flee fearing the police).
5. **Bail:** a fugitive from law "loses some of his normal rights" — the superior courts hold that **pre-arrest bail** is an extraordinary relief, ordinarily **not** available to an absconder or proclaimed offender. The application is **maintainable** (the court has jurisdiction), but it is decided against him unless he shows that the **proclamation was illegal**, that he had **no notice**, or **exceptional** circumstances of **mala fide** (e.g., false implication shown on the record). For **post-arrest** bail, abscondence weighs heavily but does not automatically bar bail where the case is otherwise one of **further inquiry**.
6. **Limitation** in civil matters is not affected; but his **appeal or revision** may not be heard while he remains a fugitive.

## 4.7 Bonds for appearance — ss. 91 to 93

The court may require a person **present** in court to execute a **bond** for his appearance (s. 91); arrest on breach (s. 92); provisions apply to all summonses and warrants (s. 93).

# Chapter 5 · Search and seizure

## 5.1 Search warrants — when issued (LHC 2023)

1. **s. 96(1)** — a court may issue a search warrant where (a) it has reason to believe that a person to whom a **summons or order under s. 94** (to produce a document or thing) has been or might be addressed **will not produce** it; (b) the document or thing is **not known** to be in the possession of any person; or (c) a **general search or inspection** will serve the purposes of an inquiry, trial or other proceeding. (Documents in the custody of the **Post or Telegraph** authorities need a District Magistrate's or higher authority's warrant.)
2. **s. 98** — a Magistrate of the first class may issue a warrant to search a place suspected to contain **stolen property**, **forged documents**, false seals, **counterfeit coin**, **obscene objects**, or used for their deposit or sale, and to seize such things.
3. **s. 99A** — search for and seizure of publications **forfeited** by Government.
4. **s. 100** — **search for persons wrongfully confined** — a Magistrate of the first class who has reason to believe that a person is confined in circumstances amounting to an offence may issue a warrant; the person found is taken before a Magistrate.

## 5.2 General rules of search

1. **s. 102** — the occupant of a closed place must allow **free ingress**; otherwise s. 48 (breaking doors).
2. **s. 103** — **two or more respectable inhabitants of the locality** must be called to **witness** the search; a **list** of things seized is prepared and signed by them; the **occupant** may attend and gets a copy. (The rule does not apply to searches under the **CNSA 1997** — s. 25 CNSA excludes s. 103.)
3. **s. 165** — **search by a police officer** during investigation, after **recording in writing the grounds** of belief and the thing to be searched for; in person if practicable; copies of the record sent to the Magistrate; search of **bank records** only with the prior written permission of a **Sessions Judge** (for ss. 403, 406, 408, 409, 421–424, 465–477A PPC) or otherwise of the **High Court**.

## 6.2 Nuisance, urgent orders and land disputes

1. **s. 133** — **conditional order** for removal of a **public nuisance** (obstruction of a public way, dangerous trade, building likely to fall, dangerous animal); the person may show cause; not questioned in a civil court.
2. **s. 144** — **order absolute at once** in urgent cases of nuisance or **apprehended danger** — directing a person or the public to abstain from an act; in force for not more than **two consecutive days and seven days in a month** unless Government extends it in cases of danger to life or riot.
3. **s. 145** — dispute concerning **land or water** likely to cause a **breach of the peace**: the Magistrate calls for written statements about **actual possession**, decides **who was in possession** on the date of the order (or was **forcibly dispossessed within two months** before it), and declares that party entitled to remain until **evicted in due course of law**; he may **attach** the property in an emergency. He does **not** decide title.
4. **s. 146** — attachment and receiver where possession cannot be decided.
5. **ss. 127–128** — dispersal of unlawful assemblies; **firing** only on the specific direction of a police officer **not below ASP/DSP** (s. 128 proviso).

# Chapter 7 · Information to the police and investigation (ss. 154 to 176)

![From FIR to challan](investigation_flow.png)

## 7.1 First Information Report — s. 154

1. **Every information** relating to a **cognizable offence** given to the **officer in charge** of a police station, if oral, shall be **reduced to writing**, **read over** to the informant, **signed** by him, and its substance entered in the prescribed book (the FIR register).
2. **Duty to register** — the SHO **must** register an FIR if the information discloses a cognizable offence; he cannot first hold an inquiry into its truth (*Muhammad Bashir v. SHO Okara Cantt*, PLD 2007 SC 539). Remedy against refusal — **s. 22A(6)** petition, a **private complaint**, or Art. 199.
3. **Women victims (2016)** — information from a woman about an offence under ss. **336B, 354, 354A, 376 or 509** PPC is recorded in the presence of a **female police officer** or a **female family member** or any person with her consent; if she is **distressed**, it is recorded at her **residence** or a place of her choice.
4. **Evidentiary value** — the FIR is **not substantive evidence**; it may be used to **corroborate** its maker (Art. 153 QSO) or to **contradict** him (Arts. 140, 151 QSO), and as a **dying declaration** if the informant dies (Art. 46 QSO). **Delay** in lodging it, unexplained, casts doubt (room for consultation and deliberation).

:::case Mst. Sughran Bibi v. The State (PLD 2018 SC 595)
A seven-member bench held that there can be only **one FIR** for one occurrence; a **different version** of the same incident by another person is not registered as a fresh FIR but **recorded as a statement under s. 161** in the same case; the investigating officer must investigate **all versions** impartially; an accused is **not to be arrested** merely because he is named in the FIR unless the investigating officer finds **sufficient material** to justify arrest; and the **police report under s. 173** must state the officer's own opinion on the facts.
:::

## 7.2 Non-cognizable cases — s. 155

The SHO enters the substance in a book and **refers the informant to the Magistrate**; no investigation without a Magistrate's **order**; the investigating officer then has all powers **except arrest without warrant**.

## 7.3 Investigation — ss. 156 to 160

1. **s. 156** — the SHO may investigate a **cognizable** case without a Magistrate's order; a Magistrate empowered under s. 190 may **order** investigation (s. 156(3)).
2. **s. 156A** — **s. 295C PPC** — investigation only by an officer **not below Superintendent of Police**.
3. **s. 156B** — **zina** (not zina-bil-jabr) — investigation only by an officer **not below SP**, and **no arrest without the court's permission**.
4. **s. 157** — report to the Magistrate **forthwith**; proceed to the spot; no full investigation where the case is not serious or there is no sufficient ground (informant notified).
5. **s. 160** — the investigating officer may require the **attendance** of witnesses by written order.

## 7.4 Statements of witnesses — ss. 161, 162 (LHC 2023)

1. **s. 161** — the police may **examine orally** any person acquainted with the facts; he must answer truly except self-incriminating questions; the officer may reduce it into writing in a **separate record** for each person. A **woman victim** of ss. 336B, 354, 354A, 376, 509 — in the presence of a female police officer or family member.
2. **s. 161A (2016)** — the victim of rape or sexual abuse must be told of the right to **legal representation**; free legal aid from Bar Council lists.
3. **s. 162** — a statement to the police **shall not be signed**, and **cannot be used for any purpose** at the trial, **except** (i) by the **accused** (and, with the court's permission, the prosecution) to **contradict** the witness under Art. 140 QSO (s. 145 Evidence Act), and in re-examination to explain; (ii) a **dying declaration** (Art. 46 QSO); and (iii) a **discovery** statement (Art. 40 QSO).

## 7.5 Confessions — s. 164 (LHC 2023)

1. **Who** — any **Magistrate of the first class**, or a second-class Magistrate specially empowered, **not being a police officer**, during investigation or afterwards **before the inquiry or trial** begins.
2. **Warning** — before recording, the Magistrate shall **explain** that the person is **not bound** to confess and that the confession **may be used as evidence against him**.
3. **Satisfaction** — he shall not record it unless, **upon questioning**, he has **reason to believe it is voluntary** (the High Court Rules require **time for reflection**, removal of police from the court-room, assurance that the accused will not be sent back to police custody, and questions about any ill-treatment).
4. **Recording** — in the manner of s. 364 (the accused's own words, read over, signed by him and the Magistrate), with the **certificate** at the foot: "I have explained to (name) that he is not bound to make a confession … I believe that this confession was voluntarily made …".
5. **Forwarding** — to the Magistrate who will try or inquire into the case.
6. **Retraction** — a retracted confession may still found a conviction if found **voluntary and true**, but the courts look for **corroboration** in material particulars. A confession to the **police** is **inadmissible** (Art. 38 QSO); in **police custody**, inadmissible unless before a Magistrate (Art. 39).

## 7.6 Medical examination and DNA — ss. 164A, 164B (2016)

The victim of **rape, unnatural offence or sexual abuse** must be examined **immediately** by a registered medical practitioner — a **female** doctor for a female victim — with **consent**, escorted where possible by a **female police officer** or family member; the report records age, **DNA material**, injuries, mental condition; **DNA samples** are collected from the victim and the accused and sent to a **forensic laboratory**, with confidentiality.

## 7.7 Remand — s. 167 and s. 344 (LHC 2023)

1. **When** — the accused is arrested and the investigation **cannot be completed in 24 hours** and there are grounds for believing the accusation **well-founded**: the SHO (or an investigating officer not below **sub-inspector**) forwards the accused with a copy of the **diary entries** to the nearest Magistrate.
2. **Physical (police) remand** — the Magistrate may authorise detention **in such custody as he thinks fit**, for **not more than fifteen days in the whole**; no third-class or unempowered second-class Magistrate may authorise **police** custody; **reasons** must be recorded; a copy goes to the **Sessions Judge**.
3. **Women (s. 167(5)–(7))** — a **female** accused shall **not** be remanded to **police custody** except in cases of **qatl or dacoity**, for reasons recorded; she is **interrogated in prison** in the presence of a **jail officer and a female police officer**; if she must be taken out, only with the Magistrate's permission, in the company of a **female police officer**, and **never between sunset and sunrise**.
4. **Judicial remand (s. 344)** — after the police custody ends, or during inquiry or trial, the court may **remand the accused to jail** (judicial lock-up) by warrant, for **not more than fifteen days at a time**, when it postpones or adjourns. *Explanation:* if sufficient evidence has been obtained to raise a suspicion and further evidence may be obtained by a remand, that is reasonable cause.

## 7.8 Release and report — ss. 169 to 173

1. **s. 169** — release on bond where evidence is **deficient**; **s. 170** — forward the accused to the Magistrate where evidence is **sufficient**.
2. **s. 172** — the **case diary**, recorded day by day; the court may use it to aid an inquiry or trial but **not as evidence**; the accused cannot see it.
3. **s. 173 — police report (challan):** completed **without unnecessary delay**, forwarded **through the Public Prosecutor** to the Magistrate; the informant is told of the action taken; if not completed within **14 days** of the FIR, an **interim report** within **three days** after that, and the court shall commence the trial on it unless it decides otherwise for reasons; copy to the accused before trial; witnesses (other than public servants) produced and **bound** for appearance.
4. **Cancellation** — where the police find the case **false or without evidence**, they submit a **cancellation report**; the Magistrate may **agree** (case cancelled), **disagree** and take cognizance (s. 190(1)(b)), or direct further investigation. The complainant may still file a **private complaint**.

## 7.9 Inquest, burns, death in custody — ss. 174 to 176 (LHC 2023)

1. **s. 174** — on information of **suicide**, killing by another, by an animal or machinery or accident, or death in **suspicious** circumstances, the SHO informs the nearest Magistrate empowered to hold inquests, goes to the spot, and in the presence of **two or more respectable inhabitants** prepares an **inquest report** describing wounds and the apparent cause of death; the body may be sent for **post-mortem**. First-class Magistrates may hold inquests.
2. **s. 174A** — person **grievously injured by burns** (fire, kerosene, acid, chemical): the duty Medical Officer or SHO informs the Magistrate; the Medical Officer records the **statement** of the injured at once, and the Magistrate also records it if possible.
3. **Death in police custody — s. 176(1):** the **nearest Magistrate empowered to hold inquests shall** hold an **inquiry** into the cause of death (it is **mandatory**), with all the powers he has in an inquiry into an offence, and may cause the body to be **disinterred** (s. 176(2)). The officers are liable under PPC (qatl, s. 337K torture) and the **Torture and Custodial Death (Prevention and Punishment) Act 2022**.

## 7.10 Remedies of a complainant when the police are protecting the accused (ASJ 2025)

1. **Petition to the ex-officio Justice of the Peace** (s. 22A(6)) — for registration of the FIR, **transfer of investigation**, or directions against **neglect, failure or excess**.
2. **Change of investigation** by the senior police hierarchy under the **Police Order 2002** (the district police officer or higher, through a board, where the complaint is about the investigation's fairness).
3. **Private complaint** under **s. 200** on the same facts — the complainant leads his own evidence; where a complaint and a police case on the same occurrence are pending, the **complaint case is tried first** or both together (the settled practice).
4. **Magistrate's powers** — on the s. 173 report, the Magistrate is **not bound** by the police opinion; he may take **cognizance** of the offence against the accused excluded by the police (s. 190(1)(b)), order **further investigation**, or summon witnesses.
5. **Trial court's powers** — summon **any person as a witness** or produce **omitted evidence** (s. 540); add an accused where evidence appears (ss. 351, 190).
6. **High Court** — **Art. 199** (mandamus to register the FIR or transfer investigation in exceptional cases) and **s. 561A** to prevent abuse of process.
7. Action against the police officer — departmental proceedings and the offences of **wrongful confinement**, **false record** (PPC ss. 218–219), and penalties under the Police Order 2002.
