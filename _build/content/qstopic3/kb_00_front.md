# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 3 covers

1. **What pleadings are**, why they matter, and the **basic rules** of Order VI.
2. **Particulars of a plaint** — every item of O. VII rr. 1–8, and the **papers** that go with it (lists, copies, address, legal representatives, court fee, valuation, notices).
3. **Particulars of a written statement** — O. VIII, **preliminary objections**, the art of **specific denial**, **additional pleas** and **set-off**.
4. **Drafting technique** — structure, language, common mistakes.
5. **Model plaints** for the suits most often set in examinations and met in a Civil Judge's court.
6. **Model written statements** in reply.
7. **Common applications** — temporary injunction, rejection of plaint, amendment.
8. **From pleadings to issues to judgment** — the syllabus says the examination tests the ability "to frame issues, to write a judgment and to discuss the admissibility of evidence".

:::tip How a drafting question is marked
Examiners look for: (1) the **correct court and title**; (2) **parties** fully described; (3) **material facts** in short numbered paragraphs, in **chronological** order, with dates and sums in **figures**; (4) **cause of action** and when it arose; (5) **jurisdiction** — territorial and pecuniary; (6) **limitation** if relevant; (7) **valuation and court fee**; (8) a precise **prayer**; (9) **signature** and **verification**; (10) the **legal basis** (the Act and section) shown in the heading. Do not write evidence, arguments or case law in a plaint.
:::
