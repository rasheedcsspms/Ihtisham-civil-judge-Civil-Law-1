## 1.2 Minto–Morley Reforms 1909 — "a tool of change" (LHC 2023)

1. **Background** — the Simla Deputation (1906) demanded separate electorates; the British wanted to satisfy moderates after the Bengal partition agitation and to associate Indians with legislation.
2. **Main provisions (Indian Councils Act 1909):** enlarged **Imperial Legislative Council** (from 16 to **60** members) and provincial councils; **non-official majority** in provincial councils; members could **discuss the budget**, ask **supplementary questions** and move resolutions; an **Indian** appointed to the Viceroy's **Executive Council** (S. P. Sinha) and to the India Council; and — most important — **separate electorates** for Muslims, with **weightage**.
3. **Significance:** the **first constitutional recognition** of Muslims as a **separate political entity**; foundation of **communal representation** retained in 1919, 1932 and 1935; the beginning of **representative** (though not responsible) government; criticised by Congress as "divide and rule"; for Muslims, the **first step** on the constitutional road to Pakistan.
4. **Limits:** franchise very narrow; councils merely advisory; executive not responsible to the legislature.

## 1.3 Why the Muslims demanded a separate homeland (LHC 2023)

1. **Two-nation theory** — Muslims and Hindus were separate **nations** by religion, culture, history, language, law and social customs (Sir Syed; Iqbal; Jinnah's Lahore address 1940).
2. **Religious and cultural identity** — fear of absorption in a Hindu majority; Urdu–Hindi controversy; **Shuddhi** and **Sangathan**.
3. **Political** — **majority rule** in a united India would mean permanent Hindu domination; the **Nehru Report** rejected Muslim safeguards; **Congress ministries 1937–39** showed what Hindu rule meant (Pirpur Report).
4. **Economic** — Muslims backward in trade, industry, services and banking; Hindu moneylenders and landlords in Bengal and Punjab.
5. **Educational** — late adoption of modern education; Aligarh as the response.
6. **Historical** — Muslims had ruled India for centuries; the 1857 aftermath fell hardest on them.
7. **Failure of constitutional formulas** — Lucknow Pact, Delhi Proposals, Fourteen Points, Cabinet Mission all failed due to Congress intransigence.
8. **Leadership and mandate** — Jinnah's leadership and the **1945–46 elections** gave the League the **mandate**.

# Chapter 2 · The first years and the constitutional history

## 2.1 Early problems (1947–1951)

1. **Refugees** — about **7 million** migrants; massacres in Punjab.
2. **Division of assets** — Pakistan's share of cash **Rs. 750 million**; **Rs. 550 million** withheld until Gandhi's fast; military stores largely withheld.
3. **Canal water dispute** — India stopped water on **1 April 1948** → **Indus Waters Treaty** (**19 September 1960**, Karachi, **World Bank** mediation; Ayub Khan and Nehru) — **eastern rivers** (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India; **western rivers** (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan; India **held the treaty "in abeyance"** in **April 2025** after the Pahalgam attack (Topic 3).
4. **Kashmir** — accession instrument disputed; war **1947–48**; UN ceasefire **1 January 1949**; **UNCIP resolutions** of **13 August 1948** and **5 January 1949** (plebiscite); India revoked **Article 370** on **5 August 2019**.
5. **Princely states** — **Junagadh** and **Manavadar** acceded to Pakistan but were occupied by India; **Hyderabad** occupied (Operation Polo, Sept 1948).
6. **Administrative** — no capital infrastructure (Karachi); Quaid's death **11 September 1948**.

# Chapter 3 · Federalism (LHC 2023 — "a federation in form and unitary in spirit")

## 3.1 Federal features of the 1973 Constitution

1. **Written, rigid** constitution (amendment by **two-thirds** of each House — Art. 239).
2. **Division of powers** — the **Federal Legislative List** (Part I and Part II); after the **18th Amendment**, **residuary** powers with the **provinces** (Concurrent List abolished).
3. **Bicameral Parliament** — the **Senate** gives **equal representation** to the provinces (23 each after 2018 restructuring; total **96**), protecting smaller units.
4. **Independent judiciary** — the Supreme Court / FCC resolves federal–provincial disputes (Art. 184(1)).
5. **Council of Common Interests** (Arts. 153–154) — PM, the four CMs and three federal members; formulates and regulates policy on Part II of the Federal List (railways, electricity, natural gas, major ports); meets at least **once in 90 days**; secretariat in the IPC Ministry.
6. **National Finance Commission** (Art. 160) — every five years; the provinces' share cannot be **reduced** below the previous award (Art. 160(3A)).
7. **National Economic Council** (Art. 156); **natural resources** jointly and equally owned (Art. 172(3) — oil and gas); **net hydel profits** to the province of generation (Art. 161).

## 3.2 The 7th NFC Award (2009, still in force)

1. **Vertical share** — provinces **57.5%** of the divisible pool from FY2011–12 (56% in FY2010–11); federation 42.5%.
2. **Horizontal criteria** — **population 82%**, **poverty / backwardness 10.3%**, **revenue collection / generation 5%**, **inverse population density 2.7%**.
3. **Provincial shares** — **Punjab 51.74%**, **Sindh 24.55%**, **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa 14.62%**, **Balochistan 9.09%**; **1%** of the pool to KP for the war on terror.
4. A new (10th / 11th) NFC has been constituted repeatedly without a new award; the federal government seeks a revision because its fiscal space shrank after the 18th Amendment.

## 3.3 Why "unitary in spirit" — weaknesses

1. **Centralised** institutions — the federal government controls major revenues (income tax, sales tax on goods, customs), the civil service (**PAS / PSP**), and security.
2. **Frequent military rule** and **centralisation** (Basic Democracies, LFO, 17th Amendment); **governor's rule** and **emergencies** (Arts. 232–234) — e.g., **KP, October 2026**.
3. **Punjab's dominance** in population, the National Assembly (about half the seats), the army and bureaucracy.
4. **Weak local governments** (Art. 140A unimplemented in many provinces).
5. **CCI and NFC** meetings delayed; **provincial grievances** — Balochistan (resources, missing persons), Sindh (water, NFC), KP (net hydel profits, merged districts).
6. **Federal ministries** retaining devolved subjects (education, health) after the 18th Amendment.

## 3.4 Strengths and recommendations

1. The **18th Amendment** (provincial autonomy), the **Senate's equality**, constitutional bodies (**CCI, NFC, NEC**), judicial review.
2. **Recommendations** — regular CCI meetings; a new NFC award with resource-based criteria; **empowered local governments** (Art. 140A); fiscal decentralisation (provincial taxation of agriculture and services); inclusive recruitment; dialogue in Balochistan; respect for provincial mandates (no misuse of emergency powers).

## 4.3 Rivers and water

1. **Indus** — about **3,180 km**; rises near **Lake Mansarovar** in **Tibet**; enters Pakistan in **Gilgit-Baltistan**; joined by the **Kabul** at Attock; by the **Panjnad** (five eastern rivers) near Mithankot; enters the Arabian Sea near **Thatta / Keti Bandar**.
2. **Jhelum** (from Verinag spring, Kashmir — Mangla dam), **Chenab** (Himachal / Kashmir), **Ravi** (Lahore), **Sutlej** (from Tibet's Rakas Tal), **Beas** (wholly in India, joins the Sutlej).
3. **Kabul, Swat, Kurram, Gomal** — western tributaries; Balochistan rivers — **Zhob, Bolan, Hingol** (longest in Balochistan), **Dasht, Porali, Hab, Nari** (mostly seasonal).
4. **Dams:** **Tarbela** (Indus, completed **1976**, among the world's largest **earth-filled** dams), **Mangla** (Jhelum, **1967**), **Warsak** (Kabul river), **Diamer-Bhasha** and **Mohmand** (under construction), **Dasu** (hydropower), **Neelum–Jhelum** (969 MW), **Khanpur, Rawal, Simly** (Islamabad's water), **Hub**.
5. **Barrages:** Guddu, **Sukkur** (Lloyd Barrage, 1932), Kotri, Taunsa, Chashma, Jinnah, Kalabagh (proposed dam — controversial).
6. **Lakes:** **Manchar** (largest freshwater lake), **Keenjhar** (Kalri — supplies Karachi), **Saif-ul-Malook** (Naran), **Hanna** (Quetta), **Satpara** (Skardu), **Attabad** (formed by a 2010 landslide).
7. **Water Apportionment Accord 1991** — among the provinces; **IRSA** regulates.

## 4.4 Deserts, plateaus, passes, ports

1. **Deserts:** **Thar** (Sindh — Thar coal), **Cholistan** (Rohi — Punjab), **Thal** (Punjab), **Kharan** (Balochistan).
2. **Plateaus:** **Potohar** (oil fields), **Balochistan plateau**.
3. **Passes:** **Khyber** (Peshawar–Kabul), **Bolan** (Quetta–Sibi), **Khunjerab** (China), **Gomal, Tochi, Kurram**, **Lowari** (Chitral — Lowari tunnel, about 8.5 km), **Babusar** (Kaghan–Chilas), **Shandur** (polo).
4. **Tunnels:** **Khojak** (railway, Balochistan, **1891**, about **3.9 km**), **Lowari** (road), **Kohat** (road, Japanese assisted), **Attabad** tunnels (KKH).
5. **Ports:** **Karachi**, **Port Qasim**, **Gwadar** (deep-sea; CPEC); **Ormara**, **Pasni** (naval / fishing).
6. **Climate** — continental; monsoon (July–September); **Jacobabad** among the hottest places on earth; Pakistan among the **ten most climate-vulnerable** countries (Germanwatch); **2022 floods** — about one-third of the country under water, **33 million** affected, losses about **US$30 billion**; floods again in **2025** (Punjab, KP — Buner cloudburst).

## 5.1 Essay: "Population explosion is a constant threat to the economy" (LHC 2020)

1. **Facts** — 241.49 million, growing **2.55%** a year (among the highest in Asia), total fertility rate about **3.6**; doubling in about 27 years; about **64%** under 30.
2. **Effects** — pressure on **food, water** (per capita water below **1,000 m³** — scarcity), **jobs** (about 2 million entrants a year), **education** (about 26 million out-of-school children), **health**, housing and **urban slums**; **low savings** and investment; **environmental** degradation; **poverty**; **security** (unemployed youth).
3. **Causes** — early marriage, low female education, son preference, limited access to contraception (unmet need about 17%), religious misconceptions, poverty.
4. **Remedies** — population as a **national priority** (CCI 2018 recommendations; Supreme Court's 2018 suo motu); **female education** and **employment**; **lady health workers**; **child marriage** laws (ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025 — age 18); integration of family planning into primary health; involvement of **ulama** (Al-Azhar and Iran examples); **Bangladesh** comparison (TFR about 2); turning the **youth bulge** into a **demographic dividend** through skills.

# Chapter 7 · Foreign policy

## 7.1 Principles and milestones

1. **Art. 40** — preserve **Islamic unity**, support the common interests of Asia, Africa and Latin America, promote **international peace**, and settle disputes **peacefully**.
2. **UN membership** — **30 September 1947** (Afghanistan voted against); **UNSC non-permanent member** eight times — **1952–53, 1968–69, 1976–77, 1983–84, 1993–94, 2003–04, 2012–13, 2025–26**.
3. **Alliances** — **SEATO** (1954), **Baghdad Pact / CENTO** (1955) — left both in the 1970s; **Non-Aligned Movement** (1979); **RCD** (1964) → **ECO** (1985); **SAARC** (1985); **OIC** (founding member 1969; 2nd summit **Lahore 1974**); **SCO** (full member **2017**; hosted the **SCO Heads of Government meeting**, Islamabad, October 2024).
4. **China** — diplomatic relations **21 May 1951**; **1963 boundary agreement**; **KKH** (1978); CPEC; "**all-weather strategic cooperative partnership**".
5. **USA** — Mutual Defence Agreement 1954; U-2 base (Badaber); 1970s opening to China via Pakistan (Kissinger 1971); Afghan jihad (1980s); **Pressler** sanctions (1990); post-9/11 alliance; **2025 thaw** (Trump; tariffs agreement; minerals and crypto cooperation; Field Marshal Munir's visits).
6. **India** — wars **1947–48, 1965** (Tashkent Declaration **10 January 1966**), **1971**, **Kargil 1999**; **Simla Agreement** (**2 July 1972**); **Lahore Declaration** (Feb 1999); **Kartarpur Corridor** (9 Nov 2019); **May 2025 conflict** — Pahalgam attack (22 April), **Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos** (10 May), ceasefire **10 May 2025** (Topic 3).
7. **Afghanistan** — Durand Line dispute; refugees (about 3 million at peak; repatriation drives since 2023); TTP sanctuaries; border clashes **October 2025** and **2026** (Topic 3).
8. **Saudi Arabia** — **Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement** (**17 September 2025**, Riyadh) — aggression against one is aggression against both; widened into the **Makkah Joint Defence Agreement** with **Türkiye** (**7 August 2026**).
9. **Iran** — gas pipeline (IP), border; Pakistan's mediation efforts in the **2026 Iran war** (Topic 3).
10. **Nuclear** — tests **28 May 1998** (Chagai — **Youm-e-Takbeer**) and 30 May 1998; **A. Q. Khan**; command — **National Command Authority** (2000); since 2025 the **Commander National Strategic Command**.

# Chapter 8 · National issues — essay outlines

:::eg 1. Corruption and accountability in Pakistan (LHC 2023)
**Facts:** Transparency International CPI — Pakistan scored about **27/100**, ranked about **135th of 180** (2024). **Root causes:** weak rule of law and slow justice; political patronage and elite capture; discretionary powers of officials; low salaries; complex taxation and a cash economy; lack of transparency in procurement; politicised accountability (NAB seen as a tool of victimisation); social tolerance of corruption; military and civil interventions. **Efforts:** Prevention of Corruption Act 1947; Ehtesab Act 1997; **National Accountability Ordinance 1999** (NAB) — amended 2022–23 (threshold Rs. 500 million); FIA; Anti-Corruption Establishments; **Right of Access to Information Act 2017**; e-procurement (EPADS); asset declarations. **Remedies:** depoliticised, independent accountability; digitisation of land records and tax; judicial reforms (time-bound trials); merit-based civil service; protection of whistle-blowers; asset recovery; civic education; Islamic ethic of trust (amanah).
:::

:::eg 2. Pakistan's water crisis
Per capita availability fell from about 5,260 m³ (1951) to below **1,000 m³** (scarcity line); storage only about **30 days** (India about 170); Indus Waters Treaty and India's 2025 suspension; glacier melt and climate change; wasteful flood irrigation; groundwater depletion; urban supply. **Remedies:** new storage (Diamer-Bhasha, Mohmand), canal lining, drip irrigation, water pricing, the **National Water Policy 2018**, legal action under the IWT, rainwater harvesting.
:::

:::eg 3. Energy crisis
Circular debt (about Rs. 2.4 trillion), expensive IPPs and capacity payments, transmission losses and theft, reliance on imported fuel (oil shock of 2026), under-used hydel and solar (solar boom 2024–25 — net-metering changes); **Remedies:** renegotiation of IPP contracts (2024–25), privatisation of DISCOs, indigenous coal (Thar), hydropower, renewables (Alternative and Renewable Energy Policy 2019 — 30% target by 2030), efficiency.
:::

:::eg 4. Climate change and Pakistan
Contributes under **1%** of global emissions but among the most vulnerable; **2022 floods**; heatwaves; glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs); **COP27 loss and damage fund** (Pakistan chaired the G-77 in 2022); **Art. 9A** (2024 — right to a clean and healthy environment); **Climate Change Act 2017**; **Living Indus Initiative**; "**Recharge Pakistan**"; National Adaptation Plan 2023.
:::

:::eg 5. Terrorism and extremism
Waves since 2001; TTP (2007); **APS Peshawar (16 Dec 2014)** → **National Action Plan** (20 points, revised 2021); operations **Zarb-e-Azb** (2014), **Radd-ul-Fasaad** (2017), **Azm-e-Istehkam** (2024); resurgence after the Taliban takeover of Kabul (Aug 2021); **Jaffar Express hijacking** (11 March 2025); Balochistan insurgency (BLA); **Paigham-e-Pakistan**; NACTA; remedies — governance in ex-FATA, Afghan border management, madrassa reform, counter-narrative, justice-sector reform.
:::

:::eg 6. Other likely essays
**Education emergency** (26 million out-of-school; Art. 25A); **judicial reforms and delay** (backlog about 2.2 million cases; ADR; e-courts); **governance and the civil service**; **CPEC — opportunities and challenges**; **Pakistan–India relations after May 2025**; **Pakistan–Afghanistan relations**; **role of youth**; **gender equality**; **Balochistan**; **local government**; **economic self-reliance and the IMF**.
:::
