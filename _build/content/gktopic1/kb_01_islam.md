## 1.2 The Quran as a source of law — modes of legislation (KPHC 2026)

1. **Gradualism (tadrij)** — prohibitions introduced in stages, e.g., **wine** in four stages (16:67; 2:219; 4:43; 5:90–91); **riba** (30:39; 4:161; 3:130; 2:275–279).
2. **Brevity and general principles** — most rules are **general** (justice, consultation, fulfilment of contracts — 5:1), leaving details to the Sunnah and ijtihad.
3. **Ease and removal of hardship** — "Allah intends for you ease" (2:185); "no soul is burdened beyond its capacity" (2:286).
4. **Reasoned commands** — many rules state their **purpose** (*'illah*), the basis of **qiyas** (e.g., the wisdom of qisas — "in qisas there is life for you", 2:179).
5. **Specific detailed rules** where needed — inheritance (4:11–12, 176), divorce and iddat (2:228–237; 65), hudood (5:38; 24:2, 4).
6. **Abrogation (naskh)** — a later ruling replacing an earlier one (2:106); classical scholars recognised it in a limited number of verses (e.g., iddat of a widow 2:240 by 2:234; wine verses); modern scholars (e.g., Shah Waliullah reduced the cases to **five**) narrow it further.

# Chapter 2 · Hadith and Sunnah

1. **Sunnah** — the sayings (*qawl*), actions (*fi'l*) and tacit approvals (*taqrir*) of the Prophet (PBUH); **Hadith** — the report of the Sunnah, with **isnad** (chain of narrators) and **matn** (text).
2. **Classification:** **sahih** (authentic), **hasan** (good), **da'if** (weak), **mawdu'** (fabricated); by number of narrators — **mutawatir** (mass-transmitted) and **ahad**.
3. **Sihah Sittah** (the six authentic books): **Sahih al-Bukhari** (Imam Muhammad bin Isma'il al-Bukhari, d. 256 AH — most authentic book after the Quran), **Sahih Muslim** (Imam Muslim bin al-Hajjaj), **Sunan Abu Dawud**, **Jami' al-Tirmidhi**, **Sunan al-Nasa'i**, **Sunan Ibn Majah**.
4. **Muwatta** — **Imam Malik** — the earliest surviving compilation of hadith and fiqh.
5. **Musnad Ahmad** — **Imam Ahmad bin Hanbal**.
6. **First compilation ordered** by Caliph **Umar bin Abdul Aziz** (Umayyad, r. 717–720 CE).
7. **Most narrations:** **Hazrat Abu Hurairah** (about 5,374); among women — **Hazrat A'ishah** (about 2,210).

## 3.1 The Charter of Madinah (Mithaq-e-Madinah), 1 AH

The **first written constitution** in history (about **47–52 clauses**), agreed between the Muhajirun, the Ansar and the **Jewish tribes** of Madinah. Key principles: (1) the signatories form **one community (ummah)** for defence; (2) **religious freedom** — "the Jews have their religion and the Muslims theirs"; (3) the Prophet (PBUH) is the **final arbiter** of disputes — the **supremacy of law** and a central **judicial authority**; (4) **collective defence** of Madinah; (5) **blood-money and ransom** to be paid by tribes as before; (6) **no protection** to Quraysh; (7) **justice** for the oppressed and no help to criminals. It laid the foundation of a **pluralistic state** governed by law.

## 3.2 The Farewell Sermon (Khutbat Hajjat al-Wida')

1. The **sanctity of life, property and honour** — "your blood, your property and your honour are sacred".
2. **Equality** — "no Arab has superiority over a non-Arab … except by piety".
3. Abolition of **riba** (starting with that of his uncle Abbas) and of **blood feuds** of the Days of Ignorance.
4. **Rights of women** — "treat women well"; rights and duties of spouses.
5. **Rule of law and personal responsibility** — "no one is responsible for another's crime".
6. **Trusts** to be returned; **slaves** treated well.
7. The **Quran and Sunnah** left as guidance; completion of religion (5:3).

It is often called the **first charter of human rights**.

# Chapter 4 · Beliefs and pillars

1. **Articles of faith (Iman-e-Mufassal):** belief in **Allah**, His **angels**, His **books** (Tawrat — Musa; Zabur — Dawud; Injil — Isa; Quran — Muhammad), His **messengers**, the **Day of Judgment**, **taqdir** (divine decree) and **life after death**.
2. **Tawhid** (oneness — *Tawhid al-Rububiyyah, al-Uluhiyyah, al-Asma wa al-Sifat*); **Risalat** (prophethood) and **Khatm-e-Nubuwwat** (finality — 33:40; Constitution Art. 260(3)); **Akhirah**.
3. **Five pillars:** **Shahadah**, **Salah** (five times; made obligatory on the **Mi'raj**), **Zakat** (2 AH), **Sawm** (Ramadan, 2 AH), **Hajj** (9 AH; once in life for those able).
4. **Zakat:** **2.5%** of savings held for a **lunar year** above the **nisab** — **52.5 tolas of silver** or **7.5 tolas of gold**; **ushr** on agricultural produce — **10%** on rain-fed land, **5%** on irrigated land; **eight heads** of recipients (9:60); in Pakistan — **Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980**.
5. **Hajj:** 8–13 **Dhul-Hijjah**; **fard** acts — **ihram**, **wuquf at Arafat** (9 Dhul-Hijjah — the essence of Hajj), **tawaf al-ziyarah**; **wajib** — Muzdalifah, stoning (rami), sa'i, sacrifice, shaving / trimming; kinds — **ifrad, qiran, tamattu'**.

# Chapter 6 · Sources of Islamic law and the schools of fiqh

## 6.1 The primary sources

{w:18,82}
|**Quran**|The word of Allah — the first and supreme source|
|**Sunnah**|The second source — explains, details and supplements the Quran (16:44; 59:7)|
|**Ijma'**|**Consensus** of qualified Muslim jurists (mujtahids) of an age on a legal question — based on "my ummah will never agree on an error"|
|**Qiyas**|**Analogical deduction** — extending a ruling to a new case sharing the same **'illah** (effective cause): e.g., prohibition of **narcotics** by analogy with **wine** (intoxication). Four elements: **asl** (original case), **far'** (new case), **hukm** (ruling), **'illah** (cause)|

The four sources are approved by the **hadith of Mu'adh bin Jabal**: when sent as judge (qazi) to **Yemen**, he said he would judge by the **Book of Allah**, then the **Sunnah**, and then by his own **ijtihad** ("I will exert my judgment"); the Prophet (PBUH) approved.

## 6.2 Secondary sources and methods

1. **Istihsan** (juristic preference) — **Hanafi**; departing from strict analogy for a stronger reason (equity).
2. **Istislah / al-maslahah al-mursalah** (public interest) — **Maliki**; e.g., Umar's diwan, Uthman's standard Quran.
3. **'Urf** (custom) — accepted if not contrary to the texts; "custom is authoritative" (*al-'adah muhakkamah*); pre-Islamic customs were **retained** (e.g., *qasamah*, diyat in camels, *mudarabah*), **modified** (marriage, divorce — limited to three) or **abolished** (riba, infanticide, *zihar* as divorce, adoption creating kinship) (KPHC 2026).
4. **Istishab** (presumption of continuity) — a state continues until the contrary is proved (basis of the presumption of innocence and of continuing ownership).
5. **Sadd al-dhara'i'** — blocking the means to evil.
6. **Maqasid al-Shari'ah** (objectives — al-Ghazali, al-Shatibi): protection of **religion (din), life (nafs), intellect ('aql), lineage (nasl) and property (mal)** (and honour).

## 6.3 Ijtihad and taqlid (KPHC 2026)

1. **Ijtihad** — the **utmost effort** of a qualified jurist (**mujtahid**) to derive a ruling from the sources. **Qualifications:** knowledge of Arabic, the Quran and its legal verses, the Sunnah, ijma', usul al-fiqh, the objectives of the Shari'ah, and personal piety. **Kinds:** *ijtihad mutlaq* (absolute — founders of schools), *ijtihad fi al-madhhab* (within a school), *ijtihad fi al-masa'il* (on particular issues). **Collective (ijtihad jama'i)** — through bodies such as the **Council of Islamic Ideology**, the **Federal Shariat Court**, the **OIC Fiqh Academy**; Allama **Iqbal** proposed that the **legislature** exercise ijtihad (*Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*, lecture 6).
2. **"Closing the door of ijtihad"** (*insidad bab al-ijtihad*) — a view that arose about the 4th century AH; modern scholars regard it as a historical fiction; ijtihad continues.
3. **Judicial ijtihad** — the judge's reasoned interpretation of Islamic law in deciding cases; in Pakistan, the **Federal Shariat Court** (Art. 203D) and the **Shariat Appellate Bench** exercise it when testing laws against the **Injunctions of Islam** (e.g., *Gul Hassan* — qisas and diyat; the **riba** judgments of 1991, 1999 and **2022**).
4. **Taqlid** — following the opinion of a recognised school without independent inquiry into its proofs.

# Chapter 7 · The Islamic concept of justice ('adl)

## 7.1 Meaning and importance

**'Adl** means placing everything in its **right place**, giving everyone his **due**, and **equality** in treatment; **qist** — equity, fairness; **zulm** — its opposite. Justice is the **purpose of the prophets' mission**: "We sent Our messengers with clear signs and sent down with them the Book and the **Balance** so that people may establish **justice**" (**al-Hadid 57:25**).

## 7.2 Key Quranic verses

1. "Allah commands you to render **trusts** to those to whom they are due, and when you **judge** between people, to judge with **justice**" — **an-Nisa 4:58**.
2. "O you who believe! Stand out firmly for **justice**, as witnesses to Allah, even though it be **against yourselves**, or your **parents**, or your **kin**, rich or poor" — **an-Nisa 4:135**.
3. "Let not the **hatred of a people** make you swerve from justice. Be just: that is nearer to piety" — **al-Ma'idah 5:8**.
4. "Allah commands **justice ('adl)**, **excellence (ihsan)** and giving to kinsfolk, and forbids indecency, evil and **rebellion**" — **an-Nahl 16:90**.
5. "Whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed — they are the **wrongdoers / transgressors / disbelievers**" — **al-Ma'idah 5:44, 45, 47**.
6. "O Dawud! We have made you a vicegerent on earth, so **judge between people in truth** and do not follow desire" — **Sad 38:26**.
7. "Do not consume one another's property unjustly, nor offer it to **judges** to devour part of others' property sinfully" — **al-Baqarah 2:188** (prohibition of **bribing judges**).
8. "If you judge, judge between them with justice; Allah loves those who are just" — **al-Ma'idah 5:42**.

## 7.3 Key ahadith on judging

1. **Three judges:** "Judges are of three kinds — **one in Paradise and two in Hell**: a judge who knows the truth and judges by it is in Paradise; a judge who knows the truth but judges unjustly, and a judge who judges in **ignorance**, are in Hell" (Abu Dawud).
2. "When a judge gives a decision, having tried his best (**ijtihad**) and is right, he has **two rewards**; if he errs, he has **one reward**" (Bukhari, Muslim).
3. "A judge shall not decide between two persons **while he is angry**" (Bukhari).
4. "The **burden of proof** is on the **claimant**, and the **oath** is on the one who **denies**" (*al-bayyinatu 'ala al-mudda'i wal-yaminu 'ala man ankar* — Tirmidhi, Bayhaqi).
5. "**Avert the hudood** by doubts" (*idra'u al-hudud bi al-shubuhat*).
6. "Whoever is appointed a judge has been **slaughtered without a knife**" (Abu Dawud) — the gravity of the office.
7. "I am only a human; you bring your disputes to me; one of you may be more eloquent … whatever I decide in his favour from his brother's right, I am giving him a **piece of fire**" (Bukhari) — the judge decides on the **evidence**; the truth remains with Allah.
8. **The Makhzumi woman** — when Usamah interceded for a noblewoman who had stolen, the Prophet (PBUH) said: "The nations before you were destroyed because they punished the poor and let off the rich. By Allah, **if Fatimah daughter of Muhammad stole, I would cut off her hand**" (Bukhari) — **equality before law**.
9. The Prophet (PBUH) told Ali when sending him to Yemen as judge: "**Do not decide** for one party **until you have heard the other**" (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi) — **audi alteram partem**.

# Chapter 8 · The judiciary in Islam — the administration of justice

![The Islamic system of administration of justice](justice_system.png)

## 8.1 Historical development

1. **The Prophet (PBUH)** was himself the **chief judge** (4:65 — "they will not believe until they make you judge in their disputes"); he appointed judges — **Mu'adh bin Jabal** and **Ali** to Yemen, **Attab bin Usaid** to Makkah; used **arbitration** (Sa'd bin Mu'adh in the case of **Banu Qurayzah**).
2. **Abu Bakr** — judged himself; **Umar** acted as judge in Madinah during his caliphate.
3. **Hazrat Umar** — **separated the judiciary from the executive**: appointed independent **qazis** in the provinces — **Abu al-Darda** (Madinah), **Shuraih** (Kufa — served about 60 years), **Ka'b bin Sur** (Basra), **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari** (Basra / Kufa), **Qais bin Abi al-As** (Egypt); fixed their **salaries** from the bait-ul-mal so that they would be independent and above bribery; required them to be men of **wealth and status** (to resist influence) and **learning**.
4. **Hazrat Ali** — wrote detailed instructions to **Malik al-Ashtar**, governor of Egypt, on choosing judges: the **best** of the people, **not easily irritated**, not **stubborn**, not greedy, **patient** in investigating, firm when the truth is clear, **well paid** and given **high status** so that no one can influence them.
5. **Umayyads and Abbasids** — the office of **Qazi al-Qudat** (chief justice) — first held by **Imam Abu Yusuf** under **Harun al-Rashid** (Abbasid); appointment of provincial qazis; growth of **mazalim** courts.
6. **Mughal India** — **Qazi-ul-Quzat**, the **Sadr-us-Sudur**, qazis in every pargana; **Fatawa-e-Alamgiri** compiled under **Aurangzeb**.

## 8.2 Hazrat Umar's letter to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari — the charter of judging

The most famous document on Islamic judicial procedure. Its principles:

1. **Judging is a firm obligation** and a followed **Sunnah**.
2. **Understand** the case well when it is presented; a right that is not **enforced** is of no use.
3. Treat the parties **equally** — in your **sitting, your look and your justice** — so that the **strong** does not hope for your **partiality** and the **weak** does not despair of your **justice**.
4. **The burden of proof is on the claimant**, and the **oath** on the one who denies.
5. **Compromise (sulh)** between Muslims is permitted, except one that makes the lawful unlawful or the unlawful lawful.
6. **Review**: nothing should prevent you from **reconsidering** a judgment given yesterday when you find on reflection that the truth was otherwise — returning to the truth is better than persisting in error.
7. **Analogy (qiyas)**: understand what is not found in the Quran and Sunnah; know **similar cases** and reason by analogy.
8. Give a claimant who says his evidence is absent a **time** to produce it; if he produces it, give him his right; otherwise decide against him.
9. **Muslims are competent witnesses** against each other, **except** a person **convicted of a hadd** (qazf), one proved to have given **false evidence**, or one suspected of **bias** through kinship or patronage.
10. Avoid **anger, anxiety, boredom** and annoying the parties at the time of judging; judging rightly brings **reward**.

## 8.3 Qualifications of a qazi

1. **Muslim** (for Muslim parties), **adult**, **sane**, **free**.
2. **'Adalah** — of **just and upright** character, not a sinner (fasiq).
3. **Knowledge ('ilm)** — of the Quran, Sunnah, ijma' and qiyas; ideally a **mujtahid** (Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali); the Hanafis allow a learned **muqallid** who follows established opinion.
4. **Sound senses** — hearing, sight, speech (to understand evidence and announce judgment).
5. **Gender** — the majority required a **male**; **Abu Hanifa** allowed a **woman** to judge in matters where her testimony is accepted (all except **hudood and qisas**); **Ibn Jarir al-Tabari** allowed women in all cases. (In Pakistan women serve at all levels of the judiciary, including the **Supreme Court** since **2022**.)
6. **Not seeking** the office — "We do not appoint one who asks for it".

## 8.4 Conduct and etiquette of a qazi (adab al-qadi)

1. **Equality** of the parties — in seating, address and attention; no private meeting with one party.
2. **Not to judge** when **angry, hungry, thirsty, sleepy, ill** or distracted.
3. **No gifts** from litigants or those who did not give gifts before appointment; **no bribery** (*rishwah*) — "Allah's curse is on the **giver and the taker** of a bribe in judgment" (Tirmidhi).
4. **Hearing both sides** before deciding.
5. **Public** hearings in the mosque or a public place; **open** justice.
6. **Not to judge** a case involving **himself**, his **children** or his **parents** (*nemo judex in causa sua*).
7. **Consultation** with jurists in difficult cases.
8. **Recording** decisions and evidence (a *katib* — clerk; a *diwan* of records).

## 8.5 Equality before law — famous cases

1. **Hazrat Ali and the Jew (armour case)** — Caliph Ali found his lost armour with a Jew and sued him before **Qazi Shuraih**; he produced his son **Hasan** and his freed slave **Qanbar** as witnesses; Shuraih **rejected the son's testimony** in favour of his father and decided **against the Caliph**. The Jew, moved by such justice, accepted Islam and returned the armour.
2. **Hazrat Umar and Ubayy bin Ka'b** — in a dispute before **Zaid bin Thabit**, Zaid offered the Caliph a seat of honour; Umar said, "This is your first injustice" and sat **beside** his opponent.
3. **Hazrat Umar and the Egyptian Copt** — when the son of **Amr bin al-As** (governor of Egypt) struck a Copt, Umar had the Copt **retaliate** and said, "Since when have you enslaved people whom their mothers bore free?"
4. **The Makhzumi woman** — no intercession in hudood (Chapter 7.3).
5. **The Prophet (PBUH) offering himself for qisas** — before his death he invited anyone he had wronged to take retaliation.

## 8.6 Courts and institutions

{w:20,80}
|**Qada'** (ordinary court)|The **qazi's** court for civil, family and criminal cases|
|**Mazalim** (court of grievances)|Hears **complaints against officials, governors and the State** — the forerunner of **administrative courts** and of **constitutional / writ** jurisdiction; held by the caliph or a senior official with judges and jurists; Hazrat Umar heard such complaints personally; the Abbasids formalised it (al-Mawardi's *al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyyah*)|
|**Hisbah** (the muhtasib)|"Enjoining good and forbidding evil" (3:104) in public life — markets, **weights and measures**, adulteration, public morality, sanitation; summary jurisdiction; Hazrat Umar appointed **Shifa bint Abdullah** (a woman) over the Madinah market|
|**Ifta'** (the mufti)|Gives **legal opinions (fatwa)**, which are **advisory**, unlike the binding **qada'** of the judge|
|**Tahkim** (arbitration)|Disputes referred to an **arbitrator (hakam)** — family disputes (4:35 — one arbiter from each family), Banu Qurayzah, Siffin|
|**Shurtah** (police)|Enforcement; under Hazrat Umar and later the caliphs|

## 8.7 Procedure and evidence in Islamic law

1. **Presumption of innocence** (*al-asl bara'at al-dhimmah*) and **istishab**.
2. **Burden of proof** on the **claimant (mudda'i)**; **oath** (*yamin*) on the **defendant (mudda'a 'alayh)**.
3. **Modes of proof:** **iqrar** (admission / confession — the "chief of evidence"), **shahadah** (testimony), **yamin** (oath), **nukul** (refusal to take oath), **qasamah** (fifty oaths in unexplained homicide), **qarinah** (circumstantial evidence), **'ilm al-qadi** (judge's own knowledge — disputed), **expert opinion**.
4. **Number of witnesses:** **zina** — **four** men (24:4; 4:15); other **hudood and qisas** — **two** men; **financial matters** — **two men, or one man and two women** (2:282); matters known only to women (childbirth, virginity) — the testimony of women alone; **hilal** (moon sighting) — one reliable witness.
5. **Tazkiyah al-shuhood** — the court's inquiry into the **integrity ('adalah)** of witnesses, openly or secretly (KPHC 2026; QSO Art. 17; Hudood Ordinances).
6. **Women as witnesses (KPHC 2026)** — **civil and financial**: two women with one man (2:282 — "so that if one errs, the other reminds her"), explained as a matter of **experience** in commercial affairs, not of inferior status; **criminal**: the majority excluded women in **hudood and qisas**, though some jurists (e.g., Ibn Hazm, Ibn al-Qayyim on qarinah) allowed more; matters **specific to women** — their testimony alone suffices; **lian** — the wife's oath equals the husband's. **In Pakistan:** **QSO Art. 17(2)(a)** — two men or one man and two women for **documents** of financial or future obligations; **Art. 17(2)(b)** — in all other matters the court may act on the testimony of **one man or one woman**; the **Hudood Ordinances** require **male** witnesses for hadd; since **2006** rape is tried under the PPC on **ordinary** evidence, including the victim's own testimony.
7. **Retraction** of confession and **resiling** of witnesses avert hadd.
8. **Appeal and review** — no formal appeal in early Islam, but a judge could **revise** his own decision (Umar's letter), and the **caliph / mazalim** could correct injustice.

# Chapter 9 · Crime and punishment in Islam (KPHC 2026)

1. **Crime (jarimah)** — "a prohibited act, which Allah has deterred by **hadd** or **ta'zir**" (al-Mawardi) — the doing of what is forbidden or the omission of what is commanded, attached to a punishment.
2. **Kinds of punishment:**
   1. **Hudood** — fixed by the Quran / Sunnah as the **right of Allah**: **zina**, **qazf** (80 stripes), **sariqah** (theft — amputation), **hirabah** (highway robbery — 5:33), **shurb al-khamr** (drinking — 80 stripes), **riddah** (apostasy) and **baghy** (rebellion) in classical lists; cannot be pardoned or compounded once reported to the ruler.
   2. **Qisas** — **retaliation in kind** for intentional killing or wounding (2:178–179; 5:45) — the **right of the victim / heirs**, who may **forgive (afw)** or accept **diyat**.
   3. **Diyat** — **blood-money** (100 camels, or 1,000 dinars, or 10,000 dirhams in classical law; in Pakistan not less than **30,630 g of silver** — PPC s. 323); **arsh** — fixed compensation for wounds; **daman** — court-assessed compensation.
   4. **Ta'zir** — **discretionary** punishment for all other offences, fixed by the State / judge (imprisonment, fine, flogging within limits, reprimand).
   5. **Kaffarah** — expiation (fasting, freeing a slave, feeding the poor).
3. **Objects and philosophy of punishment:**
   1. **Deterrence (zajr)** — "a punishment as an example from Allah" (5:38); punishment in public (24:2);
   2. **Retribution and justice** — giving the victim his due; "in qisas there is life for you" (2:179) — preventing blood feuds;
   3. **Reformation and repentance (tawbah)** — hadd falls on repentance before arrest in hirabah (5:34); ta'zir aims at reform;
   4. **Protection of society** and of the five **maqasid** (religion, life, intellect, lineage, property);
   5. **Expiation (kaffarah)** — the punishment purifies the offender from the sin in the Hereafter;
   6. **Compensation** to the victim (diyat, arsh, daman) — a **victim-centred** system;
   7. **Mercy** — strict proof, doubts avert hadd, forgiveness encouraged ("whoever forgives and makes reconciliation, his reward is with Allah" — 42:40).

# Chapter 10 · Islam and society — essay themes

## 10.1 Human rights in Islam

1. **Right to life** (5:32 — killing one person is like killing all mankind); **dignity** (17:70 — "We have honoured the children of Adam").
2. **Equality** (49:13 — "the most honoured of you is the most pious"); **Farewell Sermon**.
3. **Justice** and **fair trial** (4:58; hearing both sides).
4. **Freedom of religion** (2:256 — "there is no compulsion in religion"; 109:6 — "to you your religion, to me mine").
5. **Privacy** (24:27 — enter houses only with permission; 49:12 — no spying).
6. **Property** (2:188; 4:29).
7. **Honour and reputation** (49:11–12 — no mockery, backbiting).
8. **Freedom of expression** within limits; **right to protest** against injustice (the best jihad is a word of truth before a tyrant ruler).
9. **Rights of minorities (dhimmis)** — protection of life, property and worship; "whoever harms a dhimmi, I shall be his adversary on the Day of Judgment".
10. Compare: **UDHR 1948**; **Cairo Declaration on Human Rights in Islam 1990** (OIC); **Constitution of Pakistan**, Chapter 1 of Part II (Arts. 8–28).

## 10.2 Status of women in Islam (LHC 2023)

1. **Spiritual equality** — "whoever does good, male or female, and is a believer, We shall give him a good life" (16:97; 33:35).
2. **Right to life** — infanticide abolished (81:8–9).
3. **Education** — "seeking knowledge is obligatory on every Muslim" (Ibn Majah); Hazrat **A'ishah** — a leading scholar and narrator.
4. **Marriage** — **consent** is required; **mahr** is her property (4:4); **maintenance** is the husband's duty (4:34); right to **khula**.
5. **Inheritance** — a fixed share (4:7, 11) — at a time when women were themselves inherited.
6. **Economic rights** — own, earn, trade (Hazrat **Khadijah**, a merchant); manage property independently.
7. **Political and social role** — the Bay'ah of women (60:12); **Shifa bint Abdullah** as market inspector; women in battles (nursing — Umm Ammarah at Uhud).
8. **Dignity and protection** — hijab and modesty for both sexes (24:30–31); qazf punishes false accusation.
9. **Modern society contrast** — Western equality movements (suffrage 20th century) vs Islam's grant of rights in the 7th century; but also commodification of women, family breakdown; in Muslim societies, **customs** (denial of inheritance, forced marriage, honour killing, watta-satta, marriage with the Quran) violate Islamic teaching — Pakistan's laws against them (PPC ss. 310A, 498A–498C; Enforcement of Women's Property Rights Act 2020).

## 10.3 The Islamic concept of peace (LHC 2023)

1. **Islam** itself derives from **salam** (peace); **As-Salam** is a name of Allah; the greeting is **as-salamu alaykum**; Paradise is **Dar al-Salam** (10:25).
2. **Inner peace** — "in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest" (13:28).
3. **Social peace** — justice, brotherhood (49:10), reconciliation (49:9 — make peace between two quarrelling groups of believers), prohibition of **fasad** (corruption on earth — 2:205; 5:33).
4. **International peace** — "if they incline to peace, incline to it" (8:61); **treaties** honoured (5:1; 16:91); **Hudaibiyyah** chosen over war; **jihad** regulated — no aggression (2:190), protection of non-combatants, women, children, the old, monks, trees (Abu Bakr's instructions to Usamah's army).
5. **Religious tolerance** — 2:256; 109:6; the **Charter of Madinah**; the **Covenant of Umar** with the people of Jerusalem.
6. **Peace-making institutions** — arbitration (4:35), sulh, the prohibition of terrorism: "whoever kills a soul … it is as if he had slain mankind entirely" (5:32); *Paigham-e-Pakistan* (2018) — a national narrative of 1,800 scholars against terrorism and suicide attacks.

## 10.4 Governance and the Islamic state

1. **Sovereignty** belongs to **Allah** (Objectives Resolution); the ruler is His **vicegerent (khalifah)** and a **trustee (amanah)** of the people.
2. **Shura** (consultation) — "their affairs are by mutual consultation" (42:38); "consult them in affairs" (3:159).
3. **Accountability** — Abu Bakr's first address: "Obey me as long as I obey Allah … if I do wrong, set me right"; a man questioned Umar about his cloak.
4. **Rule of law, justice, equality, welfare** — the **bait-ul-mal**, stipends, care of the poor, the **Diwan**.
5. **Rights of non-Muslims** — protection in return for **jizyah** (exemption from military service).
6. **Welfare state** — Umar's statement: "if a dog dies of hunger on the bank of the Euphrates, Umar will be answerable".

## 10.5 The economic system

1. **Ownership** belongs to Allah; humans are trustees (57:7).
2. **Prohibition of riba** (2:275–279), **gharar** (excessive uncertainty), **maysir** (gambling), **hoarding** (ihtikar), and **israf** (waste).
3. **Zakat, ushr, sadaqah**, **inheritance** — circulation of wealth "so that it does not circulate only among the rich" (59:7).
4. **Islamic finance** — **mudarabah**, **musharakah**, **murabahah**, **ijarah**, **salam**, **istisna'**, **sukuk**; **Federal Shariat Court** judgment of **28 April 2022** declared interest-based laws **un-Islamic** and gave the Government until **31 December 2027** to eliminate riba; the **26th Constitutional Amendment (2024)** added to **Art. 38(f)** a commitment to eliminate riba **by 1 January 2028**.
