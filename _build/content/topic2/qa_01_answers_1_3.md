# Answer 1 · Ingredients for the grant of a temporary injunction (SHC 2022)

:::glance SINDH HIGH COURT 2022 · CIVIL LAW · Q. 1 (with Q. 4 short note) · 20 MARKS
Discuss the ingredients for the grant of a temporary injunction under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC. *(Short note, Q. 4: "Cases in which injunction cannot be granted".)*
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the model answer below](b1_map.png)

## Decoding the question

"Ingredients" — the examiner wants the **three conditions** (prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable loss), each explained with authority, **plus** the statutory situations in rr. 1 and 2, the **equitable** nature of the relief, the **procedure** (notice, ex parte limits) and the **cases where it cannot be granted** (s. 56 Specific Relief Act). An Islamabad candidate should add the Pakistani rules in O. XXXIX rr. 2A, 2B, 3 and 4A.

## The model answer

### 1. Introduction — preserving the subject matter

A **temporary injunction** is an interlocutory order restraining a party from doing an act (or, rarely, directing him to do one) until the suit is decided or until further orders. Its purpose is to **preserve the subject matter** and the **status quo**, so that the final decree is not rendered useless. It is regulated by the CPC (s. 53, Specific Relief Act 1877; s. 94(c) and O. XXXIX CPC).

### 2. Statutory situations — rule 1

#### i) Property in danger

Where "any property in dispute in a suit is in danger of being **wasted, damaged or alienated** by any party to the suit, or **wrongfully sold** in execution of a decree" (r. 1(a)).

#### ii) Fraud on creditors

Where "the defendant threatens, or intends, to **remove or dispose of his property with a view to defraud his creditors**" (r. 1(b)).

### 3. Statutory situations — rule 2

In any suit "for restraining the defendant from committing a **breach of contract or other injury** of any kind, whether compensation is claimed in the suit or not", the plaintiff may apply "at any time after the commencement of the suit, and either before or after judgment", and the court may grant the injunction "on such terms as to the duration …, keeping an account, giving security or otherwise" (r. 2(1)–(2)).

### 4. Ingredient 1 — prima facie case

The plaintiff must show a **serious question to be tried** — a bona fide claim with a reasonable chance of success on the material on record. He need **not prove** his title at this stage; the court makes a tentative assessment (*Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh*, (1992) 1 SCC 719). In *American Cyanamid Co. v. Ethicon Ltd.* ([1975] AC 396) Lord Diplock held that it is enough that the claim is "not frivolous or vexatious" and raises a serious question to be tried.

### 5. Ingredient 2 — balance of convenience

The court weighs the **comparative hardship**: which party would suffer greater inconvenience if the injunction were granted or refused. If the risks are evenly balanced, the court preserves the **status quo** (*American Cyanamid*).

### 6. Ingredient 3 — irreparable loss

The injury must be such that it **cannot be adequately compensated in money** — e.g., sale of unique immovable property to a third party, demolition of a building, loss of goodwill. If damages would be an adequate remedy and the defendant can pay them, an injunction is usually refused.

### 7. All three must co-exist

The three ingredients are **cumulative**; the absence of any one is fatal. Even when all three exist, the relief remains **discretionary**.

![The three ingredients of a temporary injunction](injunction_triad.png)

### 8. Equitable considerations

#### i) Clean hands and full disclosure

"He who seeks equity must do equity" and must "come with clean hands". **Suppression of material facts** or **delay** (laches) disentitles the applicant (*Morgan Stanley Mutual Fund v. Kartick Das*, (1994) 4 SCC 225).

#### ii) Mandatory interim injunctions

Granted only in **rare cases** on a **strong** prima facie case (*Dorab Cawasji Warden v. Coomi Sorab Warden*, (1990) 2 SCC 117).

### 9. Procedure — the Pakistani rules

1. **Notice** to the opposite party "in all cases" (r. 3); may be dispensed with only where delay would defeat the object — **not** in bank-pledge sales or against **Government / statutory bodies** (except ejectment or demolition); notice to them 2–7 days.
2. **Ex parte** injunction "shall not ordinarily exceed **fifteen days**" (r. 2A).
3. An injunction after hearing **lapses after six months** unless extended with recorded reasons; report to the High Court (r. 2B).
4. Injunctions concerning **public revenue** lapse after six months (r. 4A).
5. **Disobedience:** attachment of property (up to one year) and civil prison up to **six months** (r. 2(3)–(4)).
6. **Appeal:** O. XLIII r. 1(r); compensation for injunctions obtained on insufficient grounds — s. 95.

### 10. Cases in which an injunction cannot be granted — s. 56 SRA

An injunction cannot be granted: (a) to stay a judicial proceeding pending when the suit was filed (unless to prevent multiplicity); (b) to stay proceedings in a court not subordinate; (c) to restrain applications to a legislative body; (d) to interfere with the **public duties** of any department of Government or sovereign acts of a foreign government; (e) to stay **criminal** proceedings; (f) to prevent breach of a contract that would **not be specifically enforced**; (g) on the ground of nuisance where it is not reasonably clear that the act will be a nuisance; (h) to prevent a continuing breach in which the applicant **acquiesced**; (i) where **equally efficacious relief** is available by another usual mode (except breach of trust); (j) where the applicant's **conduct** disentitles him; (k) where he has **no personal interest**.

### 11. Illustration

Zubair sues for specific performance of an agreement to sell a plot in D-12 and seeks to restrain the seller from selling it to a third party. The signed agreement and admitted earnest money show a **prima facie case**; sale to a third party would multiply litigation and defeat the decree (**balance of convenience**); the plot is unique (**irreparable loss**). The court grants the injunction, with notice to the seller, for six months (r. 2B), on Zubair's undertaking to compensate if he fails.

### 12. Conclusion

A temporary injunction rests on three pillars — **prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable loss** — applied with equitable discretion and within the procedural limits of O. XXXIX (notice, 15-day ex parte limit, six-month life) and the bars of s. 56 of the Specific Relief Act. Its aim is not to decide the case but to keep the field level until the court can.

# Answer 2 · Write the order of temporary injunction — public servant and official residence (LHC 2017)

:::glance LHC CIVIL JUDGE EXAM 2017 · CIVIL LAW-II · Q. 2 · 20 MARKS
Akram being a public servant performing his function in the establishment was allotted an official residence. After six months he was transferred to a field job and was served upon with a notice to vacate the house but his children had to appear in the examination to be held after two weeks. However, under the transfer policy, he was entitled to retain the house for three years. Along with the plaint, an application for a grant of a temporary injunction was also filed. **Write the order of a temporary injunction.**
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the order below](b2_map.png)

## Decoding the question

The examiner wants a **real court order**, not an essay. It must (1) be in proper format; (2) state the facts and the rival contentions; (3) apply the **three ingredients**; (4) deal with features special to **suits against Government**: the forum (s. 24 Civil Courts Ordinance), **notice** (O. XXXIX r. 3 — here the case involves **ejectment**, so notice may be dispensed with if urgent), **s. 80 CPC** (not a bar in Pakistan), and the bar of **s. 56(d) SRA** (public duties); and (5) end with a precise operative part, including duration (O. XXXIX r. 2B). The reasoning must be **tentative**.

## The model answer

:::order IN THE COURT OF SENIOR CIVIL JUDGE (UNLIMITED PECUNIARY JURISDICTION), ISLAMABAD (WEST)
Civil Suit No. ___ of 2026 · Muhammad Akram v. Federation of Pakistan through Secretary, Ministry of Housing and Works, and 2 others · Suit for declaration and permanent injunction

__ORDER__ (on the application under Order XXXIX rules 1 and 2 read with section 151 CPC)

1. **The application.** The plaintiff, a public servant, seeks a temporary injunction restraining the defendants from ejecting him from the official residence allotted to him (House No. ___, Sector G-6/2, Islamabad) during the pendency of the suit.

2. **Facts in brief.** The plaintiff was allotted the official residence while posted at the establishment. Six months later he was transferred to a field posting and served with a notice to vacate. He pleads that under the Government's **transfer policy** an officer transferred to a field job is **entitled to retain** his residence for **three years**, and that his children are to appear in their **examinations within two weeks**. He prays for a declaration that the notice is illegal and for a permanent injunction.

3. **Forum and preliminary matters.** The suit is against the Federal Government and its officers in their official capacity; it has rightly been instituted in the court of a Civil Judge having **unlimited pecuniary jurisdiction** at district headquarters (s. 24, West Pakistan Civil Courts Ordinance 1962). The want of a **notice under s. 80 CPC** does not bar the suit in Pakistan; its only consequence is on costs and the time for the written statement (s. 80(2) CPC).

4. **Notice of the application.** Under O. XXXIX r. 3 CPC, notice of an injunction application must ordinarily be given, and against Government it cannot be dispensed with **except** in a case "involving the ejectment of any person from … premises". This case involves ejectment; the examinations are imminent and ejectment could be effected before the defendants are heard. Notice was nevertheless issued for a short date, and the Government pleader has appeared and filed a reply. Arguments of both sides have been heard.

5. **Reply of the defendants.** The defendants contend that allotment of official accommodation is at the discretion of the competent authority; on transfer the plaintiff lost his entitlement; another officer awaits allotment; and the court cannot interfere with the public duties of Government (s. 56(d), Specific Relief Act 1877).

6. **The law.** A temporary injunction may be granted where the applicant shows (i) a **prima facie case**, (ii) that the **balance of convenience** is in his favour, and (iii) that he would suffer **irreparable loss** if it were refused (*American Cyanamid Co. v. Ethicon Ltd.*, [1975] AC 396; *Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh*, (1992) 1 SCC 719). The findings are tentative.

7. **Prima facie case.** The defendants do not deny the existence of the transfer policy; they do not show that the policy has been withdrawn or that the plaintiff falls within any exception to it. If, as pleaded, the policy entitles a transferred officer to retain his residence for three years, the notice to vacate after six months is, prima facie, contrary to the defendants' **own policy**. A public authority is bound to act in accordance with its policy and cannot act arbitrarily. The plaintiff has raised a serious question to be tried; a **prima facie case** exists.

8. **Section 56(d) SRA.** That provision bars injunctions that "interfere with the public duties" of a Government department. Requiring a department to **follow its own policy** in allotment of accommodation is not interference with a public duty; the bar does not apply on the present tentative view.

9. **Balance of convenience.** If the injunction is refused, the plaintiff and his family will be ejected days before the children's examinations, and the suit may become infructuous. If it is granted, the department is only delayed in re-allotting one house, while the plaintiff continues to pay the prescribed rent and charges. The balance of convenience is clearly in favour of the plaintiff.

10. **Irreparable loss.** Ejectment of a family, with school-going children on the eve of examinations, and the consequent disruption of their education, cannot be compensated in money. The plaintiff would suffer **irreparable loss**.

11. **Conclusion.** The application is **accepted**. The defendants are **restrained from ejecting the plaintiff** from the official residence, otherwise than in accordance with law, till the final disposal of the suit, subject to the conditions that (a) the plaintiff shall continue to pay the rent, charges and deductions applicable under the rules; (b) he shall not create any third-party interest or make structural changes; and (c) he shall file an **undertaking** to vacate within thirty days if the suit is dismissed. Under O. XXXIX r. 2B CPC this order shall cease to have effect after **six months** unless extended after hearing the parties. The observations above are tentative and shall not prejudice the trial on merits.

12. The defendants to file written statement within the time allowed under s. 80(2) / O. VIII r. 1 CPC. To come up on ___ for written statement.

Announced in open court · Dated ___ · Senior Civil Judge, Islamabad (West) (seal)
:::

### Alternative outcome the examiner may expect

If the policy did **not** give a right of retention (e.g., it only allowed a short grace period at the department's discretion), there would be **no prima facie case** of a legal right; the court could at most grant **limited relief** — e.g., restrain ejectment **until the children's examinations are over** (about two weeks) on humanitarian grounds and the balance of convenience — and then dismiss the application. Mention this in one paragraph to show judicial balance.

## Why this answer scores

- It is written **as an order**, with heading, title, numbered paragraphs and an operative part — exactly the IHC syllabus skill.
- It handles the **Government angle** precisely: forum (s. 24 Ordinance), s. 80 (Pakistani rule), notice exception for **ejectment** (O. XXXIX r. 3), and **s. 56(d) SRA**.
- Each of the **three ingredients** is applied to the facts (policy, examinations, hardship).
- The **conditions** in the operative part (rent, undertaking, six-month life under r. 2B) show practical judicial sense.

# Answer 3 · Scope of review, revision, appeal and second appeal (LHC 2023)

:::glance LHC CIVIL JUDGE EXAM 2023 · CIVIL LAW-II · Q. 9 · 20 MARKS
Explain the scope of review, revision, appeal and second appeal, with illustrations. Highlight the differences amongst each of the remedies mentioned above in the context of relevant provisions of the Civil Procedure Code 1908.
:::

## The answer map

![The answer map: every branch is one numbered heading of the model answer below](b3_map.png)

## Decoding the question

Four remedies, three tasks: **scope** of each (provision, grounds, forum, limitation), **illustrations**, and **differences** (a table). Use the **Islamabad text** of ss. 96, 100, 114 and 115, and finish with how to choose the right remedy.

## The model answer

### 1. Introduction — four ways to challenge a decision

The CPC gives an aggrieved party four main remedies against a civil court's decision: **appeal** (ss. 96–108), **second appeal** (ss. 100–103), **review** (s. 114, O. XLVII) and **revision** (s. 115). They differ in **forum, grounds, depth of scrutiny and limitation**. A right of appeal is substantive and vests on the date of the suit (*Garikapati Veeraya v. N. Subbiah Choudhry*, AIR 1957 SC 540).

### 2. First appeal — scope

#### i) Provision

"An appeal shall lie from every decree passed by any Court exercising original jurisdiction to the Court authorized to hear appeal from the decisions of such Court" (s. 96(1)); an ex parte decree is appealable (s. 96(2)); **no appeal** lies from a **consent decree** (s. 96(3), Islamabad text).

#### ii) Scope

A first appeal is a **continuation of the suit** — a full rehearing on **facts and law**. The appellate court may decide finally, remand, frame issues, or take additional evidence (s. 107; O. XLI rr. 23–29), and must give findings with reasons on all points (*Santosh Hazari v. Purushottam Tiwari*, (2001) 3 SCC 179).

#### iii) Forum and time

Civil Judge → District Judge (s. 18(1) Ordinance, 2025) in **30 days** (Art. 152); DJ / ADJ original → High Court (s. 17) in **90 days** (Art. 156).

#### iv) Illustration

A Civil Judge dismisses Sana's suit for recovery of Rs. 1.2 million, disbelieving her witnesses. She appeals to the District Judge, who re-appraises the evidence and decrees the suit.

### 3. Second appeal — scope

#### i) Provision

An appeal lies to the High Court from every **appellate decree** on three grounds only: (a) decision **contrary to law** or usage having the force of law; (b) **failure to determine a material issue of law**; (c) **substantial error or defect in procedure** which may have produced error on the merits (s. 100). "No second appeal shall lie except on the grounds mentioned in section 100" (s. 101).

#### ii) Scope

**No re-appraisal of facts**; findings of fact are final unless based on no evidence, misreading or non-reading of evidence, or inadmissible evidence (errors of law). The High Court may decide issues of fact in the s. 100 situations (s. 103). Value limit as prescribed (s. 102). **90 days**.

#### iii) Illustration

The District Judge, in appeal, decrees possession by ignoring a registered sale deed proved on record. That is **non-reading of evidence** — a ground under s. 100(a); a second appeal lies.

### 4. Review — scope

#### i) Provision

Any person aggrieved by a decree or order from which an appeal is allowed but **not preferred**, or from which **no appeal** is allowed, may apply for review "to the Court which passed the decree or made the order" (s. 114).

#### ii) Grounds

(1) **New and important matter or evidence** not available despite due diligence; (2) **mistake or error apparent on the face of the record**; (3) **any other sufficient reason** — analogous to the first two (O. XLVII r. 1; *Moran Mar Basselios Catholicos v. Mar Poulose Athanasius*, AIR 1954 SC 526).

#### iii) Scope

Review is **not an appeal in disguise**; the error must be **self-evident**, not one requiring a long process of reasoning (*Parsion Devi v. Sumitri Devi*, (1997) 8 SCC 715). Same judge (r. 2, r. 5); notice before grant (r. 4); rejection not appealable (r. 7); **no review of review** (r. 9). **90 days** (Art. 173).

#### iv) Illustration

A Civil Judge dismisses a suit as time-barred, overlooking that the plaint pleads acknowledgment under s. 19 Limitation Act supported by an exhibited letter. The error is apparent on the record — review lies.

### 5. Revision — scope

#### i) Provision (Islamabad text, 2023)

The High Court may call for the record of "any case which has been decided by any Court subordinate … and **in which no appeal lies** thereto", and interfere if the court (a) **exercised a jurisdiction not vested** in it, (b) **failed to exercise a jurisdiction** so vested, or (c) acted **illegally or with material irregularity** (s. 115(1)).

#### ii) Scope

**Supervisory** and **discretionary** — limited to jurisdictional errors (*Keshardeo Chamria v. Radha Kissen Chamria*, AIR 1953 SC 23); "case decided" includes interlocutory orders (*Major S.S. Khanna v. Brig. F.J. Dillon*, AIR 1964 SC 497). Islamabad provisos: copies of pleadings and orders; decision without calling for the record unless reasons recorded; filed **within 90 days**; decided **within six months**. The **District Court** has the same power within its appellate limits (s. 115(2)); **only one revision** to either court (s. 115(3)); the High Court cannot revise the District Court's revisional order (s. 115(4)).

#### iii) Illustration

A Civil Judge refuses to return a plaint for a partition suit of land in another district. No appeal lies (only an order *returning* a plaint is appealable — O. XLIII r. 1(a)); the court exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it — **revision** lies.

### 7. Choosing the remedy

![Choosing the right remedy against a civil court's decision](remedy_chooser.png)

### 8. Can remedies be combined?

A party cannot pursue **review and appeal** on the same ground simultaneously — review lies only where no appeal has been preferred (s. 114(a)), and once an appeal is decided on merits, the decree merges in the appellate decree. Revision lies only where **no appeal** lies; it is not an alternative to an available appeal. Errors in non-appealable interlocutory orders may also be raised in the appeal from the final decree (s. 105).

### 9. Conclusion

**Appeal** corrects any error by a full rehearing; **second appeal** corrects errors of **law** in appellate decrees; **review** lets the same court correct a **patent** error or consider genuinely new evidence; **revision** lets the superior court correct **jurisdictional** errors where no appeal lies. Choosing the correct remedy — and filing it within time — is as important as having a good case.
