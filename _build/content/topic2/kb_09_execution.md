# Chapter 9 · Execution I: courts, limitation, parties and s. 47 (ss. 36 to 50; Order XXI rr. 1 to 29)

## 9.1 What execution is

A decree is only a piece of paper until it is **enforced**. **Execution** is the process by which the court **gives the decree-holder the fruits of his decree** — money, possession, the act ordered — through the machinery of the court. The Privy Council's famous remark that "the difficulties of a litigant in India begin when he has obtained a decree" (*General Manager of the Raj Durbhunga v. Coomar Ramaput Sing*, 1872) is still true in Pakistan; examiners therefore love execution questions.

![Execution of a decree — the main roads](execution_map.png)

## 9.2 Which court executes — ss. 36 to 46

- **s. 36:** provisions on execution of decrees apply to **orders** capable of execution.
- **s. 37:** "the Court which passed a decree" includes (a) the **court of first instance** where the decree was passed in **appeal**, and (b) where that court has ceased to exist or lost jurisdiction, the court that would now have jurisdiction to try the suit.
- **s. 38:** a decree may be executed by the court **which passed it** or by the court to which it is **sent** for execution.
- **s. 39 — transfer:** on the decree-holder's application, the decree may be sent to another court if the judgment-debtor resides / works there, has property there (and insufficient property within the first court), the decree directs sale or delivery of immovable property there, or for any other recorded reason; the court may also send it **of its own motion** to a subordinate court.
- **s. 40:** transfer to another province — according to that province's rules.
- **s. 41:** the transferee court **certifies** the result of execution to the decree court.
- **s. 42:** the transferee court has the **same powers** as if it had passed the decree, including (s. 42(2)) further transfer, execution against legal representatives (s. 50), correction of clerical errors (s. 152), recognising assignment (O. XXI r. 16), etc.
- **ss. 43–45:** decrees of courts outside the area; **s. 44A** — execution of decrees of superior courts of **reciprocating territories** (notified by the Federal Government) as if passed by a district court here, on filing a certified copy; the foreign decree is subject to the exceptions of **s. 13**.
- **s. 46 — precept:** the decree court may issue a **precept** to another court to **attach** property, valid for **two months** unless extended (Topic 1, Chapter 7).

:::trap The executing court cannot go behind the decree
The executing court must take the decree **as it stands**. It cannot question its correctness, add to it or vary it — even if it thinks the decree is wrong in law or fact. The only exception is where the decree is a **nullity** (passed without inherent jurisdiction).
:::

:::case Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi v. Rajabhai Abdul Rehman (AIR 1970 SC 1475)
__Held.__ A court executing a decree **cannot go behind the decree**: it must take the decree according to its tenor and cannot entertain any objection that the decree was incorrect in law or on facts. Only when the decree is a **nullity** — passed by a court lacking **inherent jurisdiction** — can its invalidity be set up in execution. An erroneous decision, even on a question of jurisdiction which depended on facts, remains binding until set aside in appeal or revision.

__Ratio.__ Execution proceedings are not a substitute for appeal; nullity is the only ground on which an executing court can refuse to execute.
:::

## 9.3 Questions to be decided by the executing court — s. 47

> "47. (1) All questions arising between the parties to the suit in which the decree was passed, or their representatives, and relating to the execution, discharge or satisfaction of the decree, shall be determined by the Court executing the decree and not by a separate suit. (2) The Court may, subject to any objection as to limitation or jurisdiction, treat a proceeding under this section as a suit or a suit as a proceeding and may, if necessary, order payment of any additional court-fees. (3) Where a question arises as to whether any person is or is not the representative of a party, such question shall … be determined by the Court.
> Explanation.— For the purposes of this section, a plaintiff whose suit has been dismissed and a defendant against whom a suit has been dismissed, are parties to the suit."

### Three conditions

1. The question arises **between the parties** to the suit (or their **representatives** — including transferees and auction-purchasers who are decree-holders' representatives);
2. it **relates to the execution, discharge or satisfaction** of the decree; and
3. then it **must** be decided by the **executing court** — a **separate suit is barred**.

**Examples:** whether the decree has been satisfied by payment out of court; whether the decree is executable; whether property is liable to attachment as the judgment-debtor's; whether execution is time-barred; whether a person is the legal representative of a deceased party.

**Appeal:** an order under s. 47 is **appealable as an order** under **s. 104(1)(ff)** (in Pakistan it is no longer a "decree" — Topic 1, Chapter 2).

:::why Why s. 47?
To provide a **cheap and speedy** decision of disputes about execution in the same proceedings, and to prevent the judgment-debtor from starting a fresh round of litigation (suit, appeal, second appeal) to delay the decree-holder.
:::

## 9.4 Limitation for execution — s. 48 and Art. 181

:::ict The Pakistani scheme
- **s. 48(1):** where an application to execute a decree (other than a decree granting an injunction) has been made, no order for execution shall be made upon any **fresh** application presented after the expiry of **six years** from (a) the date of the decree, or (b) where payment or delivery is directed at a certain date or at recurring periods, the date of the default.
- **s. 48(2):** the six-year limit does not apply where the judgment-debtor, by **fraud or force**, prevented execution within the six years before the application; nor does it affect **Art. 183** (decrees of a High Court in its ordinary original civil jurisdiction — six years).
- **Article 182** of the Limitation Act (the old detailed article for execution applications) was **omitted** by the **Law Reforms Ordinance 1972**. Execution applications are now governed by **Article 181** — **three years** "when the right to apply accrues" — within the overall six-year ceiling of s. 48 for fresh applications.
:::

In practice: the **first** application must be made within **three years** of the decree (when the right to apply accrues); **subsequent** applications must stay within the **six-year** outer limit of s. 48 (counted from the decree or default), unless fraud or force by the judgment-debtor prevented execution.

## 9.5 Transferees and legal representatives — ss. 49 to 53

- **s. 49:** a **transferee of a decree** holds it subject to the **equities** the judgment-debtor could have enforced against the original decree-holder (e.g., a set-off or a payment already made).
- **s. 50:** if the **judgment-debtor dies** before full satisfaction, the decree may be executed against his **legal representative**, who is liable **only to the extent of the deceased's property** that came into his hands and has not been duly disposed of; the court may compel him to produce accounts.
- **s. 52:** a decree against a party **as legal representative** for money out of the deceased's property is executed against that property; if he fails to account for the property that came to him, he becomes **personally** liable to that extent.
- **O. XXI r. 16:** a **transferee** (assignee) of a decree may apply for execution; notice is given to the transferor and judgment-debtor. **r. 15:** a **joint decree-holder** may apply for execution of the whole decree for the benefit of all.

## 9.6 Applying for execution — O. XXI rr. 10 to 25

1. **r. 10:** the decree-holder applies to the court which passed the decree (or to the transferee court).
2. **r. 11:** for a money decree, the court may order **oral application** at the time of the decree for immediate arrest (if the judgment-debtor is in court); otherwise a **written application** in tabular form stating the suit number, parties, date of decree, appeals, payments, amount due, costs, the **mode** of execution sought (arrest, attachment of property, etc.), and signed and verified.
3. **rr. 12–14:** applications for attachment of movable property not in the judgment-debtor's possession, or of **immovable property** (with description and the judgment-debtor's share), with revenue-record extracts where needed.
4. **r. 17:** the court checks compliance; if defective, it may reject or allow amendment; if in order, **admits** and registers it.
5. **r. 22 — notice to show cause:** required where the application is made (a) **more than one year after the decree**, (b) against the **legal representative** of a party, or (c) for execution of a decree under **s. 44A**; the court may dispense with notice for reasons recorded if it would cause unreasonable delay or defeat justice (r. 22(2)).
6. **r. 23:** if no cause is shown, execution is ordered; objections are decided.
7. **rr. 24–25:** **process** for execution issues with a date for its return; the executing officer endorses the manner of execution.

:::ict O. XXI r. 23-A — no objection without deposit (Law Reforms Ordinance 1972)
> "23-A. An objection by the judgment-debtor to the execution of a decree shall not be considered by the Court unless— (a) in the case of a decree for the payment of money, he either **deposits the decretal amount** in Court or **furnishes security** for its payment; and (b) in the case of any other decree, he furnishes security for the due performance of the decree."

This rule stops judgment-debtors from filing frivolous objections merely to delay execution. It applies to the **judgment-debtor's** objections — not to objections by third parties (claimants) under r. 58 or r. 97–100.
:::

## 9.7 Payment and adjustment — O. XXI rr. 1 and 2

- **r. 1:** money under a decree may be paid **into the executing court**, **out of court** to the decree-holder (by postal money order, bank or as the court directs), or as the court directs.
- **r. 2:** where money is paid **out of court** or the decree is otherwise **adjusted** (compromise), the decree-holder must **certify** it to the court; the judgment-debtor may also inform the court and apply for notice to the decree-holder (limitation: **90 days** from the payment / adjustment — Art. 174). An **uncertified** payment or adjustment "shall not be recognised by any Court executing the decree".

## 9.8 Stay of execution — O. XXI rr. 26 to 29; O. XLI r. 5

- **r. 26:** a **transferee court** may stay execution for a reasonable time, on sufficient cause, to enable the judgment-debtor to approach the decree court or appellate court; it may restore seized property or discharge an arrested judgment-debtor pending the result.
- **r. 29:** where a **suit is pending** by the judgment-debtor against the decree-holder in the same court, execution may be stayed on terms (security) until that suit is decided.
- **O. XLI r. 5:** an **appeal does not operate as a stay**; the appellate court (or the trial court before the time for appeal expires) may stay execution for sufficient cause, if satisfied that (a) **substantial loss** may result, (b) the application is **without unreasonable delay**, and (c) **security** has been given (Chapter 11).

:::recap Chapter 9 in seven lines
- Execution by the court which passed the decree (s. 37 includes first-instance court) or the transferee court (ss. 38–42); s. 44A reciprocating territories; precept s. 46.
- Executing court **cannot go behind the decree** except for nullity (*Vasudev Dhanjibhai Modi*).
- **s. 47:** questions between parties relating to execution, discharge, satisfaction → executing court only; appealable under s. 104(1)(ff).
- **Limitation:** Art. 182 omitted (1972); **Art. 181 — three years**; **s. 48 — six-year** ceiling for fresh applications (fraud / force excepted).
- Transferee subject to equities (s. 49); legal representative liable to extent of estate (ss. 50, 52).
- Application in tabular form (r. 11); notice after one year / against legal representative / s. 44A (r. 22); **r. 23-A deposit before objection** (Pakistan).
- Out-of-court payment must be certified (r. 2; Art. 174 — 90 days); stay by transferee court (r. 26), pending suit (r. 29), appeal (O. XLI r. 5).
:::

# Chapter 10 · Execution II: modes of execution, attachment, sale and possession (ss. 51 to 74; Order XXI rr. 30 to 103)

## 10.1 The modes of execution — s. 51

> "51. Subject to such conditions and limitations as may be prescribed, the Court may, on the application of the decree-holder, order execution of the decree— (a) by delivery of any property specifically decreed; (b) by attachment and sale or by sale without attachment of any property; (c) by arrest and detention in prison; (d) by appointing a receiver; or (e) in such other manner as the nature of the relief granted may require."

The **decree-holder chooses** the mode (subject to the rules); he may apply for several modes simultaneously, though the court may refuse simultaneous execution against person and property (O. XXI r. 21).

### Modes according to the kind of decree — O. XXI rr. 30 to 36

|Decree for|Mode of execution|
|**Payment of money** (r. 30)|Detention in civil prison of the judgment-debtor, or attachment and sale of his property, or both|
|**Specific movable property** (r. 31)|Seizure and delivery to the decree-holder; or detention / attachment to compel delivery; damages if not delivered|
|**Specific performance, restitution of conjugal rights, injunction** (r. 32)|Detention in civil prison and / or attachment of property of the person who wilfully fails to obey; after attachment for a period, sale and compensation; for a mandatory injunction or specific performance, the court may get the act done by another person at the judgment-debtor's cost|
|**Execution of a document or endorsement of a negotiable instrument** (r. 34)|The court itself **executes the document** (through an officer) on the judgment-debtor's behalf after giving him notice of the draft|
|**Immovable property** (r. 35)|Delivery of possession — removing any person bound by the decree who refuses to vacate|
|**Immovable property in occupancy of a tenant** (r. 36)|**Symbolic** possession — affixing a copy of the warrant and proclaiming by beat of drum that the decree-holder's interest has been transferred|

## 10.2 Arrest and detention — s. 51 proviso, ss. 55 to 58, O. XXI rr. 37 to 40

### The safeguard in s. 51

Detention in prison for a money decree **shall not be ordered** unless, after giving the judgment-debtor an **opportunity to show cause**, the court, **for reasons recorded in writing**, is satisfied that:

- **(a)** he, with the object or effect of obstructing or delaying execution, (i) is likely to **abscond** or leave the jurisdiction, or (ii) has, after the suit, **dishonestly transferred, concealed or removed** his property or committed other acts of bad faith; or
- **(b)** he **has or has had since the decree the means to pay** the decree (or a substantial part) and **refuses or neglects** to pay; or
- **(c)** the decree is for a sum he was bound in a **fiduciary capacity** to account for.

In computing "means", property exempt from attachment is left out.

:::case Jolly George Varghese v. Bank of Cochin ((1980) 2 SCC 360)
__Held.__ Krishna Iyer J. held that to cast a person in prison merely because he has not paid a decretal debt, without proof that he **has the present means to pay and wilfully refuses**, would violate personal liberty (Art. 21 of the Indian Constitution) and Art. 11 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights ("No one shall be imprisoned merely on the ground of inability to fulfil a contractual obligation"). The proviso to s. 51 must be read in that light: **simple default is not enough; there must be an element of bad faith** — wilful refusal despite means.

__Ratio.__ Civil imprisonment is a coercive measure against the dishonest debtor, not a punishment for poverty.
:::

### Procedure and limits

- **O. XXI r. 37:** for a money decree, the court **shall** first issue a **notice** to the judgment-debtor to show cause why he should not be detained, instead of a warrant — unless satisfied (by affidavit) that he is likely to **abscond** to delay execution.
- **r. 40:** on appearance or arrest, the court hears the decree-holder's evidence and the judgment-debtor's cause; it may give him up to **fifteen days** in custody of a court officer (or on security) to satisfy the decree; it may then order detention or disallow the application.
- **s. 55(1):** a judgment-debtor may be arrested at any hour and day, and brought before the court, "which may make an order for his detention in prison to suffer simple imprisonment for a period **not exceeding one year**". No dwelling-house may be entered **between sunset and sunrise**; the outer door may be broken only if the house is in the judgment-debtor's occupancy and he prevents access; a **pardanashin woman** (not the judgment-debtor) must be given notice and facility to withdraw; on payment of the decretal amount and costs to the arresting officer, he is released at once.
- **s. 55(3):** detention does **not discharge** the debt, but he cannot be re-arrested under the same decree.
- **s. 56:** **no woman** shall be arrested or detained in execution of a **money decree**.
- **s. 57** (subsistence allowance) and **s. 59** (release on illness) were **omitted** by Ordinance X of 1980.
- **s. 58:** release before expiry if the amount is paid, the decree is otherwise satisfied, or the decree-holder requests — only by **order of the court**.
- **s. 81 / s. 135:** public officers sued for official acts, and certain persons (judges, parties and witnesses going to and from court, members of legislatures — s. 135A), are exempt from arrest under civil process in specified circumstances.

## 10.3 Attachment of property — ss. 60 to 64, O. XXI rr. 41 to 57

### What can be attached — s. 60(1)

"Lands, houses or other buildings, goods, money, bank-notes, cheques, bills of exchange, hundis, promissory notes, Government securities, bonds or other securities for money, debts, shares in a corporation and … all other saleable property, moveable or immoveable, belonging to the judgment-debtor or over which, or the profits of which, he has a disposing power which he may exercise for his own benefit, whether the same be held in the name of the judgment-debtor or by another person in trust for him or on his behalf."

### What cannot be attached — the main exemptions in the proviso

1. **(a)** necessary wearing apparel, cooking vessels, beds and bedding of the judgment-debtor, his wife and children; personal ornaments a woman cannot part with by religious usage;
2. **(b)** tools of artisans; for an agriculturist, implements of husbandry, cattle and seed-grain necessary for his livelihood;
3. **(c)** houses and buildings (with sites) belonging to an **agriculturist** and occupied by him;
4. **(d)** books of account;
5. **(e)** a **mere right to sue for damages**;
6. **(f)** a right of personal service;
7. **(g)** stipends and gratuities of Government pensioners; **political pensions**;
8. **(h)** wages of labourers and domestic servants;
9. **(i)** **salary** to the extent of the **first hundred rupees and one-half of the remainder** (with a further protection for State servants after 24 months of attachment);
10. **(j)** pay and allowances of persons subject to the **Pakistan Army Act** (and certain Navy personnel);
11. **(k)** compulsory deposits under the **Provident Funds Act 1925**;
12. **(l)** allowances of public servants declared exempt by Government; subsistence grant during suspension;
13. **(m)** an **expectancy of succession** or other merely contingent right;
14. **(n)** a **right to future maintenance**;
15. **(o)** allowances exempt under any Pakistan law;
16. **(p)** movables exempt from sale for land-revenue arrears.

:::why Why exemptions?
Execution should satisfy the creditor without **destroying the debtor's ability to live and earn**. A decree-holder cannot take the tools by which the debtor earns, the clothes he wears, or his whole salary. Exemptions balance the creditor's right with the debtor's dignity and livelihood.
:::

### How attachment is made — O. XXI rr. 43 to 54

- **Movable property in the judgment-debtor's possession** (r. 43) — by **actual seizure**; perishables may be sold at once.
- **Agricultural produce** (rr. 44–45) — by affixing a copy of the warrant on the land / granary.
- **Debts, shares and other property not in his possession** (r. 46) — by a written **prohibitory order** — to the judgment-debtor (as creditor) not to recover the debt, and to his debtor (the "garnishee") not to pay it; for shares, to the person in whose name they stand not to transfer them or receive dividends. The garnishee may **pay into court**, which discharges him (r. 46(3)).
- **Salary of public officers** (r. 48) — by order to the disbursing officer to withhold the attachable portion monthly.
- **Partnership property** (r. 49) — only the partner's **interest** in the partnership, by charging order.
- **Negotiable instruments** (r. 51) — actual seizure; **property in custody of a court or public officer** (r. 52) — notice to that court / officer.
- **Decrees** (r. 53) — attachment of a decree held by the judgment-debtor.
- **Immovable property** (r. 54) — by an **order prohibiting** the judgment-debtor from transferring or charging it and all persons from taking any benefit from such transfer, **proclaimed** at the spot (beat of drum), affixed on the property and in the court-house, and (for revenue-paying land) in the Collector's office.

### Effect of attachment — s. 64

> "64. Where an attachment has been made, any private transfer or delivery of the property attached or of any interest therein and any payment to the judgment-debtor of any debt, dividend or other monies contrary to such attachment, shall be **void as against all claims enforceable under the attachment**."

The transfer is not void **altogether** — it is void only **against the attaching decree-holder** (and those entitled to rateable distribution). If the attachment is lifted (decree satisfied), the transfer becomes fully effective.

- **s. 63:** where property is attached under decrees of several courts, the **court of highest grade** (or, if equal, the court that first attached) receives the property and decides claims.
- **O. XXI r. 55:** attachment is **withdrawn** when the decree is satisfied or set aside; **r. 57:** if the execution application is dismissed, the court states whether the attachment continues or ceases.

## 10.4 Claims and objections to attachment — O. XXI rr. 58 to 63 (the Pakistani scheme)

![Who objects in execution, under which rule — and what follows](objections_map.png)

:::ict Pakistan: claims are fully decided in execution — no separate suit
- **r. 58(1):** where any **claim** is preferred to, or **objection** made to the attachment of, attached property on the ground that it is **not liable** to attachment, the court shall **investigate** it "with the like power … as if he was a party to the suit".
- **Proviso (added 1980):** no investigation where the claim was **designedly or unnecessarily delayed**, or not made within a **reasonable time** or within **one year of the first attachment**, whichever is earlier — unless the claimant proves title acquired **in good faith and for consideration after** the attachment, or that his predecessors fraudulently omitted to claim (and impleads them).
- **r. 58(2):** the court may **postpone the sale** pending the investigation.
- **r. 59:** the claimant must prove that **at the date of attachment** he had title to, or an interest in, the property.
- **r. 60:** if satisfied, the court **releases** the property wholly or partly; **r. 61:** if the property is subject to a mortgage or charge, attachment may continue subject to it.
- **r. 62:** "All questions relating to the right, title or interest of the claimant or objector in the attached property shall be **adjudicated upon and determined by the Court** and **no separate suit shall lie** to establish such title, right or interest."
- **r. 63** (which in India allows a regular suit by the unsuccessful party) was **omitted** by the Law Reforms Ordinance 1972.
- The order is a **deemed decree** (s. 2(2) includes an order under O. XXI r. 60) — appealable as a decree; orders under r. 62 / r. 103 are also appealable under **O. XLIII r. 1(ii)**.
:::

:::why Why decide title in execution?
To end the old practice of endless "claim suits" after summary execution orders, which delayed decree-holders for years. The executing court now decides title **fully** — with evidence, as in a suit — and the decision is final subject to appeal.
:::

## 10.5 Sale of attached property — O. XXI rr. 64 to 94

![Sale of immovable property in execution — from attachment to sale certificate](sale_flow.png)

### The steps

1. **Order of sale** (r. 64) — only so much property as is necessary to satisfy the decree.
2. **Public auction** by a court officer (r. 65).
3. **Proclamation of sale** (r. 66) — drawn up after notice to both parties, stating time and place, the property, revenue assessed, encumbrances, the amount to be recovered, and other material facts; published (r. 67) by beat of drum and as directed; **no sale** within **30 days** (immovable) or **15 days** (movable) of the proclamation's affixation, without the judgment-debtor's consent (r. 68).
4. **Adjournment / stoppage** (r. 69) — the sale stops if the debt and costs are tendered before the hammer falls.
5. **Decree-holder may not bid** without the court's **permission** (r. 72); if he purchases with permission, he may set off the price against the decree; if without, the sale may be set aside. **Court officers** cannot bid (r. 73).
6. **Immovable property — deposit and balance:** the purchaser deposits **25%** of the price **immediately** on being declared purchaser; in default, the property is re-sold at once (r. 84). The **balance** must be paid "before the Court closes on the **fifteenth day** from the sale" (r. 85); in default, the deposit may be **forfeited** and the property re-sold (r. 86). A co-sharer's equal bid gets **preference** (r. 88).

- **r. 92:** if no application is made, or it is dismissed, the court **confirms** the sale and it becomes **absolute**; no suit lies to set aside an order under r. 92 (r. 92(3)).
- **r. 93:** if the sale is set aside, the purchaser gets his money back.
- **r. 94:** the court grants a **sale certificate** to the purchaser, dated the day the sale became absolute.
- **s. 65:** the property **vests** in the purchaser from the date of the **sale**, not from the date it becomes absolute.
- **s. 66:** no suit lies against a certified purchaser on the ground that he purchased **on behalf of the plaintiff** (anti-benami rule for court sales).

## 10.6 Delivery of possession and resistance — O. XXI rr. 95 to 103, s. 74

- **r. 95:** where the property sold is in the occupancy of the **judgment-debtor** (or someone on his behalf), the court orders **actual delivery** to the purchaser — removing any person who refuses to vacate.
- **r. 96:** where it is in the occupancy of a **tenant**, **symbolic** delivery — affixing a copy of the certificate and proclaiming to the occupant that the judgment-debtor's interest has passed to the purchaser.

### Resistance and dispossession — the Pakistani scheme

1. **r. 97:** where the **decree-holder** for possession, or an **auction-purchaser**, is **resisted or obstructed** by any person, he may **complain** to the court (within **30 days** of resistance — Art. 167); the court fixes a day and **investigates**.
2. **r. 98:** if the resistance was **without just cause** by the **judgment-debtor** (or at his instigation), the applicant is put into possession; on continued resistance, the obstructer may be **detained** in civil prison for up to **thirty days**.
3. **r. 99:** if the resistance is by a person (other than the judgment-debtor) **claiming in good faith** to be in possession **on his own account** (or for someone other than the judgment-debtor), the application is **dismissed** — but see r. 103.
4. **r. 100:** a person (other than the judgment-debtor) who has been **dispossessed** by the decree-holder or purchaser may **complain** (within **30 days** of dispossession — Art. 165); **r. 101:** if he was in possession on his own account, he is **restored**.
5. **r. 102:** rules 99 and 101 do **not** protect a **transferee pendente lite** from the judgment-debtor (he is bound by the decree — *lis pendens*).
6. **r. 103 (Pakistan, 1972):** "**All questions arising as to title, right or interest in, or possession of immoveable property** between an applicant under rule 97 and the opposite party, or between an applicant under rule 100 and the opposite party, **shall be adjudicated upon and determined by the Court, and no separate suit shall lie** for the determination of any such matter." Orders under rr. 98, 99, 101 and 103 are **deemed decrees** (s. 2(2)) — appealable as decrees.

### Police assistance — s. 74 (Islamabad text)

> "74. (1) Where the Court is satisfied that the holder of a decree for the possession of property or a purchaser of property sold in execution of a decree has been resisted or obstructed in obtaining possession of the property by the judgment-debtor or any other person on his behalf and that such resistance or obstruction was without any just cause, the Court may … order the judgment-debtor or such other person to undergo **simple imprisonment for a term which may extend to thirty days** and may further direct that the decree-holder or the purchaser be put into possession of the property. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), where a judgment-debtor or any other person resists or obstructs the execution of a decree, the Court may direct the **officer in charge of the police-station** … to provide the necessary **police assistance** for the execution of the decree."

:::eg The LHC ASJ 2020 problem — obstruction before possession
*Facts:* A, an auction-purchaser holding a sale certificate (O. XXI r. 94), applies for delivery of possession (r. 95). Before A goes to the spot, B applies saying he is in possession **on his own account** and possession should not be given to A until his application is decided. A objects: the warrant should issue first and no inquiry should begin on a "prospective" objection. The court treats B's application as resistance and starts an inquiry under r. 97. Is this procedure warranted?
*Answer:* Rule 97 is triggered when the decree-holder or purchaser "is resisted or obstructed … in obtaining possession", and the **application under r. 97 is to be made by the decree-holder / purchaser**, not by the third party. A third party who has not yet been dispossessed has no application under r. 97 or r. 100 (r. 100 requires actual **dispossession**). Strictly, therefore, B's anticipatory application is not maintainable under r. 97; the warrant of possession should issue under r. 95; if B then actually **resists**, A may apply under r. 97 and the court investigates (r. 99 / r. 103); if B is **dispossessed**, he may apply under r. 100–101. However, because r. 103 makes the executing court the **only** forum for deciding B's title and possession (no separate suit), many courts treat such an objection as one under s. 47 / O. XXI r. 58 read with s. 151 and inquire to **avoid a multiplicity of proceedings and a breach of the peace**. A balanced answer: the court's procedure is **not strictly warranted by r. 97**, but the court has power under s. 151 to entertain the objection and decide it under the r. 103 scheme to secure the ends of justice — provided the inquiry is not used merely to delay A.
:::

## 10.7 Rateable distribution — s. 73

Where assets are **held by a court** and **more persons than one** have, **before the receipt** of the assets, applied to that court for execution of **money decrees** against the **same judgment-debtor** and have not obtained satisfaction, the assets (after deducting costs of realisation) are **rateably distributed** among all such decree-holders. Property sold subject to a mortgage or charge: the proceeds pay first the costs of sale, then the mortgage debt, then interest, then rateably among decree-holders (s. 73(1) provisos). A person who receives more than his share may be sued to refund (s. 73(2)). Government's priority is saved (s. 73(3)).
