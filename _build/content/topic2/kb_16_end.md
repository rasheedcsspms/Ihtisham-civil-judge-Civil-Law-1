# Chapter 17 · The great distinctions of Topic 2

{w:25,37,38}
|Pair|First|Second|
|Temporary vs perpetual injunction|Interim, until disposal or further order; CPC O. XXXIX (s. 53 SRA)|Final, by decree after trial; ss. 54–57 SRA|
|Prohibitory vs mandatory injunction|Restrains an act|Compels an act; at interim stage only in rare cases (*Dorab Cawasji Warden*)|
|Attachment before judgment vs attachment in execution|Protective; intent to obstruct execution must be shown (O. XXXVIII r. 5)|Coercive; to satisfy an existing decree (s. 60; O. XXI)|
|Arrest before judgment vs arrest in execution|Security for appearance; prison ≤ 6 months (O. XXXVIII r. 4)|Coercion to pay; detention ≤ 1 year after show-cause (ss. 51, 55)|
|Withdrawal with vs without permission|Fresh suit allowed (formal defect / sufficient grounds)|Fresh suit barred; costs (O. XXIII r. 1(3))|
|Compromise decree vs contested decree|No appeal (s. 96(3)); challenge via s. 12(2)|Appeal as of right (s. 96)|
|Abatement (India) vs no abatement (Pakistan)|Suit abates if legal representatives not substituted in time|Court may proceed; judgment binds; heir may apply under r. 9(2) in 60 days|
|Next friend vs guardian for the suit|For a minor **plaintiff** (O. XXXII r. 1)|For a minor **defendant**, appointed by court (r. 3)|
|Legal set-off vs interpleader|Defendant's cross-claim of money|Stakeholder's suit against rival claimants|
|Summary suit vs ordinary suit|Leave to defend needed; plaint deemed admitted otherwise; decree executable forthwith|Defendant defends as of right|
|s. 47 objection vs O. XXI r. 58 claim|Between parties / representatives; execution, discharge, satisfaction; r. 23-A deposit|By a third party claiming the attached property; within 1 year; no separate suit (r. 62)|
|O. XXI r. 97 vs r. 100|Decree-holder / purchaser **resisted** complains (30 days)|Stranger **dispossessed** complains (30 days)|
|r. 89 vs r. 90 vs r. 91 (setting aside sale)|Owner deposits 5% + decree amount in 30 days|Material irregularity / fraud + substantial injury; deposit up to 20%; purchaser — no saleable interest (r. 91)|
|Appeal vs review|Higher court; facts and law|Same court; error apparent / new evidence; narrow|
|Appeal vs revision|Right; facts and law|Discretionary; jurisdictional errors only; no appeal must lie|
|Review vs s. 152 amendment|Changes the decision for legal grounds; 90 days|Corrects clerical slips; any time; decision unchanged|
|First vs second appeal|Facts and law; s. 96|Law only — three grounds; s. 100|
|Reference vs revision|Lower court asks High Court before deciding (doubt on law)|High Court corrects a decided case|
|s. 144 vs s. 151 restitution|Decree varied / reversed; statutory|Other cases (interim orders vacated); inherent power|
|s. 148 vs s. 5 Limitation Act|Enlarges time **fixed by the court**|Extends **statutory** limitation for appeals / applications on sufficient cause|

# Chapter 18 · Exam technique: injunction orders, execution problems and remedies

## 18.1 Specimen: order on an application for temporary injunction

:::order IN THE COURT OF CIVIL JUDGE 1st CLASS, ISLAMABAD (EAST)
Civil Suit No. ___ of 2026 · Faisal Mehmood v. Arshad Ali and another · Suit for specific performance and permanent injunction

__ORDER__ (on the application under Order XXXIX rules 1 and 2 read with section 151 CPC)

1. **The application.** The plaintiff seeks a temporary injunction restraining the defendants from alienating, transferring or creating any third-party interest in House No. 14, Street 7, Sector G-13/1, Islamabad, during the pendency of the suit.
2. **Plaint in brief.** The plaintiff relies on an agreement to sell dated 10.01.2026 for Rs. 32,000,000, under which he paid Rs. 6,000,000 as earnest money through a crossed cheque; he alleges that defendant No. 1 now intends to sell the house to defendant No. 2.
3. **Reply.** Defendant No. 1 admits receipt of Rs. 6,000,000 but claims it was a loan; he denies any intention to sell to defendant No. 2.
4. **Arguments heard; record perused.**
5. **Law.** A temporary injunction is granted where the applicant shows (i) a **prima facie case**, (ii) that the **balance of convenience** lies in his favour, and (iii) that he will suffer **irreparable loss** if the injunction is refused (*American Cyanamid Co. v. Ethicon Ltd.*, [1975] AC 396; *Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh*, (1992) 1 SCC 719). The findings at this stage are **tentative**.
6. **Prima facie case.** The agreement to sell bears the signature of defendant No. 1, which he does not deny; payment of Rs. 6,000,000 by crossed cheque is admitted; the only dispute is the nature of the payment. A serious question arises for trial; a **prima facie case** exists.
7. **Balance of convenience.** If the injunction is refused and the house is sold, the plaintiff's suit for specific performance may be defeated or multiplied by a further suit against a purchaser; if it is granted, the defendant only remains unable to sell during the trial, while he continues to live in and enjoy the house. The balance of convenience favours the plaintiff.
8. **Irreparable loss.** Immovable property is **unique**; its loss cannot ordinarily be compensated in money. The plaintiff would suffer irreparable loss.
9. **Conclusion.** The application is **accepted**. Defendants are restrained from alienating, transferring or creating any third-party interest in the suit property till the final disposal of the suit, subject to the plaintiff furnishing an **undertaking** to compensate the defendants if the suit fails. Under O. XXXIX r. 2B, this injunction shall remain in force for six months and may be extended thereafter after hearing the parties. The observations above are tentative and shall not influence the decision on merits.

Announced · ___ · Civil Judge 1st Class, Islamabad (East) (seal)
:::

## 18.2 How to attack an execution problem

1. **Identify the decree** (money / possession / injunction / specific performance) and the **mode** sought (s. 51; O. XXI rr. 30–36).
2. **Forum and limitation:** which court (ss. 37–39); Art. 181 / s. 48; notice (r. 22).
3. **Who is objecting?** Judgment-debtor (s. 47 — deposit first, r. 23-A) or third party (claim r. 58 within one year; resistance r. 97; dispossession r. 100).
4. **What is the test?** (e.g., possession on own account in good faith — r. 99; title at the date of attachment — r. 59; material irregularity + substantial injury — r. 90; means and wilful refusal — s. 51 proviso).
5. **Final forum:** decided in execution; **no separate suit** (rr. 62, 103; s. 47); appeal against the order (s. 104(1)(ff); O. XLIII r. 1(ii), (j); deemed decree).
