# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 2 covers

Topic 1 took the suit from the plaint to the framing of issues and the summoning of witnesses. Topic 2 completes the journey:

- **the hearing and the judgment** — evidence, affidavits, judgment and decree, correction of judgments (Orders XVIII–XX; ss. 33, 152–153);
- **events during the suit** — death of a party (with Pakistan's special "no abatement" rule), withdrawal and compromise, payment into court, commissions (Orders XXII–XXVI);
- **suits in particular cases** — suits by or against Government (with Pakistan's non-mandatory **s. 80** notice), minors, paupers, firms and corporations, **interpleader**, summary suits on negotiable instruments (ss. 79–93; Orders XXVII–XXXVII);
- **interim relief** — arrest and attachment before judgment, **temporary injunctions** (with the Pakistani time limits of O. XXXIX rr. 2A and 2B), receivers (s. 94; Orders XXXVIII–XL);
- **execution** — which court executes, limitation, s. 47, modes of execution, arrest and detention, attachment and sale, claims and objections, delivery of possession and resistance (ss. 36–74; Order XXI);
- **remedies against decisions** — first appeal, second appeal, appeals from orders, review, revision and reference (ss. 96–115; Orders XLI–XLVII), in the Islamabad text as amended in 2023;
- **general powers** — restitution (s. 144), enlargement of time (s. 148), court fees (s. 149), inherent powers (s. 151).

:::ict Five Pakistani rules that Indian textbooks get wrong
1. **No abatement on death** (O. XXII rr. 3(2), 4(3)–(4), as substituted in 1972): if legal representatives are not brought on record in time, the court may simply proceed and its judgment binds as if pronounced before the death. Indian books say the suit "abates".
2. **Section 80 notice is not a bar**: in Pakistan a suit against Government filed without the two-month notice is maintainable; the plaintiff only loses costs if the claim is settled within two months, and Government gets at least three months for its written statement.
3. **No separate suit after execution claims** (O. XXI rr. 58–63, 97–103): questions of title raised by claimants and objectors are decided by the executing court itself, and no separate suit lies (r. 63 was omitted in 1972).
4. **Deposit before objection** (O. XXI r. 23-A): an objection by the judgment-debtor to execution is not even considered unless he deposits the decretal amount or furnishes security.
5. **Time limits on injunctions** (O. XXXIX rr. 2A, 2B, 4A): an ex parte injunction ordinarily lasts 15 days; an injunction after hearing lapses after six months unless extended with reasons (report to the High Court); injunctions against public revenue lapse after six months.
:::
