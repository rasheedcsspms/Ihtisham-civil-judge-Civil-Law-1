# Chapter 11 · First appeals (ss. 96 to 99, 107; Order XLI)

## 11.1 Appeal — a right created by statute

An **appeal** is the judicial examination by a higher court of the decision of a lower court. Three foundational principles:

1. **Right of appeal is a creature of statute.** There is no inherent right of appeal; it exists only where a statute gives it.
2. **It is a substantive, vested right.** The right of appeal **vests on the date the suit is instituted** and is governed by the law then in force, unless a later law takes it away expressly or by necessary implication.
3. **A first appeal is a continuation of the suit** — a rehearing on **facts and law**.

:::case Garikapati Veeraya v. N. Subbiah Choudhry (AIR 1957 SC 540)
__Held.__ The legal pursuit of a remedy — suit, appeal and second appeal — is one proceeding, connected by an intrinsic unity. The right of appeal is **not a mere matter of procedure but a substantive right**, which **vests in the suitor at the date of the institution of the suit** and is to be governed by the law then prevailing, not by the law at the date of the decision or of filing the appeal. This vested right can be taken away only by a subsequent enactment that does so **expressly or by necessary intendment**.
:::

:::ict Section 159 and appeals in Islamabad
The Islamabad saving clause (s. 159, Act XIX of 2023) confirms the principle: proceedings instituted before the 2023 Amendment continue under the old provisions — including the old forum and scope of appeal.
:::

## 11.2 Appeal from original decree — s. 96 (Islamabad text)

> "96. Save where otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or by any other law for the time being in force, an appeal shall lie from **every decree** passed by any Court exercising original jurisdiction to the Court authorized to hear appeal from the decisions of such Court. (2) An appeal may lie from an original decree passed **ex-parte**. (3) **No appeal shall lie from a decree passed by the Court with consent of the parties.**" (substituted by Act XIX of 2023)

- **First appeal lies as of right** against every decree (including a **deemed decree** — rejection of plaint, s. 144 orders, O. XXI rr. 60, 98, 99, 101, 103).
- **Forum:** decided by the Civil Courts Ordinance — Civil Judge → **District Judge** (s. 18(1), Act XV of 2025); DJ / ADJ original → **High Court** (s. 17) (Topic 1, Chapter 3).
- **Ex parte decree** — appeal lies (s. 96(2)), alongside O. IX r. 13.
- **Consent decree** — no appeal (s. 96(3)); remedy for fraud is s. 12(2).
- **Who may appeal:** a **party aggrieved** by the decree, his legal representative, or, **with leave**, a person not a party who is bound or prejudicially affected by the decree.
- **Limitation:** **30 days** to the District Judge (Art. 152), **90 days** to the High Court (Art. 156), from the date of the decree; time for obtaining certified copies is excluded (s. 12 Limitation Act).

### Related sections

- **s. 97:** a party who does **not appeal from a preliminary decree** cannot challenge its correctness in an appeal from the **final** decree.
- **s. 98:** appeals heard by two or more judges are decided by majority; if no majority to vary or reverse, the decree is **confirmed**.
- **s. 99:** **no reversal or remand** for misjoinder or any error, defect or irregularity **not affecting the merits or the jurisdiction** of the court.
- **s. 105:** an error in an **interlocutory order** affecting the decision may be raised as a ground in the appeal from the decree; but a party who did not appeal from an appealable **remand order** cannot later dispute it.

## 11.3 Procedure in first appeal — Order XLI

### The memorandum — rr. 1 to 4

- **r. 1:** appeal by **memorandum** signed by the appellant or pleader, accompanied by a **copy of the decree** and (unless dispensed with) of the **judgment**; grounds stated **concisely under distinct heads**, numbered, **without argument or narrative**.
- **r. 2:** the appellant cannot urge grounds not in the memorandum **without leave**; but the court may decide on any ground if the affected party had an opportunity to contest it.
- **r. 3:** a defective memorandum may be **rejected** (with reasons) or **returned for amendment**.
- **r. 4:** where the decree proceeds on a ground **common** to all plaintiffs or defendants, **any one** may appeal from the whole decree, and the court may reverse or vary it in favour of all.

### Stay — rr. 5 and 6

> "5. (1) An appeal shall not operate as a stay of proceedings under a decree or order appealed from except so far as the Appellate Court may order, nor shall execution of a decree be stayed by reason only of an appeal having been preferred from the decree; but the Appellate Court may for sufficient cause order stay of execution of such decree. … (3) No order for stay of execution shall be made … unless the Court making it is satisfied— (a) that **substantial loss** may result to the party applying for stay of execution unless the order is made; (b) that the application has been made **without unreasonable delay**; and (c) that **security** has been given by the applicant for the due performance of such decree or order as may ultimately be binding upon him. (4) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-rule (3), the Court may make an **ex parte** order for stay of execution pending the hearing of the application."

- **r. 5(2):** the **trial court** may also stay execution if applied to **before the time for appeal expires**.
- **r. 6:** where execution proceeds pending appeal, the court shall take **security for restitution**; sale of immovable property shall be **stayed** on the judgment-debtor's application, on terms.
- **O. XXVII r. 8A:** no security is required from **Government**.

### Admission and hearing — rr. 9 to 21

- **r. 11:** the appellate court may **dismiss the appeal in limine** (after hearing the appellant, without notice to the respondent) if it finds no merit; if the appellant does not appear, it may dismiss for default.
- **rr. 12–15:** if admitted, a day is fixed and **notice** issued to the respondent and the lower court.
- **r. 17:** if the appellant does not appear on the hearing date, the appeal may be **dismissed for default**; if the respondent does not appear, it is heard **ex parte**.
- **r. 19:** re-admission of an appeal dismissed for default on **sufficient cause** (30 days — Art. 168; s. 5 LA applies).
- **r. 20:** a person who was a party below but not joined in the appeal, and is interested in the result, may be **made a respondent**.
- **r. 21:** **re-hearing** at the instance of a respondent against whom the appeal was decided **ex parte** (no due service / sufficient cause; 30 days — Art. 169).

### Cross-objection — r. 22

A respondent who has not appealed may (a) **support the decree** on any ground decided against him below, and (b) file a **cross-objection** to the decree which he could have taken by way of appeal, **within one month** of service of notice of the appeal hearing (or further time allowed). The cross-objection is heard even if the main appeal is **withdrawn or dismissed for default** (r. 22(4)).

### Remand, issues and final decision — rr. 23 to 26

- **r. 23 — remand:** where the trial court disposed of the suit on a **preliminary point** and the decree is reversed in appeal, the appellate court may **remand** the case for trial, directing which issues to try; evidence already recorded remains evidence.
- **r. 24:** where the evidence on record is **sufficient**, the appellate court may **finally determine** the suit itself, even if the trial court proceeded on another ground — remand is not automatic.
- **r. 25:** where the trial court omitted to frame or try an **essential issue**, the appellate court may frame it and **refer it for trial** to the lower court, which returns its findings with the evidence (r. 26); the appellate court then decides.

### Additional evidence — rr. 27 to 29

> "27. (1) The parties to an appeal shall not be entitled to produce additional evidence, whether oral or documentary, in the Appellate Court. But if— (a) the Court from whose decree the appeal is preferred has refused to admit evidence which ought to have been admitted, or (b) the Appellate Court requires any document to be produced or any witness to be examined to enable it to pronounce judgment, or for any other substantial cause, the Appellate Court may allow such evidence or document to be produced, or witness to be examined. (2) Wherever additional evidence is allowed to be produced by an Appellate Court, the Court shall record the reason for its admission."

:::case Arjan Singh v. Kartar Singh (AIR 1951 SC 193)
__Held.__ The legitimate occasion for O. XLI r. 27 is when, on examining the evidence as it stands, some **inherent lacuna or defect becomes apparent** to the appellate court — not where a party discovers fresh evidence and wishes to use it. The appellate court must **require** the evidence for itself "to enable it to pronounce judgment"; additional evidence is not to be admitted to **patch up the weak parts** of a party's case or to fill gaps that he failed to cover at the trial.

__Ratio.__ Additional evidence is an **exception**, admitted on the court's own need or for a substantial cause, with reasons recorded; it is not a second chance at trial.
:::

### Judgment in appeal — rr. 30 to 37

- **r. 31:** the appellate judgment shall state **(a) points for determination, (b) the decision, (c) the reasons, and (d) where the decree is reversed or varied, the relief**.
- **r. 32:** the judgment may **confirm, vary or reverse** the decree, or pass a decree by consent of the parties.
- **r. 33 — wide power:** the appellate court may pass any decree that **ought to have been passed**, and further orders, even if the appeal is as to **part** of the decree, and **in favour of respondents who have not appealed** or filed objections (*Illustration:* A sues X and Y in the alternative and gets a decree against X; X appeals; the court may pass a decree against Y). Proviso: no s. 35A order on an objection the lower court refused.

:::case Santosh Hazari v. Purushottam Tiwari ((2001) 3 SCC 179)
__Held.__ A first appeal is a valuable right; the **whole case is open for rehearing** on both facts and law. The judgment of the first appellate court must show **conscious application of mind**, recording findings supported by reasons **on all the issues** and the contentions put forth. When reversing findings of fact, the appellate court must deal with the **reasons** given by the trial court and come to grips with them. A first appellate court cannot merely endorse or reverse by a cryptic order.
:::

### Powers of the appellate court — s. 107

> "107. (1) Subject to such conditions and limitations as may be prescribed, an Appellate Court shall have power— (a) to determine a case finally; (b) to remand a case; (c) to frame issues and refer them for trial; (d) to take additional evidence or to require such evidence to be taken. (2) … the Appellate Court shall have the same powers and shall perform as nearly as may be the same duties as are conferred and imposed by this Code on Courts of original jurisdiction in respect of suits instituted therein."

:::recap Chapter 11 in eight lines
- Appeal: statutory, substantive, vests on date of suit (*Garikapati Veeraya*); first appeal is a rehearing on facts and law.
- s. 96 (Islamabad 2023): appeal from every original decree; **ex parte** decree appealable (s. 96(2)); **no appeal from consent decree** (s. 96(3)).
- Limitation: **30 days** (District Judge, Art. 152); **90 days** (High Court, Art. 156); s. 97 preliminary decree; s. 99 no reversal for harmless irregularity.
- Memorandum with decree copy; grounds numbered without argument (O. XLI r. 1).
- Stay only for **sufficient cause**: substantial loss + no delay + security (r. 5); restitution security (r. 6).
- Dismissal in limine (r. 11); default and readmission (rr. 17, 19); ex parte re-hearing (r. 21); **cross-objection within one month** (r. 22).
- Remand only after reversal on preliminary point (r. 23); decide finally if evidence sufficient (r. 24); refer issues (r. 25); **additional evidence only if the court requires it**, reasons recorded (r. 27; *Arjan Singh*).
- Judgment: points, decision, reasons, relief (r. 31); full rehearing with findings on all issues (*Santosh Hazari*); wide powers (r. 33; s. 107).
:::

# Chapter 12 · Second appeals, appeals from orders and appeals to the Supreme Court (ss. 100 to 112; Orders XLII to XLV)

## 12.1 Second appeal — ss. 100 to 103 (Pakistani text)

> "100. (1) Save where otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or in any other law, for the time being in force, an appeal shall lie to the High Court from every decree passed in appeal by any Court subordinate to that High Court, on any of the following grounds, namely:— (a) the decision being **contrary to law** or to some **usage having the force of law**; (b) the decision having **failed to determine some material issue of law** or usage having the force of law; or (c) a **substantial error or defect in the procedure** provided by this Code or by any other law for the time being in force, which may possibly have produced error or defect in the decision of the case upon the merits."

- **s. 101:** no second appeal on any other ground — in particular, **not on facts**, however erroneous. A finding of fact is final at the first appellate stage — unless it is based on **no evidence**, on **misreading or non-reading of evidence**, or on **inadmissible evidence**, which are treated as errors of law.
- **s. 102:** "No second appeal shall lie in any suit except when the amount or value of the subject matter of the original suit does not exceed the amount or value as may be prescribed." (The prescribing authority sets the value limit; check the current notification.)
- **s. 103:** in a second appeal, the High Court may determine an **issue of fact** if the evidence is sufficient and the issue was not determined, or was wrongly determined by the lower appellate court because of an illegality, omission, error or defect referred to in s. 100(1).
- **Order XLII:** the rules of Order XLI apply to appeals from appellate decrees.
- **Limitation:** **90 days** (Art. 156).

:::tip Pakistan vs India on second appeal
India (since 1976) requires a "**substantial question of law**" for a second appeal. Pakistan's s. 100 keeps the **older three grounds** — contrary to law, failure to determine a material issue of law, substantial procedural error. In an IHC answer, quote the Pakistani grounds.
:::

## 12.2 Appeals from orders — ss. 104 to 106, Order XLIII

> "104. (1) An appeal shall lie from the following orders, and save as otherwise expressly provided in the body of this Code or by any law for the time being in force, from no other orders:— … (f) an order under **section 35A**; (ff) an order under **section 47**; (fff) an order under **section 91 or section 92 refusing leave** to institute a suit; (g) an order under **section 95**; (h) an order under any of the provisions of this Code imposing a **fine** or directing the **arrest or detention** in the civil prison of any person except where such arrest or detention is in execution of a decree; (i) any order made under **rules** from which an appeal is expressly allowed by rules:
> Provided that no appeal shall lie against any order specified in clause (f) save on the ground that no order, or an order for the payment of a less amount, ought to have been made.
> (2) No appeal shall lie from any order passed in appeal under this section."

## 12.3 Appeals to the Supreme Court; pauper appeals

- **s. 109** (as adapted) provides for appeals from High Court judgments where the value in both courts is **Rs. 50,000 or more** and the High Court has **varied or set aside** the decision below, or on a High Court **certificate** that the case involves a substantial question of law as to the interpretation of the **Constitution**. In practice, the Supreme Court's appellate jurisdiction is governed by **Article 185 of the Constitution** (appeal as of right in the cases listed in Art. 185(2); otherwise by **leave** under Art. 185(3)). **s. 112** saves the Supreme Court's powers.
- **Order XLIV:** a person unable to pay the court fee for an **appeal** may apply to appeal as a **pauper** (30 days — Art. 170); the court checks his means and whether the decree is contrary to law or unjust.

- **Appeals from orders:** only those in **s. 104** and **O. XLIII r. 1** (return of plaint, rejection of O. IX applications, compromise, interpleader, attachment, injunction, receiver, remand, review granted …); s. 35A orders only on quantum; **no second appeal** from appellate orders (s. 104(2)).

- Forum (s. 106); **notice before appeal** against interim orders (O. XLIII r. 3, Pakistan).
- Supreme Court: Art. 185 Constitution; pauper appeals O. XLIV.

# Chapter 13 · Reference, review and revision (ss. 113 to 115; Orders XLVI and XLVII)

## 13.1 Reference — s. 113, Order XLVI

Where, in a suit or appeal **in which the decree is not subject to appeal** (or in execution of such a decree), a **question of law or usage having the force of law** arises on which the court **entertains reasonable doubt**, it may — of its own motion or on application — **state a case** with its own opinion and **refer** it to the **High Court** (O. XLVI r. 1). It may stay the proceedings or pass a decree **contingent** on the High Court's decision, but the decree is not executed until the High Court decides (r. 2); the High Court decides the point and the referring court disposes of the case accordingly (r. 3). Rule 6 allows reference of doubts whether a suit is cognizable by a Court of Small Causes.

## 13.2 Review — s. 114, Order XLVII

> "114. (1) Subject as aforesaid, any person considering himself aggrieved by a decree or order from which— (a) an appeal is allowed by this Code, but from which no appeal has been preferred; or (b) no appeal is allowed by this Code, may apply for a review of judgment to the Court which passed the decree or made the order, and the Court may make such order thereon as it thinks fit." (Islamabad text)

### Grounds — O. XLVII r. 1

1. **Discovery of new and important matter or evidence** which, **after the exercise of due diligence**, was not within the applicant's knowledge or could not be produced by him when the decree was passed;
2. **Mistake or error apparent on the face of the record**; or
3. **Any other sufficient reason** — read as a reason **analogous** to the first two (*ejusdem generis*).

:::case Moran Mar Basselios Catholicos v. Mar Poulose Athanasius (AIR 1954 SC 526)
__Held.__ The power of review is **not inherent**; it must be conferred by law. Under O. XLVII r. 1, review lies only on (i) discovery of new and important matter or evidence not available despite due diligence, (ii) mistake or error apparent on the face of the record, or (iii) any other sufficient reason — and the words "any other sufficient reason" mean a reason **sufficient on grounds at least analogous** to those specified in the rule.
:::

:::case Thungabhadra Industries v. Government of Andhra Pradesh (AIR 1964 SC 1372) · Parsion Devi v. Sumitri Devi ((1997) 8 SCC 715)
__Held.__ A review is **not an appeal in disguise**. An error that has to be **established by a long-drawn process of reasoning** on points where there may conceivably be two opinions is not an "error apparent on the face of the record". The error must be **self-evident** — one that strikes on mere looking at the record. An erroneous decision on a debatable point may be corrected in appeal, not review.
:::

### Procedure and limits

- **Forum — r. 1, r. 2, r. 5:** to the **court which passed** the decree; a review on grounds **other than** new evidence or a clerical / arithmetical / apparent error lies only to the **same judge** (unless he has left, when his successor may hear it after notice was ordered); in a multi-judge court, the same judges hear it if available within six months.
- **r. 1(2):** a party not appealing may seek review even though another party has appealed, unless the ground is common to both or he can present it as respondent in the appeal.
- **r. 4:** the court **rejects** the application if no sufficient ground; it **grants** it only after **notice** to the opposite party, and new-evidence grounds require **strict proof**.
- **r. 7:** an order **rejecting** review is **not appealable**; an order **granting** review may be objected to (contravention of rr. 2, 4 or limitation) by appeal from that order (**O. XLIII r. 1(w)**) or in the appeal from the final decree.
- **r. 8:** if granted, the case is **re-heard**.
- **r. 9:** **no review of a review** — no application to review an order on a review application, or a decree / order passed on review.
- **Limitation:** **90 days** (Art. 173); **15 days** for small cause courts (Art. 161); **20 days** for a High Court in original jurisdiction (Art. 162). Restoration of a review application dismissed for default — 15 days (Art. 160).

## 13.3 Revision — s. 115 (Islamabad text, Act XIX of 2023)

> "115. (1) A High Court may call for the record of any case which has been decided by any Court subordinate to that High Court and **in which no appeal lies thereto**, and if such subordinate Court appears— (a) to have **exercised a jurisdiction not vested** in it by law; or (b) to have **failed to exercise a jurisdiction so vested**; or (c) to have acted in the exercise of its jurisdiction **illegally or with material irregularity**, the High Court may make such order in the case as it thinks fit:
> Provided that where a person makes an application under this sub-section, he shall, in support of such application, furnish **copies of the pleadings, documents and order** of the subordinate Court and the High Court shall, except for reasons to be recorded, dispose of such application **without calling for the record** of the subordinate Court:
> Provided further that such application shall be made **within ninety days** of the decision of the subordinate Court which shall provide a copy of such decision **within three days** thereof and the High Court shall dispose of such application **within six months**.
> (2) A **District Court** may exercise the powers conferred on the High Court by sub-section (1) in respect of any case decided by a Court subordinate to that District Court in which no appeal lies and the amount or value of the subject-matter whereof does not exceed the limits of the **appellate jurisdiction of the District Court**.
> (3) If an application under sub-section (1) in respect of a case within the competence of the District Court has been made either to the High Court or the District Court, **no further such application** shall be made to either of them.
> (4) **No proceedings in revision shall be entertained by the High Court against an order made under sub-section (2) by the District Court.**"

### Conditions

1. A **case decided** (includes an **interlocutory order** deciding some right or obligation — *Major S.S. Khanna v. Brig. F.J. Dillon*, AIR 1964 SC 497);
2. by a court **subordinate** to the High Court / District Court;
3. **no appeal lies** (to the revisional court);
4. a **jurisdictional error**: (a) exercising jurisdiction not vested, (b) failing to exercise vested jurisdiction, or (c) illegality or **material irregularity** in its exercise.

### Nature of revision

- Revision is a **supervisory** power, not a right of appeal. The revisional court does not re-appraise evidence or correct mere errors of fact or law **within** jurisdiction — unless they amount to illegality or material irregularity (e.g., **misreading or non-reading of evidence**, decision on inadmissible evidence, violation of natural justice).
- It is **discretionary**: even if an error exists, revision may be refused if substantial justice has been done.
- The High Court may act **suo motu** ("may call for the record").
- In Islamabad, after the 2025 amendment of s. 18 of the Ordinance, the District Judge's appellate jurisdiction over Civil Judges is unlimited in value, so the **District Court** can revise any non-appealable decision of a Civil Judge under s. 115(2); the High Court keeps its concurrent power under s. 115(1). By **s. 115(3)–(4)** there is **one revision only**: once an application is made to either court, no further revision lies to either, and the High Court cannot revise the District Court's revisional order (the remaining remedy is constitutional — Art. 199 — in exceptional cases).

:::case Keshardeo Chamria v. Radha Kissen Chamria (AIR 1953 SC 23)
__Held.__ Section 115 applies to matters of **jurisdiction alone** — irregular exercise, non-exercise, or illegal assumption of jurisdiction. If a subordinate court has jurisdiction to decide a question, it does not act without jurisdiction merely because it decides it **wrongly**. The revisional court cannot correct errors of fact or of law which do not relate to jurisdiction.
:::

## 13.4 Appeal, second appeal, review and revision compared (LHC 2023)

|Basis|First appeal|Second appeal|Review|Revision|
|Source|s. 96; O. XLI|s. 100; O. XLII|s. 114; O. XLVII|s. 115|
|Forum|Higher court (DJ / HC)|High Court|**Same** court (same judge)|High Court / District Court|
|Against|Original decree|Appellate decree|Decree / order where no appeal filed or none lies|Case decided where **no appeal lies**|
|Grounds|Facts and law — full rehearing|Law only (3 grounds)|New evidence; error apparent; analogous reason|Jurisdictional error; illegality / material irregularity|

|Further remedy|Second appeal|Supreme Court (Art. 185)|No review of review|No further revision (s. 115(3)–(4))|
|Can it re-appraise facts?|Yes|No (except s. 103)|No|No (except misreading / non-reading)|

:::eg Choosing the right remedy (LHC 2020 Q1 pattern)
A Civil Judge refuses to return a plaint for want of territorial jurisdiction. (1) **Appeal?** No — only an order *returning* a plaint is appealable (O. XLIII r. 1(a)). (2) **Review?** Only if an error apparent on the face of the record exists (e.g., the plaint itself shows the property is in another district) — narrow. (3) **Revision?** Yes — a "case decided", no appeal lies, and the court has **exercised a jurisdiction not vested** in it; in Islamabad, ordinarily to the **District Court** (s. 115(2)) or the High Court (s. 115(1)) — but only one of them (s. 115(3)) — within **90 days**. (4) **Direct appeal to the Supreme Court?** No. (5) The objection can also be urged in the appeal from the final decree (s. 105), having been raised early (s. 21).
:::

:::recap Chapter 13 in six lines
- **Reference** (s. 113, O. XLVI): reasonable doubt on law in non-appealable cases → High Court; contingent decree.
- **Review** (s. 114, O. XLVII): new evidence (due diligence), **error apparent**, analogous reason (*Moran Mar Basselios*); not an appeal in disguise (*Thungabhadra*, *Parsion Devi*); same judge; notice before grant; rejection not appealable; no review of review; **90 days** (Art. 173).
- **Revision** (s. 115, Islamabad 2023): case decided, subordinate court, **no appeal**, jurisdictional error / illegality / material irregularity (*Keshardeo*); copies filed; **90 days**; decided in **6 months**; District Court within its appellate limits (concurrent with High Court); **one revision only**.
- Interlocutory orders can be "cases decided" (*S.S. Khanna*).
- Compare: appeal (facts + law), second appeal (law), review (same court, narrow), revision (supervisory, jurisdiction).
- Wrong remedy wastes limitation — choose carefully.
:::

# Chapter 14 · General powers of the court (ss. 141 to 153)

## 14.1 Restitution — s. 144

> "144. (1) Where and in so far as a decree is varied or reversed, the Court of first instance shall, on the application of any party entitled to any benefit by way of restitution or otherwise, cause such restitution to be made as will, so far as may be, place the parties in the position which they would have occupied but for such decree or such part thereof as has been varied or reversed; and, for this purpose, the Court may make any orders, including orders for the refund of costs and for the payment of interest, damages, compensation and mesne profits, which are properly consequential on such variation or reversal. (2) No suit shall be instituted for the purpose of obtaining any restitution or other relief which could be obtained by application under sub-section (1)."

**Conditions:** (1) a decree (or order) has been **varied or reversed**; (2) the applicant is **entitled to a benefit** by way of restitution; (3) the benefit is **consequential** on the variation or reversal; (4) application to the **court of first instance**. A decision under s. 144 is a **deemed decree** (s. 2(2)) — appealable. A separate suit is barred (s. 144(2)). Courts also order restitution under **s. 151** in situations outside s. 144 (e.g., when an interim order is vacated).

:::why The principle of restitution
*Actus curiae neminem gravabit* — an act of the court shall prejudice no one. If a party received a benefit under a decree that is later reversed, he must **give it back**, with interest or mesne profits, so that the other party is restored to the position he would have been in. "The court should not allow a party to retain an unmerited advantage gained through a judicial order later set aside" (*South Eastern Coalfields Ltd. v. State of M.P.*, (2003) 8 SCC 648).
:::

## 14.2 Enlargement of time and court fees — ss. 148 and 149 (LLB 2009 question)

> "148. Where any period is fixed or granted by the Court for the doing of any act prescribed or allowed by this Code, the Court may, in its discretion, from time to time, enlarge such period, even though the period originally fixed or granted may have expired."

> "149. Where the whole or any part of any fee prescribed for any document by the law for the time being in force relating to court-fees has not been paid, the Court may, in its discretion, at any stage, allow the person, by whom such fee is payable, to pay the whole or part, as the case may be, of such court-fee; and upon such payment the document, in respect of which such fee is payable, shall have the same force and effect as if such fee had been paid in the first instance."

- **s. 148** applies only to periods **fixed by the court**, not to periods fixed by **statute** (e.g., limitation, or the 15-day balance in O. XXI r. 85, which are fixed by law).
- **s. 149** allows deficiency in court fee to be made good **at any stage** — even in appeal — and the payment **relates back** to the date of filing, saving limitation, unless the party acted **mala fide** or the court refuses in its discretion for good reason.

## 14.3 Inherent powers — s. 151 (Islamabad text)

> "151. Nothing in this Code shall be deemed to limit or otherwise affect the inherent power of the Court **to be exercised after recording reasons** to make such orders as may be necessary for the ends of justice or to prevent abuse of the process of the Court." (words inserted by Act VII of 2020)

### Principles

1. Inherent powers are **not conferred** by s. 151 — they are **inherent** in every court; s. 151 only **saves** them.
2. They are exercised **ex debito justitiae** — to do real and substantial justice — and to **prevent abuse of process**.
3. They **cannot** be used where the Code provides a **specific remedy** covering the matter, or **contrary to** an express provision or prohibition of the Code.
4. They **cannot** create a **jurisdiction** or a right of appeal that the law does not give.
5. In Islamabad, they must be exercised **after recording reasons**.

**Examples:** consolidating suits; staying a suit in cases not covered by s. 10; granting an injunction in cases outside O. XXXIX rr. 1–2; recalling an order obtained by fraud; restoring a case dismissed by the court's own mistake; restitution outside s. 144; correcting the court's own mistakes; deciding an application to set aside an order passed without notice.

:::case Manohar Lal Chopra v. Seth Hiralal (AIR 1962 SC 527)
__Held.__ The inherent powers are not controlled by the provisions of the Code except where the Code **expressly or by necessary implication** deals with the matter. The court may issue a temporary injunction under s. 151 in circumstances not covered by O. XXXIX. But the inherent power is not to be exercised in conflict with what has been **expressly provided** in the Code or against the intentions of the legislature.
:::

:::case Padam Sen v. State of U.P. (AIR 1961 SC 218)
__Held.__ The inherent powers of the court are **in addition to** the powers specifically conferred by the Code. They are complementary to those powers, and the court is free to exercise them for the ends of justice or to prevent abuse of process, so long as they do not conflict with express provisions. The court has no inherent power to do what is prohibited by the Code.
:::

## 14.4 Other general provisions

- **s. 141 — Miscellaneous proceedings:** "The procedure provided in this Code with regard to suits shall be followed, as far as it can be made applicable, in all proceedings in any Court of civil jurisdiction" (Islamabad text, 2023) — e.g., s. 12(2) applications, guardianship and succession petitions.
- **s. 132:** women who by custom do not appear in public are exempt from **personal appearance** (not from arrest where permitted); **s. 133:** exemption of other persons (high officials) — evidence by commission.
- **s. 135:** judges, magistrates, and parties, pleaders and witnesses going to, attending and returning from court are **exempt from arrest** under civil process; **s. 135A:** members of legislative bodies exempt during sessions.
- **s. 145:** a **surety's** liability is enforceable in execution as if he were a party (after notice).
- **s. 146:** proceedings may be taken by or against persons **claiming under** a party (e.g., assignees).
- **s. 147:** consent by next friend / guardian of a person under disability, **with express leave** of the court, binds as if he were under no disability.
- **ss. 152–153:** correction of clerical errors and general power to amend (Chapter 2).

:::recap Chapter 14 in five lines
- **s. 144:** restitution on variation / reversal — restore the parties; interest, mesne profits; to court of first instance; no separate suit; deemed decree.
- **s. 148:** enlarge **court-fixed** time even after expiry (not statutory periods); **s. 149:** make up court-fee deficiency at any stage — relates back.
- **s. 151:** inherent powers for ends of justice / prevent abuse; **with recorded reasons** (Islamabad); not against express provisions or where a specific remedy exists (*Manohar Lal Chopra*; *Padam Sen*).
- s. 141: suit procedure applies to miscellaneous proceedings; ss. 132–135A exemptions; s. 145 surety; s. 147 consent with leave.
- ss. 152–153: slips corrected any time; real controversy amendments.
:::
