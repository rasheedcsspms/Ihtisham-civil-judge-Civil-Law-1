# How to use this knowledge base

## What Civil Law Paper II covers

The IHC syllabus for Civil Law Paper II has four parts: (i) **Muhammadan Law or Islamic Law**; (ii) the **Registration Act 1908**; (iii) the **Limitation Act 1908**; and (iv) the **Specific Relief Act 1877**. These volumes divide the paper into four topics:

1. **Topic 1 — Muslim family law** (this volume): marriage, dower, divorce, iddat, maintenance, parentage, guardianship and custody, with the statutes and the Family Court procedure under which these matters are decided.
2. **Topic 2 — Muslim law of property**: inheritance, wills, gifts, waqf and pre-emption.
3. **Topic 3 — the Limitation Act 1908.**
4. **Topic 4 — the Specific Relief Act 1877 and the Registration Act 1908.**

:::trap No bare Act for Muslim law
The syllabus note says that bare copies of the Acts may be consulted **"except in case of Muhammadan Law or Islamic law"**. For Topics 1 and 2 you must **remember** the rules, the section numbers of the MFLO and DMMA, and the leading cases. This volume is therefore written to be **memorised**: each chapter ends with a short recap, and Chapter 12 is a last-night revision sheet.
:::

## The statutes that apply in Islamabad

{w:32,68}
|Law|What it does|
|**West Pakistan Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1962**, s. 2|Makes **Muslim personal law (Shariat)** the rule of decision, "notwithstanding any custom or usage", in questions of succession, special property of females, betrothal, marriage, divorce, dower, adoption, guardianship, minority, legitimacy, family relations, wills, legacies, gifts, waqfs and trusts — **subject to any enactment** (s. 2). Extends to the whole of Pakistan.|
|**Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 (MFLO)**|Overrides any law, custom or usage (s. 3): succession of orphaned grandchildren (s. 4); registration of marriages (s. 5); polygamy (s. 6); talaq (s. 7); other dissolutions (s. 8); maintenance (s. 9); dower (s. 10). Amended in **2021** (Acts XXVIII and XXIX) for **Fiqh-e-Jafria** inheritance and talaq.|
|**Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 (DMMA)**|Grounds on which a wife may obtain a **decree** dissolving her marriage (s. 2); effect of apostasy (s. 4); dower preserved (s. 5).|
|**Family Courts Act 1964** (West Pakistan Act XXXV of 1964, applied in the Islamabad Capital Territory)|Exclusive jurisdiction of **Family Courts** over dissolution of marriage (including khula), dower, maintenance, restitution of conjugal rights, custody and guardianship, jactitation, dowry and personal property; special procedure; pre-trial reconciliation.|
|**Guardians and Wards Act 1890**|Appointment, duties, powers and removal of guardians; custody of minors; the Family Court acts as the "District Court" for this Act.|
|**ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025**|Fixes **18** as the minimum age for both parties in the ICT; penalises child marriage; bars Nikah Registrars from registering it.|
|**Dowry and Bridal Gifts (Restriction) Act 1976**|Limits dowry and bridal gifts; they vest absolutely in the bride.|
|**Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984**, Art. 128|Conclusive proof of legitimacy (Chapter 8).|
|**Constitution**, Arts. 35, 227, 203D|Protection of marriage and family; laws to conform to Islam; the Federal Shariat Court's power to strike down laws repugnant to Islam.|

:::ict The user's post — Civil Judge (Family / Presiding Officer)
The examination is for the post of Civil Judge-cum-Magistrate **including Female Civil Judge (Family / Presiding Officer)**. In Islamabad, Civil Judges preside over **Family Courts**. Family-law questions are therefore not only about doctrine: examiners expect you to know **how the Family Court decides** these cases — pre-trial reconciliation, khula decrees, interim maintenance, custody and visitation orders. Chapter 10 deals with this.
:::

|LHC ASJ, Civil Law-I, 2020 and 2025|Duties of a **guardian of a minor's property** and his removal; the Family Court's power in **maintenance** decrees; **mediation** in custody disputes|
|Sindh High Court, 2022 (short note)|**Guardian of property** of a ward and the limits of his powers|

:::tip The pattern
Three areas dominate the papers: (1) **divorce** in all its forms and the **MFLO s. 7 procedure**; (2) **dower**; and (3) **guardianship and custody** under Muslim law and the Guardians and Wards Act. Master Chapters 3 (dower), 4–5 (divorce) and 9 (guardianship and custody) first.
:::
