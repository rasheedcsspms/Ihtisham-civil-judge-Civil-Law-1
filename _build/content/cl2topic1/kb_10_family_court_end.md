
# Chapter 10 · The Family Court — jurisdiction and procedure

The **West Pakistan Family Courts Act 1964**, as amended (notably by the Family Courts (Amendment) Ordinance 2002), applies in the **Islamabad Capital Territory**. It creates special courts and a simplified, speedy procedure for family disputes. (Section references below are to that Act.)

## 10.1 Object

To provide for the **establishment of Family Courts** "for the expeditious settlement and disposal of disputes relating to marriage and family affairs and for matters connected therewith" (Preamble). The Act emphasises **reconciliation**, **speed** and **simplicity**.

## 10.2 Exclusive jurisdiction — s. 5 and the Schedule

The Family Court has **exclusive jurisdiction** to entertain, hear and adjudicate the matters in the **Schedule**:

1. **dissolution of marriage**, including **khula**;
2. **dower**;
3. **maintenance**;
4. **restitution of conjugal rights**;
5. **custody of children** and the **visitation rights** of parents;
6. **guardianship**;
7. **jactitation of marriage** (a false claim that one is married to another);
8. **dowry**; and
9. **personal property and belongings of a wife**.

Civil courts' jurisdiction over these matters is ousted. The Family Court is deemed a **District Court** for the purposes of the **Guardians and Wards Act** (s. 25).

**Territorial jurisdiction:** under the Family Courts Rules 1965 (r. 6), a suit for **dissolution of marriage, dower or maintenance** may be filed where the **wife ordinarily resides** (a protection for the wife); other suits where the cause of action arose or the defendant resides.

## 10.3 Procedure — step by step

![Procedure in a Family Court suit](family_court_procedure.png)

1. **Plaint (s. 7)** — with a **schedule** listing the witnesses and a **précis of facts** (the substance of each witness's evidence), and the documents relied on. In a suit for dissolution, the plaint also states the dower and dowry, and any claim for their recovery may be joined.
2. **Summons (s. 8)** — served through the process server, by registered post, courier, and other modes including **publication**; service at the wife's or husband's address as stated.
3. **Written statement (s. 9)** — with a list of witnesses and précis; a husband sued for dissolution or maintenance may claim **restitution of conjugal rights** in his written statement; the wife may claim dower or dowry in reply to a restitution suit (joinder of claims arising from the marriage).
4. **Pre-trial hearing (s. 10)** — the Court examines the plaint, written statement and précis, **ascertains the points at issue** and **attempts to effect a compromise or reconciliation** between the parties, if possible.
5. **Khula at pre-trial (s. 10(4) proviso)** — in a suit for dissolution, **if reconciliation fails**, the Court "shall pass decree for dissolution of marriage **forthwith**" and order the wife to **restore the haq mehr received** at the time of marriage (Chapter 5).
6. **Issues and evidence (ss. 10(3), 11)** — if no compromise, the Court **frames issues** and records **evidence** of the witnesses in the schedule (others only with permission); evidence may be recorded in the form of affidavits.
7. **Second attempt at reconciliation (s. 12)** — after the close of evidence, the Court makes **another effort** at compromise or reconciliation before pronouncing judgment.
8. **Judgment and decree (ss. 12, 13)** — the Court must decide the case **within six months** of institution (s. 12A); the decree may direct payment in **instalments**; it is executed by the Family Court as a decree of a civil court.
9. **Interim maintenance (s. 17A)** — at any stage of a suit for maintenance, the Court shall fix **interim maintenance** payable by the **14th** of each month; on default, the defence may be **struck off** and the suit decreed.
10. **Decree of dissolution (s. 21)** — a certified copy is sent to the **Chairman** within **seven days**; the Chairman proceeds as if he had received notice of talaq, and the decree becomes **effective 90 days** after the copy is sent.

### Application of the CPC and the Qanun-e-Shahadat — s. 17

"The provisions of the Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984 and of the Code of Civil Procedure 1908, **except sections 10 and 11**, shall not apply to proceedings before any Family Court." So the Family Court follows its **own** simplified procedure; the rules of **res sub judice** (s. 10 CPC) and **res judicata** (s. 11 CPC) do apply. In practice, courts apply the **principles** of the CPC and QSO for justice and fair trial where the Act is silent, without being bound by their technicalities.

### Appeal — s. 14

1. An appeal lies to the **High Court** where the Family Court is a **District Judge or Additional District Judge**, and to the **District Judge** in other cases, within **30 days** (s. 14(1)).
2. **No appeal** lies against a decree (a) for **dissolution of marriage**, except dissolution under **DMMA s. 2(viii)(d)** (husband disposing of her property); (b) for **dower or dowry** not exceeding **Rs. 30,000**; (c) for **maintenance** of **Rs. 5,000 or less per month** (s. 14(2)).
3. **No appeal or revision** lies against an **interim order** (s. 14(3)).
4. Further challenge to the High Court is by way of a **constitutional petition** (Art. 199) where no appeal lies.

## 10.4 The significance of pre-trial reconciliation — the LHC 2023 question

1. **Quranic mandate** — "If you fear a breach between them, appoint an arbiter from his family and an arbiter from her family; if they desire reconciliation, Allah will bring about their reconciliation" (4:35).
2. **Statutory duty** — s. 10 (pre-trial) and s. 12 (post-evidence) **require** the Court to attempt reconciliation; it is not a formality.
3. **Benefits** — saves the marriage where possible; reduces acrimony; protects **children's welfare**; narrows the issues; resolves ancillary claims (dower, dowry, maintenance, custody) together; reduces the burden of successive suits and counter-claims.
4. **Danger of a perfunctory effort** — a mechanical "reconciliation failed" order encourages hostility, harms the minor, and multiplies litigation (claims and counter-claims for dowry, custody, maintenance). A meaningful attempt involves hearing both parties **in chambers**, involving family elders or a **mediator**, exploring practical arrangements, and recording the reasons for failure.
5. **Khula link** — because s. 10(4) requires a decree **forthwith** on failure of reconciliation, the quality of the reconciliation effort is the wife's main safeguard against hasty dissolution and the husband's main opportunity to save the marriage.

# Chapter 11 · Maxims and the great distinctions

## 11.1 Maxims of Muslim family law

## 11.2 The great distinctions

{w:25,37,38}
|Pair|First|Second|
|Valid vs irregular vs void marriage|Full effects|Irregular — curable defect; effects only after consummation, no inheritance / Void — no effect|
|Prompt vs deferred dower|Payable on demand; wife may refuse cohabitation before consummation; Art. 103|Payable on dissolution or agreed event; Art. 104|
|Specified vs proper dower|Fixed by agreement (minimum 10 dirhams)|By her father's family's dower and personal qualities|
|Talaq-e-ahsan vs hasan vs biddat|One pronouncement; revocable in iddat|Three in three tuhrs; irrevocable on third / Three at once — irrevocable (Hanafi)|
|Talaq vs khula|Husband's unilateral act; dower due|Wife's initiative; consideration to husband; court may decree|
|Khula vs mubarat|Aversion of the wife|Mutual aversion|
|Khula vs judicial dissolution (DMMA)|No fault needed; dower restored|Proof of a ground; dower preserved (s. 5)|
|Tafweez vs khula|Wife pronounces under delegated power; dower intact|Wife buys her release; dower returned|
|Iddat of divorce vs of death|Three courses / three months|Four months ten days; even before consummation|
|Guardianship vs custody|Legal authority — father|Physical care — mother (boy 7, girl puberty)|
|Legal vs de facto guardian of property|Father, executor, grandfather, executor, court — may alienate for necessity|No power to alienate immovables (*Imambandi*)|
|Court guardian (s. 29) vs testamentary guardian (s. 28)|Court's previous permission needed|Restrictions of the will; Court may permit|
|Arbitration Council (MFLO) vs Family Court|Administrative body: talaq notice, polygamy permission, maintenance certificate|Court: decrees in all Schedule matters|
