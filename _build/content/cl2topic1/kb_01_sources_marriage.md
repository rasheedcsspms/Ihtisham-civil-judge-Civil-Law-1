
# Chapter 1 · Sources, schools and the application of Muslim law in Pakistan

## 1.1 Sources of Muslim law

Muslim law (*Shariah* — "the path"; its human understanding is *fiqh*) is derived from four **primary** sources and several **secondary** ones.

{w:24,76}
|Source|Meaning and authority|
|**Quran**|The word of Allah — the first and supreme source. About 200 to 500 verses are legal (*ayat al-ahkam*), many on family law: marriage (4:3, 4:22–24), dower (4:4), divorce (2:228–232; 65:1–7), iddat (2:228, 2:234; 65:4), inheritance (4:11–12, 4:176).|
|**Sunnah / Hadith**|The practice and sayings of the Prophet (peace be upon him); explains and supplements the Quran (e.g., witnesses for nikah; the khula of the wife of Thabit bin Qays).|
|**Ijma**|Consensus of qualified jurists of the community on a legal question after the Prophet's time.|
|**Qiyas**|Analogical deduction — extending a rule to a new case having the same effective cause (*illah*). Accepted by Sunni schools; the Shia use **aql** (reason).|
|**Secondary sources**|*Istihsan* (juristic preference — Hanafi), *istislah* / *maslaha mursala* (public interest — Maliki), *urf* (custom, if not contrary to the texts), *ijtihad* (independent reasoning), **legislation** (MFLO, DMMA) and **judicial precedent** (Art. 189 of the Constitution).|

## 1.2 Schools (madhahib)

1. **Sunni**: **Hanafi** (Imam Abu Hanifa; the majority in Pakistan; his disciples **Abu Yusuf** and **Imam Muhammad** — the *Sahibain*), **Maliki** (Imam Malik), **Shafi'i** (Imam Shafi'i), **Hanbali** (Imam Ahmad bin Hanbal).
2. **Shia**: **Ithna Ashari / Jafari** (the main Shia school in Pakistan), **Ismaili**, **Zaidi**.

Each Muslim is governed by the law of **his or her own school**. In Pakistan, Hanafi law is applied unless the parties are Shia. Since **2021**, the MFLO expressly provides that the inheritance and divorce matters of **Ahl-e-Tashih** (Shia) are to be decided according to **Fiqh-e-Jafria** (MFLO ss. 4(8), 7(1A)), with recourse to a **Mujtahid-e-Alam** from a panel kept by the Council of Islamic Ideology, whose decision has the status of an arbitration award.

:::trap Classical texts cited in Pakistani courts
- **The Hedaya** (Hanafi, by Burhanuddin al-Marghinani, 12th century; translated by Hamilton) and **Fatawa-e-Alamgiri** (Hanafi digest compiled under Aurangzeb).
- **Sharaya-ul-Islam** (Shia).
- **Mulla, Principles of Mahomedan Law**; **Fyzee, Outlines of Muhammadan Law**; **Ameer Ali, Mohammedan Law**; **Tanzil-ur-Rahman, A Code of Muslim Personal Law**.
Courts cite these by paragraph (e.g., "Mulla, para 287"). Quote them as authorities in the exam.
:::

## 1.3 Application in Pakistan

1. **Shariat Application Act 1962, s. 2** — in the listed matters, "notwithstanding any custom or usage", the rule of decision is the **Muslim personal law (Shariat)** where the parties are Muslims, **subject to any enactment**. Section 3 **terminated limited estates** of Muslim females under customary law; ss. 4–5 dealt with successive legatees under wills.
2. **MFLO s. 3** — "The provisions of this Ordinance shall have effect **notwithstanding any law, custom or usage**." The MFLO therefore overrides classical rules where they conflict (e.g., the 90-day rule for talaq; the orphaned grandchild's share).
3. **Constitution, Art. 227** — all existing laws are to be brought into conformity with the Injunctions of Islam; **Art. 203D** — the **Federal Shariat Court** may examine whether a law is repugnant to Islam. **Art. 203B(c)** excludes "**Muslim personal law**" from the FSC's jurisdiction; the Supreme Court has read this as the personal law of each sect **as interpreted by its own jurists**, so **statutes** such as the MFLO **can** be examined by the FSC (*Dr. Mahmood-ur-Rahman Faisal v. Government of Pakistan*, PLD 1994 SC 607).

:::case Allah Rakha v. Federation of Pakistan (PLD 2000 FSC 1)
__Background.__ About thirty-seven petitions under Art. 203D challenged ss. 4 to 7 of the MFLO as repugnant to the Injunctions of Islam.

__Held (Federal Shariat Court).__
1. **Section 4** (orphaned grandchildren inherit their predeceased parent's share) — **repugnant** to the Injunctions of Islam, since under the Quranic scheme the nearer excludes the remoter (the remedy suggested was an obligatory bequest — *wasiyyat wajibah* — or other provision for them).
2. **Section 5** (registration of marriages) — **not** repugnant; registration is a useful administrative measure and does not affect validity.
3. **Section 6** (polygamy with the Arbitration Council's permission) — **not** repugnant; it regulates, and does not prohibit, a second marriage.
4. **Section 7(3) and (5)** (talaq not effective until 90 days after notice to the Chairman; pregnancy) — **repugnant**, as talaq takes effect according to its own terms under Islamic law and the notice requirement cannot postpone it; the penal sanction for not giving notice was not itself struck down.

__Status.__ Appeals were filed before the **Shariat Appellate Bench of the Supreme Court** (2000) and are reported to be **pending**. Under Art. 203D(2) and 203F, a decision under appeal **does not take effect** until the appeal is decided. The **MFLO therefore continues to be applied as enacted**, and courts in Islamabad apply ss. 4 and 7 in full.

__Exam use.__ The KPHC 2026 paper asked candidates to "critically evaluate" the MFLO with reference to this case. Explain each section, the FSC's view, the pending appeal and its effect, and give your own reasoned evaluation (e.g., the welfare objects of ss. 6 and 7; the hardship to orphaned grandchildren that s. 4 cures).
:::

:::recap Chapter 1 in five lines
- Sources: Quran, Sunnah, Ijma, Qiyas; secondary — istihsan, istislah, urf, ijtihad, legislation, precedent.
- Schools: Hanafi (majority), Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali; Shia Jafari (MFLO ss. 4(8), 7(1A) since 2021).
- Shariat Application Act 1962, s. 2 — personal law governs Muslims notwithstanding custom, subject to enactments.
- MFLO overrides any law, custom or usage (s. 3); Art. 227; FSC may examine statutes (*Mahmood-ur-Rahman Faisal*).
- *Allah Rakha* (PLD 2000 FSC 1): ss. 4 and 7(3), (5) repugnant; ss. 5, 6 not; appeal pending — MFLO applied as enacted.

# Chapter 2 · Marriage (nikah)

## 2.1 Meaning and nature

Marriage (*nikah*) is a **contract** for the legalisation of sexual intercourse and the procreation and legitimation of children (Hedaya). It is also a **sacred** institution and an act of **ibadah** (worship).

:::case Abdul Kadir v. Salima ((1886) ILR 8 All 149, Mahmood J)
__Held.__ "Marriage among Muhammadans is **not a sacrament, but purely a civil contract**." Its incidents — consent, consideration (dower), dissolution, enforcement of rights — are those of a contract. A husband could sue for **restitution of conjugal rights** although prompt dower was unpaid, once consummation had taken place.

__Note.__ The modern view combines both aspects: a **contract** with **religious** and **social** dimensions (*Anis Begum v. Muhammad Istafa Wali Khan*, AIR 1933 All 634 — Sulaiman CJ). Pakistani courts stress its contractual basis (consent, mahr, terms in the nikahnama) and its sanctity.
:::

## 2.2 Essentials of a valid marriage

1. **Proposal and acceptance (ijab and qabul)** — by or on behalf of the parties, **at one meeting** (*ittihad-e-majlis*), in words that show a present intention to marry. A marriage contracted for a fixed period (*muta*) is void under Sunni law.
2. **Competence of the parties** — each must be **Muslim** (subject to the kitabia rule), of **sound mind**, and have attained **puberty** (*bulugh*; presumed at 15 under classical Hanafi law). A minor or lunatic can be married by his or her **guardian** (*wali*) — subject now to the **ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025** (2.6).
3. **Free consent** — consent obtained by **force or fraud** makes the marriage invalid unless ratified. An **adult** Muslim woman's own consent is essential and sufficient.
4. **Witnesses** — under **Hanafi** law, **two adult Muslim male witnesses, or one male and two females**, must be present and hear the proposal and acceptance. Absence of witnesses makes the marriage **irregular** (*fasid*), not void. Under **Shia** law witnesses are not required.
5. **No legal prohibition** — the parties must not be within the prohibited relationships (2.4).
6. **Dower** — an incident, not a condition: if not fixed, the wife is entitled to **proper dower** (Chapter 3).

:::case Hafiz Abdul Waheed v. Mrs. Asma Jahangir (PLD 2004 SC 219 — the "Saima Waheed case")
__Facts.__ An adult woman, Saima Waheed, married without her father's consent. The father sought to have the marriage declared invalid for want of the **wali's** consent.

__Held.__ Under the Hanafi school, an **adult Muslim woman (sui juris)** may contract her own marriage **without the consent of her wali**. The marriage was valid. (The Court noted the moral and social desirability of parental involvement, but this does not affect validity.)

__Ratio.__ The consent of the **parties**, not of their guardians, is the essence of a Muslim marriage between adults.
:::

## 2.3 Guardians for marriage (wilayat al-ijbar) and the option of puberty

**Who may contract a minor's marriage (Hanafi order):** (1) the **father**; (2) the father's father, how high soever; (3) the brother and other male relations on the father's side in the order of inheritance; (4) the **mother**; (5) maternal relations; (6) the **State / Qazi**.

**Option of puberty (khiyar-ul-bulugh).** A minor married by a guardian may **repudiate** the marriage on attaining puberty. In Pakistan this is codified in **DMMA s. 2(vii)**:

> "that she, having been given in marriage by her father or other guardian before she attained the age of **sixteen** years, repudiated the marriage before attaining the age of **eighteen** years: Provided that the marriage has **not been consummated**."

Points: (i) it applies to marriages by **any** guardian, including the father; (ii) the repudiation must be made **before 18**; (iii) the marriage must not have been **consummated** (consummation with her consent after puberty ends the option); (iv) she must obtain a **decree** from the Family Court.

## 2.4 Prohibited relationships

{w:24,40,36}
|Kind|Who|Effect|
|**Consanguinity** (blood)|Ascendants, descendants, descendants of parents (brothers, sisters, nephews, nieces), immediate children of grandparents (uncles, aunts) — Quran 4:23|**Void** (*batil*)|
|**Affinity** (marriage)|Wife's ascendants; wife's descendants (if the marriage with the wife was consummated); father's or son's wife|**Void**|
|**Fosterage** (*rida'a*)|Persons related through suckling within the first two years — the foster-mother and those related through her (with the recognised Hanafi exceptions)|**Void**|
|**Unlawful conjunction**|Two women so related that, if one were a man, they could not marry — e.g., **two sisters**, aunt and niece, at the same time|**Irregular** (Hanafi); void (Shia)|
|**A fifth wife**|While four marriages subsist (or one is in iddat)|**Irregular**|
|**Absence of witnesses**|Hanafi|**Irregular**|
|**Difference of religion**|A Muslim **man** may marry a **kitabia** (Christian or Jew), but not an idolater or fire-worshipper (irregular in Hanafi law); a Muslim **woman** may marry **only a Muslim** — Pakistani courts treat her marriage with a non-Muslim as **void**|Irregular / void|
|**Woman undergoing iddat**|—|**Irregular** (Hanafi); void (Shia)|
|**Triply divorced wife**|Cannot remarry the same husband without an intervening consummated marriage with another (**halala**) — but see **MFLO s. 7(6)**|Irregular / void|
|**Polyandry**|A married woman with another man|**Void** (and an offence)|

**MFLO s. 7(6):** "Nothing shall debar a wife whose marriage has been terminated by talaq effective under this section from re-marrying the same husband, without an intervening marriage with a third person **unless such termination is for the third time** so effective." So the **halala** requirement applies only after a **third** effective talaq.

## 2.5 Classification of marriages — valid, irregular, void

{w:18,27,27,28}
|Point|Valid (*sahih*)|Irregular (*fasid*)|Void (*batil*)|
|Defect|None|A **temporary or curable** impediment (no witnesses, fifth wife, unlawful conjunction, iddat, kitabi-idolater difference)|A **permanent** impediment (consanguinity, affinity, fosterage, another's wife)|
|Before consummation|Full effects|**No effect**; may be terminated by either party by words|No effect at all|
|After consummation|Full effects|Wife gets **dower** (proper or specified, whichever is less) and observes **iddat**; children **legitimate**; **no mutual inheritance**|No dower (in principle), no iddat; children illegitimate|
|Inheritance|Mutual|None between spouses|None|

## 2.6 Statutory regulation

### (1) Registration — MFLO s. 5

"Every marriage solemnized under Muslim Law **shall be registered**." The Union Council licenses **Nikah Registrars**; a marriage solemnised by someone else must be **reported** to the Nikah Registrar by the person who solemnised it, on pain of **three months' simple imprisonment** or **Rs. 1,000** fine, or both (s. 5(3)–(4)). **Non-registration does not invalidate** the marriage; it remains valid and may be proved by other evidence (upheld in *Allah Rakha*). The **nikahnama** (Form II under the West Pakistan Muslim Family Laws Rules 1961) records dower, its mode of payment, delegation of the right of divorce (clause 18), restrictions on the husband's right of divorce, and other conditions (clauses 19–20).

### (2) Polygamy — MFLO s. 6

1. **No man**, during an existing marriage, shall contract another marriage **except with the previous written permission of the Arbitration Council**, and a marriage without permission shall **not be registered** (s. 6(1)).
2. The application to the **Chairman** must state the **reasons** and whether the **existing wife or wives consent** (s. 6(2)).
3. The Arbitration Council may grant permission if satisfied that the proposed marriage is **"necessary and just"** (s. 6(3)), recording reasons; revision lies to the **Collector** (s. 6(4)).
4. **Consequences of contravention** (s. 6(5)): (a) the husband must **pay immediately the entire dower**, prompt or deferred, of the existing wife or wives — recoverable as **arrears of land revenue**; and (b) on **complaint**, simple imprisonment up to **one year**, or fine up to **Rs. 5,000**, or both.
5. The **second marriage remains valid** in Muslim law (the MFLO does not declare it void), but the existing wife gets a ground for **dissolution** — **DMMA s. 2(iia)**: "that the husband has taken an additional wife in contravention of the provisions of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961".

### (3) Child marriage — ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025

1. A **child** is a person under **18**, male or female (s. 2(a)); a **child marriage** is one where either party is a child (s. 2(e)).
2. **No Nikah Registrar shall register** a marriage where a party is under 18, and must verify **CNICs** with dates of birth (s. 3) — penalty up to one year and Rs. 100,000 fine.
3. An adult male who contracts a child marriage — **rigorous imprisonment of two to three years** and fine (s. 4); **cohabitation** resulting from a child marriage is **child abuse** — five to seven years and a fine of not less than Rs. 1 million (s. 5); **child trafficking** for marriage (s. 6); a **parent or guardian** who promotes, permits or negligently fails to prevent a child marriage — two to three years (s. 7), with a presumption against the person in charge of the child.
4. Only the **Court of Sessions** tries these offences (s. 8); it may issue an **injunction** prohibiting an intended child marriage (s. 9); offences are **cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable** (s. 10); trial within **90 days** (s. 11). The **Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929** is repealed for the ICT (s. 13).
5. **Validity.** Like the 1929 Act, the 2025 Act **punishes** child marriage but does not expressly declare it **void**; the validity of such a marriage is governed by Muslim law (with the minor's option of puberty — DMMA s. 2(vii)).

## 2.7 Effects of a valid marriage

1. Cohabitation becomes lawful; children are **legitimate**.
2. The wife is entitled to **dower** (Chapter 3) and **maintenance** (Chapter 7).
3. **Mutual rights of inheritance** arise (Topic 2).
4. **Prohibited degrees of affinity** arise.
5. The wife must observe **iddat** on dissolution (Chapter 6).
6. The wife **retains her separate legal personality** and **property**; the husband acquires no rights over her property.
7. **Restitution of conjugal rights** — either spouse who withdraws from the society of the other without lawful cause may be sued in the Family Court; non-payment of **prompt dower** is a good defence for the wife before consummation (and, in Pakistani practice, a ground the courts weigh afterwards too); **cruelty**, **non-maintenance**, or an **unregistered second marriage** may also defeat the husband's suit.

## 2.8 Muta (temporary marriage)

A marriage for a **fixed period** for a fixed dower, recognised only in **Ithna Ashari** (Shia) law. No mutual inheritance (unless agreed); the wife is not entitled to maintenance (unless agreed); no divorce — it ends on expiry of the term; children are legitimate. It is **void** in Sunni law.

:::recap Chapter 2 in eight lines
- Nikah: a civil contract (*Abdul Kadir v. Salima*) with sacred character; proposal and acceptance at one meeting.
- Essentials: competent parties (Muslim, sane, pubescent), free consent, two witnesses (Hanafi), no prohibition; dower an incident.
- Adult woman may marry without wali (*Hafiz Abdul Waheed v. Asma Jahangir*, PLD 2004 SC 219).
- Guardians for marriage: father, grandfather, agnates, mother…; option of puberty — DMMA s. 2(vii): married before 16, repudiate before 18, no consummation.
- Prohibitions: consanguinity, affinity, fosterage (void); conjunction, fifth wife, no witnesses, iddat, religion (irregular); halala only after the third talaq (MFLO s. 7(6)).
- Valid / irregular / void — effects on dower, iddat, legitimacy and inheritance.
- MFLO s. 5 registration (not affecting validity); s. 6 polygamy — permission, whole dower payable, punishment, DMMA s. 2(iia).
- ICT Child Marriage Restraint Act 2025: 18 for both; punishments; Sessions Court; injunction; 90-day trial.
:::

# Chapter 3 · Dower (mahr)

## 3.1 Meaning and nature

**Dower (mahr)** is a sum of money or other property which the wife is entitled to receive from the husband **in consideration of the marriage** (Mulla). It is ordained by the Quran: "And give the women (on marriage) their dower as a free gift" (4:4).

**Purpose:** (i) a mark of **respect** for the wife; (ii) a **check** on the husband's arbitrary power of divorce; (iii) **financial security** for the wife after dissolution.

:::maxim "Dower is a debt"
Dower is not the price of the wife; it is an **obligation imposed by law** on the husband as a token of respect, and once fixed (or once proper dower is due) it is a **debt** owed to the wife, ranking with other unsecured debts of the husband and recoverable from his estate after death (*Hamira Bibi v. Zubaida Bibi* (1916) 43 IA 294, PC). This is the theme of the KPHC 2026 question.
:::

## 3.2 Kinds of dower

![Kinds of dower](dower_kinds.png)

### (1) Specified dower (mahr-i-musamma)

Fixed by agreement, before, at, or after the marriage. Under **Hanafi** law the minimum is **ten dirhams**; there is **no maximum** (the husband may fix any amount, even beyond his means — but courts look at good faith). The husband may increase it after the marriage. A guardian may fix the dower of a minor, binding on the minor.

Specified dower is either:

1. **Prompt (mu'ajjal)** — payable **immediately on demand** after the marriage; or
2. **Deferred (mu'wajjal)** — payable on **dissolution** of the marriage by death or divorce, or on a specified event. The wife cannot demand deferred dower during the marriage (unless the event occurs), but interest in it **vests** at marriage.

**MFLO s. 10:** "Where **no details about the mode of payment** of dower are specified in the nikah nama, or the marriage contract, the **entire amount** of the dower shall be presumed to be **payable on demand**." (Classical Hanafi law divided it according to custom; s. 10 replaces that rule in Pakistan.)

### (2) Proper or customary dower (mahr-ul-misl)

Where no dower is fixed (or the fixation is invalid), the wife is entitled to **proper dower**, determined by reference to the dower of **women of her father's family** (sisters, paternal aunts), and her **age, beauty, fortune, understanding and virtue**, and the social position of her family. The right exists **even if** the marriage contract excluded dower.

## 3.3 Rights of the wife and widow to secure her dower

1. **Refusal of cohabitation.** Before **consummation**, a wife may **refuse to live with her husband** until **prompt dower** is paid; the husband's suit for restitution of conjugal rights then fails (*Nasra Begum v. Rizwan Ali*, AIR 1980 All 119). After consummation, the classical position was that she could not refuse (*Abdul Kadir v. Salima*); courts in Pakistan still consider non-payment a relevant factor in refusing restitution.
2. **Suit for recovery** — a suit in the **Family Court** (Family Courts Act, Schedule). A wife may sue for prompt dower at any time during the marriage, and for deferred dower on dissolution.
3. **Dower as a debt against the husband's estate** — after his death, the widow (and her heirs if she dies) may claim the dower as a **debt** from his estate, **before** distribution among heirs (Topic 2 — debts are paid before inheritance). Her claim is unsecured, ranking with other creditors (*Hamira Bibi*).
4. **Widow's right of retention.** A widow who has **lawfully obtained possession** of her husband's property (with his or his heirs' consent, not by force or fraud) in lieu of unpaid dower is entitled to **retain** it until her dower is paid. The right: (i) does **not** give her **title** to the property; (ii) she cannot **alienate** it (*Maina Bibi v. Chaudhri Vakil Ahmad* (1925) 52 IA 145, PC — her transfer of the property did not bind the heirs); (iii) she must **account** for the income; (iv) it is **heritable** in the sense that it passes to her heirs with the debt; (v) it gives no priority over other creditors.
5. **Polygamy without permission** — the entire dower, prompt and deferred, becomes payable **at once** and is recoverable as **arrears of land revenue** (MFLO s. 6(5)(a)).
6. **DMMA s. 5** — a decree of dissolution under the Act does not affect the wife's right to dower.

## 3.4 Confirmation of dower

The whole specified dower becomes **due** (is confirmed) on: (i) **consummation**; (ii) a **valid retirement** (*khilwat-us-sahiha* — the spouses alone together in circumstances where there is no legal, moral or physical impediment to intercourse); (iii) the **death** of either spouse (even before consummation).

**Divorce before consummation or valid retirement:** the wife gets **half** the specified dower (Quran 2:237); if no dower was specified, she gets a present (*mut'a* — e.g., three articles of dress).

## 3.5 Dower on dissolution

{w:30,70}
|Mode of dissolution|Dower|
|**Talaq** after consummation|Entire dower (prompt and deferred) becomes due.|
|**Talaq** before consummation|Half the specified dower (or a present if none specified).|
|**Death**|Entire dower due (confirmed by death).|
|**Khula**|The wife usually **returns** dower or other benefits as consideration; in a Family Court decree under s. 10(4) Family Courts Act, the court directs **restoration of the haq mehr received** "in consideration of marriage at the time of marriage" (Chapter 5).|
|**Mubarat**|As agreed; usually mutual release.|
|**Judicial dissolution under the DMMA**|Dower **not affected** (s. 5) — the wife keeps her right to dower, unless the court, in a khula-type decree, orders otherwise.|
|**Apostasy**|DMMA s. 4: a woman's apostasy does not by itself dissolve the marriage.|

## 3.6 Limitation for recovery of dower

{w:24,22,54}
|Dower|Period|Time runs from (Limitation Act, First Schedule)|
|**Prompt (exigible) — Art. 103**|Three years|"When the dower is **demanded and refused** or (where, during the continuance of the marriage no such demand has been made) when the marriage is **dissolved by death or divorce**."|
|**Deferred — Art. 104**|Three years|"When the marriage is **dissolved by death or divorce**."|

So a wife who never demanded her prompt dower during the marriage does not lose it; time starts only on dissolution. Once she **demands** and the husband **refuses**, time runs from the refusal.

## 3.7 Dower, dowry and bridal gifts distinguished

- **Dower (mahr)** — from the husband to the wife; an obligation of Muslim law.
- **Dowry (jahez)** — property given by the **bride's parents** to the bride in connection with marriage.
- **Bridal gifts (bari)** — gifts from the bridegroom or his family to the bride.

Under the **Dowry and Bridal Gifts (Restriction) Act 1976**, the dowry, bridal gifts and presents given to the bride **vest absolutely** in her, and her property in them may not be restricted; the Act also limits their value and requires lists to be prepared and delivered to the Registrar. A wife may recover dowry articles in the **Family Court** (Schedule: "dowry" and "personal property and belongings of a wife").

:::eg The nikahnama condition — LHC 2023
A nikahnama provides that, if the husband divorces the wife, he will pay her Rs. 2 million as **compensation**. Is this a **clog** on his right of talaq (and so void), or a valid agreement?
1. Muslim law permits **conditions** in the marriage contract that are not contrary to the purpose of marriage or the Shariah; a condition that **destroys** the husband's right of talaq would be invalid, but one that merely **makes divorce costly** is generally upheld as a form of **additional dower or financial security** for the wife.
2. The prevailing approach of Pakistani courts is to treat such a stipulation as part of the wife's **dower or a contractual entitlement** in her favour, recoverable in the **Family Court**, unless it is **oppressive** or **penal** (Contract Act s. 74 by analogy) or contrary to public policy. The Family Court has jurisdiction because the claim arises out of the marriage contract and the nikahnama.
3. A better-drafted answer distinguishes: (i) a fixed sum stated as **deferred dower payable on divorce** — enforceable as dower; (ii) a **penalty** designed only to prevent divorce — may be reduced or refused as a clog; (iii) conditions in clauses 17–19 of the nikahnama (delegated right of divorce, restrictions on polygamy) — valid.
:::

- Dower: money or property due to the wife in consideration of marriage (Quran 4:4); respect, check on divorce, security.
- "Dower is a debt" — unsecured debt against the husband and his estate (*Hamira Bibi*).
- Specified (minimum 10 dirhams Hanafi; prompt or deferred) and proper dower (by her father's family and personal qualities).
- MFLO s. 10 — where mode of payment not specified, the **whole** dower is payable **on demand**.

- Confirmed by consummation, valid retirement or death; half on divorce before consummation.
- Polygamy without permission — entire dower at once, as land revenue (MFLO s. 6(5)).
- Limitation: prompt — 3 years from demand and refusal, or dissolution (Art. 103); deferred — 3 years from dissolution (Art. 104). Dowry and bridal gifts vest in the bride (Act of 1976).
:::
