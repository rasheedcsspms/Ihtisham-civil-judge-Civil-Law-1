
# Chapter 4 · Talaq — divorce by the husband, and the MFLO procedure

## 4.1 Dissolution of marriage — the map

A Muslim marriage may be dissolved in the following ways:

![Modes of dissolution of a Muslim marriage](dissolution_modes.png)

1. **By death** of either spouse.
2. **By the husband** — **talaq**; and the rare forms **ila** (vow of continence for four months or more) and **zihar** (comparing the wife to a prohibited female relation).
3. **By the wife, under a delegated power** — **talaq-e-tafweez**.
4. **By mutual agreement** — **khula** (at the wife's instance, for consideration) and **mubarat** (mutual release).
5. **By judicial decree** — under the **DMMA 1939** (including **khula** decreed by the Family Court, and **lian**).
6. **By operation of law** — e.g., discovery of a void marriage; in classical law, apostasy of the husband (but see DMMA s. 4 for the wife).

:::maxim "Of all lawful things, the most hateful to Allah is divorce" (Hadith, Abu Dawud)
Islam permits divorce as a necessary remedy for a failed marriage, but discourages its abuse. The Quran prescribes **arbitration** before divorce (4:35) and **iddat** as a period of reflection (65:1). The MFLO's notice and **Arbitration Council** reflect the same policy.
:::

## 4.2 Talaq — meaning and capacity

**Talaq** means "release" — the repudiation of the marriage by the husband, by words or in writing, without any need to give reasons or to go to court.

**Capacity (Hanafi):** a husband who is **adult** (has attained puberty) and of **sound mind**. Classical Hanafi law holds valid even a talaq pronounced under **compulsion**, in **jest**, or in voluntary **intoxication**; **Shia** law requires a free intention and the presence of witnesses. For **Fiqh-e-Jafria**, the MFLO (2021 proviso to s. 7(1)) now provides that the man may pronounce talaq himself or through a duly authorised **vakil**, "uttering in literal Arabic words (*seegha*) in the physical presence of at least **two witnesses** qualifying the requirements of clause (1) of Article 17 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat"; and that the pronouncement "shall be **ineffective if it is done jokingly or under anger, intoxication, insanity, duress or coercion** of any kind".

Pakistani courts have increasingly refused to give effect to a talaq proved to have been obtained by **coercion**, or pronounced in a state in which the husband did not understand what he was saying, treating the **intention** of the husband as decisive.

## 4.3 Forms and modes of talaq

**Forms:** (i) **express** (*sarih*) — clear words ("I divorce you"), effective without proof of intention; (ii) **implied / ambiguous** (*kinaya*) — words capable of other meanings ("you are free"), effective only if **intention** is proved; (iii) **oral** or **written** (*talaqnama*) — a written talaq takes effect as the deed states (subject to the MFLO).

**Modes:**

{w:22,46,32}
|Mode|Method|Revocability|
|**Talaq-e-ahsan** (most approved)|A **single** pronouncement during a period of purity (*tuhr*) in which there has been no intercourse, followed by abstinence during the **iddat**|**Revocable** during iddat (by words or resumption of cohabitation); becomes final on expiry of iddat|
|**Talaq-e-hasan** (approved)|**Three** pronouncements in **three successive tuhrs**, without intercourse in between|Revocable after the first and second; **irrevocable** on the **third** — remarriage only after halala|
|**Talaq-ul-biddat** (innovative / "triple talaq")|**Three** pronouncements **at one time** ("I divorce you, I divorce you, I divorce you"), or one pronouncement clearly intended to be irrevocable|**Irrevocable at once** in classical Hanafi law (*Rashid Ahmad v. Anisa Khatun* (1932) 59 IA 21, PC); **not recognised** as three by Shia law|

:::case Rashid Ahmad v. Anisa Khatun ((1932) 59 IA 21, Privy Council)
__Facts.__ A husband pronounced three talaqs in the absence of his wife and executed a talaqnama, but afterwards continued to live with her and treated her as his wife; children were born.

__Held.__ Under Hanafi law, a **triple talaq** is effective and **irrevocable** from the moment of pronouncement, even though it is sinful (*biddat*). The later cohabitation could not revive the marriage without an intervening marriage of the wife with another man; the children were held illegitimate.

__Pakistan today.__ The MFLO has changed the practical result: under **s. 7(3)** no talaq is effective until **90 days** after notice to the Chairman, and it may be **revoked** in that period; under **s. 7(6)** the wife may remarry the same husband without halala "unless such termination is for the **third time** so effective". A "triple" pronouncement made at one time produces **one** termination under s. 7, so it does not by itself bar remarriage without halala.
:::

### Ila and zihar

- **Ila** — the husband swears not to have intercourse with his wife for **four months or more** and keeps the vow: under Hanafi law the marriage is dissolved on the expiry of four months (Quran 2:226–227).
- **Zihar** — the husband compares his wife to a woman within his **prohibited degrees** (e.g., "you are to me as my mother's back"): he may not resume cohabitation until he performs **expiation** (*kaffara*); the wife may seek dissolution (DMMA s. 2(ix)).

## 4.4 The procedure under MFLO s. 7

> "7. (1) Any man who wishes to divorce his wife shall, **as soon as may be after the pronouncement of talaq in any form whatsoever**, give the **Chairman notice in writing** of his having done so, and shall supply a **copy** thereof to the wife."

![The talaq procedure under section 7 of the MFLO](talaq_procedure.png)

1. **Pronouncement** of talaq in any form (oral, written, single, triple).
2. **Written notice** to the **Chairman** of the Union Council (in the ICT, the Chairman under the **ICT Local Government Act 2015**, or a person appointed by the Government), with a **copy to the wife**.
3. **Penalty for not giving notice** — simple imprisonment up to **one year**, or fine up to **Rs. 5,000**, or both (s. 7(2)).
4. **Arbitration Council** — within **30 days** of receiving the notice, the Chairman shall constitute an Arbitration Council (the Chairman and a representative of each party — s. 2(a)) "for the purpose of bringing about a **reconciliation**", and it "shall take all steps necessary" for that purpose (s. 7(4)).
5. **Effectiveness** — "Save as provided in sub-section (5), a talaq **unless revoked earlier, expressly or otherwise** shall not be effective until the expiration of **ninety days** from the day on which notice under sub-section (1) is delivered to the Chairman" (s. 7(3)).
6. **Pregnancy** — "If the wife be pregnant at the time talaq is pronounced, talaq shall not be effective until the period mentioned in sub-section (3) **or the pregnancy, whichever be later**, ends" (s. 7(5)).
7. **Revocation** — the husband may revoke the talaq within the 90 days, **expressly or otherwise** (e.g., by resuming cohabitation).
8. **Remarriage of the same parties** — permitted without halala, unless this is the **third** effective termination (s. 7(6)).
9. **Certificate** — after 90 days, the Union Council issues a **certificate of effectiveness of divorce**, which is the usual proof of divorce for NADRA and remarriage.
10. **Section 8** applies the same procedure, *mutatis mutandis*, to **talaq-e-tafweez** and to dissolution "otherwise than by talaq" (khula, mubarat).

### Effect of failure to give notice

1. The husband is liable to **punishment** (s. 7(2)).
2. On **validity**, the superior courts have not spoken with one voice. The predominant view is that, the MFLO having overriding effect (s. 3), a talaq of which **no notice** is given to the Chairman **does not become effective** under s. 7(3), and the marriage subsists until notice is given and 90 days pass. Another line of decisions — reflected in the FSC's view in *Allah Rakha* that s. 7(3) is repugnant to Islam — treats a talaq **proved** to have been pronounced as effective despite the absence of notice. As the FSC judgment is under appeal and not in effect, the safer statement of the law in the exam is: **the date on which talaq becomes effective is reckoned from the notice to the Chairman**, and the courts look at the whole conduct of the parties (e.g., an admitted talaq followed by long separation).
3. The **Arbitration Council's** failure to meet or to attempt reconciliation does **not** prevent the talaq from becoming effective after 90 days.

## 4.5 Talaq-e-tafweez (delegated divorce)

The husband may **delegate** his power of talaq to the wife (or to a third person), absolutely or on conditions — e.g., if he marries again without her consent, or fails to maintain her. In Pakistan this is done through **clause 18** of the nikahnama ("whether the husband has delegated the power of divorce to the wife, and if so, under what conditions").

1. The delegation, once made, is **irrevocable** by the husband.
2. The wife exercises it by **pronouncing** talaq on herself; the marriage is dissolved as if the husband had pronounced it — **without** any court decree.
3. The **MFLO s. 8** procedure applies: she must give **notice to the Chairman**, and the dissolution takes effect after **90 days** (s. 7(3) applied *mutatis mutandis*).
4. The wife does **not** lose her **dower**, since the dissolution is in law by the husband's act (through his agent).

## 4.6 Consequences of divorce

1. **Cohabitation** becomes unlawful after the talaq becomes effective (in practice, after the 90 days).
2. The wife must observe **iddat** (Chapter 6), unless the marriage was not consummated.
3. **Dower** — the whole (if consummated) becomes **immediately payable**; half if not consummated.
4. **Maintenance** — during iddat (Hanafi), not afterwards; children's maintenance continues.
5. **Mutual rights of inheritance** cease once the talaq is effective (a wife divorced by a husband on his **death-bed** — *marz-ul-maut* — to deprive her of inheritance may still inherit if he dies during her iddat: Hanafi rule).
6. **Remarriage** — the wife may remarry another after the talaq is effective and iddat is over; the same husband — subject to s. 7(6).
7. **Custody** of children — governed by the rules of **hizanat** (Chapter 9).

:::recap Chapter 4 in eight lines
- Dissolution by death, by husband (talaq, ila, zihar), delegated (tafweez), agreement (khula, mubarat), court (DMMA), operation of law.
- Talaq: adult, sane husband; express / implied; oral / written; Fiqh-e-Jafria — Arabic words, two witnesses, ineffective in jest, anger, intoxication, duress (MFLO s. 7(1) proviso, 2021).
- Modes: ahsan (single, revocable in iddat), hasan (three tuhrs), biddat (triple — irrevocable in Hanafi law: *Rashid Ahmad v. Anisa Khatun*).
- MFLO s. 7: notice to Chairman + copy to wife; Arbitration Council within 30 days; effective after 90 days unless revoked; pregnancy — later of the two; penalty up to one year / Rs. 5,000.
- s. 7(6): remarriage without halala unless third effective termination — a triple pronouncement counts as one termination.
- Non-notice: penal; effectiveness reckoned from notice (predominant view); *Allah Rakha* contrary but under appeal.
- Tafweez: clause 18 nikahnama; wife pronounces; s. 8 procedure; dower intact.
- Consequences: iddat, dower due, iddat maintenance, inheritance ends (except marz-ul-maut), remarriage, custody.
:::

# Chapter 5 · Dissolution at the wife's instance — khula, mubarat and the DMMA

## 5.1 Khula

**Khula** (from *khal'* — to put off, as a garment: "they are your garments and you are their garments", Quran 2:187) is a dissolution of marriage at the instance of the **wife**, in return for a **consideration** (usually the return of dower or other benefits) paid to the husband.

**Quranic basis:** "If you fear that they would not be able to keep the limits ordained by Allah, there is no blame on either of them if she gives something for her freedom" (2:229). **Sunnah:** the wife of **Thabit bin Qays** came to the Prophet and said she found no fault in his character or religion but could not live with him; the Prophet asked, "Will you return his garden (the dower)?" She agreed, and the Prophet directed Thabit to accept the garden and divorce her.

### Is the husband's consent necessary?

Classical Hanafi texts treat khula as a **contract** requiring the husband's acceptance. Pakistani courts have held that the **court** may decree khula **without** the husband's consent, where it is satisfied that the spouses cannot live together within the limits prescribed by Allah.

:::case Mst. Balqis Fatima v. Najm-ul-Ikram Qureshi (PLD 1959 Lahore 566, Full Bench)
__Held.__ A wife is entitled to dissolution by **khula** through the court if the court is satisfied that, in the circumstances, the parties **cannot live together in harmony** and within the **limits of Allah**; the husband's consent is **not** necessary, and the wife must restore what she received (consideration). The ground is covered by **DMMA s. 2(ix)** ("any other ground which is recognised as valid for the dissolution of marriages under Muslim law").
:::

:::case Mst. Khurshid Bibi v. Baboo Muhammad Amin (PLD 1967 SC 97)
__Held.__ The Supreme Court affirmed that a Muslim wife is entitled to **khula as of right** if she satisfies the court that she would otherwise be forced into a hateful union; she need not prove any fault of the husband. The court's role is to be satisfied that the "limits of Allah" cannot be kept, and to fix the consideration (ordinarily the return of the dower received). An aversion so strong that cohabitation is impossible is enough.

__Ratio.__ Khula is the wife's **right**, enforceable by the court, corresponding to the husband's right of talaq.
:::

### Khula in the Family Court — s. 10(4), Family Courts Act

By the 2002 amendment (applicable in the ICT), in a suit for dissolution of marriage, **if reconciliation fails at the pre-trial stage**, "the Family Court **shall pass decree for dissolution of marriage forthwith** and shall also **restore to the husband the haq mehr received by the wife** in consideration of marriage at the time of marriage". So a wife who seeks khula need not go through a full trial: if the pre-trial reconciliation fails, the decree follows, conditional on restoring the dower **received** (not unpaid deferred dower, which she simply forgoes).

### Effect of khula

1. It operates as a **single irrevocable (bain) talaq** (Hanafi).
2. The wife must observe **iddat**.
3. She loses the dower she agreed (or was ordered) to return, and any **deferred** dower claim she gave up.
4. **Children's** maintenance and custody are not affected by the consideration; a khula agreement cannot validly waive the children's right of maintenance.
5. A certified copy of the decree is sent to the **Chairman** within **seven days**, and the decree becomes **effective after 90 days** from the day the copy is sent (s. 21, Family Courts Act).

## 5.2 Mubarat

**Mubarat** is dissolution by **mutual consent** where **both** desire separation (mutual aversion). The offer may come from either side; on acceptance it operates as an irrevocable divorce. Usually each releases the other's claims. The **MFLO s. 8** procedure applies.

{w:22,39,39}
|Point|Khula|Mubarat|
|Initiative|Wife|Either party|
|Aversion|On the wife's side|Mutual|
|Consideration|Wife gives consideration to the husband|Usually mutual release; no consideration necessary|
|Court|May be decreed by court without the husband's consent (*Khurshid Bibi*)|By agreement only|

**Other provisions:**

- **s. 4 — apostasy:** "The renunciation of Islam by a married Muslim woman or her conversion to a faith other than Islam shall **not by itself operate to dissolve her marriage**", but she may obtain a decree on any s. 2 ground; this does not apply to a woman converted to Islam from another faith who **re-embraces her former faith**.
- **s. 5 — dower:** nothing in the Act affects her right to **dower** on dissolution.

**Procedure.** A suit for dissolution lies to the **Family Court** (Family Courts Act, Schedule, item 1). After the decree, the s. 21 procedure (copy to Chairman; effective after 90 days) applies.

:::eg Choosing the right ground
Sadia's husband, a trader in G-9, married a second wife without the Arbitration Council's permission, has not paid her maintenance for 14 months, and frequently abuses her. She wants a divorce and to keep her dower. Her best grounds are **s. 2(iia)** (second marriage without permission — also making her entire dower immediately payable, MFLO s. 6(5)) and **s. 2(viii)(a)** (cruelty of conduct) and, if she prefers, **s. 2(viii)(f)** (inequitable treatment). Ground (ii) is not yet available (two years). On these grounds she keeps her dower (s. 5). If she sued only for **khula**, the decree would follow on failure of pre-trial reconciliation, but she would have to **restore** the dower received (s. 10(4), Family Courts Act).
:::

# Chapter 6 · Iddat

## 6.1 Meaning

**Iddat** ("counting") is the **period of waiting** which a woman must observe after the dissolution of her marriage, by divorce or death, **before she can lawfully remarry**.

## 6.2 Philosophy — why iddat?

1. **Certainty of paternity** — to ascertain whether she is **pregnant** by the former husband, so that the parentage of a child is not confused (*ikhtilat-un-nasab*).
2. **Reconciliation** — in a **revocable** divorce, the iddat gives the husband time to **revoke** and the couple time to reflect (Quran 65:1: "you know not; it may be that Allah will thereafter bring about some new situation").
3. **Mourning and respect** — in the case of death, a period of mourning (*hidad*) and respect for the marriage tie.
4. **Protection of the woman** — she is entitled to residence and maintenance during the iddat of divorce (Quran 65:6).

## 6.3 Periods of iddat

{w:38,62}
|Situation|Period|
|**Divorce** (talaq, khula, mubarat, judicial dissolution) — woman who menstruates|**Three menstrual courses** (*qur'u*) (Quran 2:228)|
|**Divorce** — woman who does not menstruate (minor, or past menopause)|**Three lunar months** (Quran 65:4)|
|**Death** of the husband|**Four months and ten days** (Quran 2:234)|
|**Pregnant** woman — divorce|Until **delivery** (Quran 65:4)|
|**Pregnant** widow|**Hanafi**: until delivery; **Shia**: the **longer** of delivery and four months and ten days|
|Divorce **before consummation** (and without valid retirement)|**No iddat** (Quran 33:49)|
|Death **before consummation**|Iddat of death **must** be observed|
|**Irregular** marriage consummated|Three courses, whether dissolved by death or divorce|
|**Muta** (Shia)|Two courses (45 days if not menstruating); four months ten days on death|

**Commencement.** Iddat begins from the date of the **divorce or death**, not from the date on which the woman learns of it (Hanafi); if she learns of it only after the period has expired, she need not observe a fresh iddat.

## 6.4 Is iddat compulsory in all separations?

1. **Yes**, after **consummation** (or valid retirement), in every dissolution — talaq, tafweez, khula, mubarat, judicial dissolution — and on **death** (even before consummation).
2. **No**, where the marriage is dissolved **before consummation** (and without valid retirement) otherwise than by death (Quran 33:49).
3. **Void** marriage — no iddat is strictly required, but where it was consummated, prudence and the courts favour observance.

## 6.5 Iddat and the MFLO

Under MFLO s. 7, a talaq becomes effective **90 days** after notice to the Chairman (or on the end of pregnancy if later). In practice, the woman **remarries after the 90 days** and the certificate of effectiveness. Courts have treated the 90-day period as broadly corresponding to the iddat of divorce and as fulfilling its purposes (reconciliation and certainty of pregnancy). The iddat of **death** (four months and ten days) is unaffected by the MFLO.

## 6.6 Legal effects of iddat

1. The woman **cannot remarry** during iddat; a marriage during iddat is **irregular** (Hanafi) and void (Shia).
2. The husband cannot marry her **sister** or another woman within the prohibited conjunction, or a **fifth** wife, until her iddat ends.
3. **Maintenance** — a divorced wife is entitled to **maintenance and residence** during iddat (Hanafi — in both revocable and irrevocable divorce; Shia — only in revocable divorce). A **widow** is **not** entitled to maintenance during iddat (she inherits).
4. In a **revocable** talaq, the husband may resume the marriage during iddat (under the MFLO, during the 90 days).
5. **Mutual inheritance** continues during the iddat of a revocable talaq; a wife divorced in **marz-ul-maut** inherits if the husband dies during her iddat.
6. **Deferred dower** becomes payable on dissolution.

:::recap Chapter 6 in five lines
- Iddat: waiting period after dissolution before remarriage.
- Philosophy: paternity certainty, reconciliation, mourning and respect, protection (Quran 2:228, 2:234, 65:1–6).
- Periods: three courses / three lunar months; death — 4 months 10 days; pregnancy — delivery (Shia widow — longer); none after divorce before consummation (33:49).
- Compulsory after consummation in all dissolutions and on death; MFLO 90 days broadly serves the iddat of divorce.
- Effects: no remarriage; husband's restrictions; maintenance for divorced wife (not widow); revocation; inheritance in revocable talaq.
:::
