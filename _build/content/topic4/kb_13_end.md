# Chapter 14 · The great distinctions of Topic 4

{w:25,37,38}
|Pair|First|Second|
|Indemnity vs guarantee|Two parties; primary liability; save from loss (s. 124)|Three parties; secondary liability on default (s. 126)|
|Specific vs continuing guarantee|Single transaction; ends on payment|Series of transactions (s. 129); revocable for the future (ss. 130–131)|
|Variance (s. 133) vs forbearance (s. 137)|Change in terms without consent — discharges surety|Mere inaction — does not discharge|
|Promise to give time to debtor (s. 135) vs to a third person (s. 136)|Discharges the surety|Does not discharge|
|Bailment vs pledge|Delivery for any purpose; return the goods (s. 148)|Delivery as **security**; power of sale on default (ss. 172, 176)|
|Pledge vs hypothecation|Possession with the creditor|Possession with the debtor; charge only|
|Particular vs general lien|Specific goods improved by labour or skill (s. 170)|Any goods, for the general balance — bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy-brokers (s. 171)|
|Gratuitous vs non-gratuitous bailment|Recall any time with indemnity (s. 159); ends on death (s. 162)|For reward; bailor liable for unknown faults (s. 150)|
|Agent vs servant|Represents the principal in dealings with third persons|Works under control; no general power to contract|
|Sub-agent vs substituted agent|Agent's agent; answerable to the agent (s. 192)|Principal's agent named by the agent (s. 194)|
|Revocable vs irrevocable agency|Revocable before exercise (s. 203)|Coupled with interest (s. 202); partly exercised (s. 204)|
|Disclosed vs undisclosed principal|Third party deals knowingly with an agent|Third party thinks the agent is the principal (ss. 231–232)|
|Sale vs agreement to sell|Property passes now; price suit; buyer's risk|Property passes later / on condition; damages; seller's risk (s. 4)|
|Sale vs hire-purchase|Buyer bound to buy|Hirer has an option; not "agreed to buy" (*Helby v. Matthews*)|
|Condition vs warranty|Essential; rejection + damages (s. 12(2))|Collateral; damages only (s. 12(3))|
|Specific vs unascertained goods|Identified and agreed at contract (s. 2(14))|Generic; no property until ascertained (s. 18)|
|Delivery vs transfer of property|Transfer of **possession** (s. 2(2))|Transfer of **ownership** (ss. 18–26)|
|Actual vs symbolic / constructive delivery|Physical handing over of the goods|Keys or documents of title (symbolic); attornment by the person in possession (constructive — s. 36(3))|
|Lien vs stoppage in transit|Seller keeps possession; no insolvency needed (s. 47)|Seller resumes possession; buyer insolvent; goods in transit (s. 50)|
|Resale with vs without notice|Damages recoverable; buyer gets no profit (s. 54(2))|No damages; buyer gets the profit|
|Voidable title (s. 29) vs void contract|Rogue's buyer in good faith before rescission gets title (*Phillips v. Brooks*)|No title passes (*Cundy v. Lindsay*)|
|Suit for price vs damages for non-acceptance|Property passed or price due on a day certain (s. 55)|Otherwise (s. 56)|

# Chapter 15 · Exam technique for Topic 4

## 15.1 Guarantee problems — the six questions

1. **Is there a guarantee** at all — or an indemnity? (Who is primarily liable? s. 124 vs s. 126.)
2. Is it **valid** — consideration (s. 127), free consent (ss. 142–143), condition precedent (s. 144), form and proof (Art. 17, 79 QSO), capacity (*ultra vires* for companies)?
3. **What kind** — specific or continuing (s. 129)? Was it revoked (ss. 130–131)?
4. **Extent** of liability — co-extensive, limited by the contract (s. 128); immediate (*Bank of Bihar*).
5. Has the surety been **discharged** — ss. 133–139, 141?
6. What are the surety's **rights** — ss. 140, 141, 145–147?

## 15.2 Agency problems — the five questions

1. Was there an **agency** — and how was it created (express, implied, estoppel, necessity, ratification)?
2. Did the agent act **within his authority** — actual, incidental (s. 188), ostensible (s. 237)?
3. **Who is liable** to the third party — the principal (s. 226), the agent personally (s. 230), or either (s. 233)?
4. Did the agent breach a **duty** — directions, skill, accounts, **self-dealing** (s. 215), secret profit (s. 216)?
5. Has the agency **terminated**, and when did termination take effect (ss. 201–210)?

## 15.3 Sale of goods problems — the four questions

1. **What kind of contract and goods** — sale or agreement to sell; specific or unascertained (ss. 4, 18)?
2. **Has property (and risk) passed** — ss. 19–26?
3. **Was a condition or warranty broken** — ss. 12–17, **16A**; has the buyer **accepted** (ss. 13(2), 42)?
4. **Title and third parties** — nemo dat and exceptions (ss. 27–30); unpaid seller's rights (ss. 45–54); remedies (ss. 55–61).

# Chapter 16 · Last-night revision sheet

:::recap The 40 points of Topic 4
1. Indemnity: save from loss caused by the promisor or another (s. 124); narrower than English law (*Adamson v. Jarvis*).
2. Indemnity-holder recovers damages, prudent costs and prudent compromises (s. 125); may sue once liability is absolute (*Gajanan Moreshwar*).
3. Guarantee: perform the promise / discharge the liability of a third person on default (s. 126); oral or written; Art. 17 QSO attestation.
4. Consideration for the principal debtor's benefit suffices (s. 127); past consideration without request — void (illus. (c)).
5. Continuing guarantee (s. 129) — revoked as to the future by notice (s. 130) or death (s. 131).
6. Surety's liability co-extensive (s. 128) and immediate — no need to sue the debtor first (*Bank of Bihar v. Damodar Prasad*).
7. Bank guarantees independent; injunction only for fraud or irretrievable injustice.
8. Discharge by variance (s. 133 — illus. (a)–(e)) and by release of the principal debtor (s. 134).
9. Composition, giving time, or promise not to sue — discharges (s. 135); not with a third person (s. 136); not mere forbearance (s. 137).
10. Release of a co-surety does not discharge others (s. 138); creditor impairing the surety's remedy discharges (s. 139).
11. Loss of securities discharges to their value (s. 141); invalid guarantees — misrepresentation, concealment, co-surety not joining (ss. 142–144).
12. Surety's rights: subrogation (s. 140), securities (s. 141), indemnity (s. 145), contribution (ss. 146–147).
13. Bailment: delivery of goods for a purpose, on a contract to return (s. 148); possession, not ownership; not a bank deposit of money.
14. Bailor's duty to disclose known faults; hirer liable even for unknown faults (s. 150).
15. Bailee's care — ordinary prudence in **all** bailments (s. 151); no liability if taken (s. 152); limiting clauses doubtful (*Lily White*).
16. Unauthorised use — voidable (s. 153) and strict liability (s. 154); mixing (ss. 155–157).
17. Return without demand; risk after default (ss. 160–161; *Shaw v. Symmons*); increase to the bailor (s. 163).
18. Gratuitous loan recallable with indemnity (s. 159); gratuitous bailment ends on death (s. 162).
19. Liens: particular (s. 170 — labour or skill; credit excludes) and general (s. 171 — five classes).
20. Finder: bailee's duties (s. 71); retain for expenses; sue for offered reward (s. 168); sell if perishing or charges reach two-thirds (s. 169).
21. Pledge: bailment as security (s. 172); retain for debt, interest, expenses (s. 173); subsequent advances presumed (s. 174).
22. On default: sue and retain, or sell after reasonable notice; balance / surplus (s. 176; *Lallan Prasad*); redeem before sale (s. 177).
23. Pledge by mercantile agent (s. 178), by voidable-title holder (s. 178A), limited interest (s. 179).
24. Agent: represents another (s. 182); minor agent binds principal (s. 184); no consideration needed (s. 185).
25. Authority express or implied (ss. 186–187 — Quetta shop); incidental (s. 188); emergency (s. 189; *Swaffield*; *Springer*).
26. Ostensible authority (s. 237; *Freeman & Lockyer*); powers of attorney construed strictly.
27. Ratification: existing competent principal (*Kelner v. Baxter*), disclosed principal (*Keighley Maxsted*), full knowledge (s. 198), whole transaction (s. 199), no injury to third parties (s. 200), relates back.
28. Sub-agent (ss. 190–193) vs substituted agent (ss. 194–195).
29. Agent's duties: directions / custom (s. 211), skill (s. 212), accounts (s. 213), communicate (s. 214), pay over (s. 218).
30. **s. 215** — self-dealing without informed consent: repudiate if concealment or disadvantage; **s. 216** — secret profits (*Armstrong v. Jackson*).
31. Agent's rights: retainer, remuneration, lien, indemnity (ss. 217–223); none for criminal acts (s. 224); injury (s. 225).
32. Principal bound (s. 226); excess of authority (ss. 227–228); notice (s. 229); agent's fraud (s. 238).
33. Agent personally liable (s. 230 — foreign principal, undisclosed name, principal not suable); undisclosed principal (ss. 231–232); election (s. 233); pretended agent (s. 235).
34. Termination (s. 201); coupled with interest (s. 202); partly exercised (s. 204); compensation and notice (ss. 205–206); effective on knowledge (s. 208).
35. Sale vs agreement to sell (s. 4); goods include electricity, water, gas (s. 2(7)); perishing (ss. 7–8); price (ss. 9–10).
36. Condition vs warranty (s. 12); waiver / acceptance (s. 13 — Pakistani text); title (s. 14; *Rowland v. Divall*); description (s. 15).
37. Caveat emptor and exceptions: fitness (*Priest v. Last*), merchantable (*Grant*), trade name (*Baldry*), usage, sample (s. 17); **s. 16A** duty to disclose defects; exclusion (s. 62; *Wallis v. Pratt*).
38. Property: unascertained (s. 18), intention (s. 19), rules ss. 20–24, right of disposal (s. 25), risk (s. 26 — *res perit domino*).
39. Nemo dat (s. 27) and exceptions: estoppel, mercantile agent, joint owner, voidable title, seller / buyer in possession (ss. 27–30); finder, pawnee, unpaid seller resale.
40. Delivery (ss. 2(2), 33–40 — actual, symbolic, constructive); acceptance (ss. 41–44); unpaid seller — lien, stoppage, resale (ss. 45–54); suits (ss. 55–61); auctions (s. 64).
