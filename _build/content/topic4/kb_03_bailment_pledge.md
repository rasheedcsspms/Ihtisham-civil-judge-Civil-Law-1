
# Chapter 3 · Bailment (ss. 148 to 171, 180, 181)

## 3.1 Meaning

> "148. A '**bailment**' is the **delivery of goods** by one person to another **for some purpose**, upon a **contract** that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be **returned or otherwise disposed of** according to the directions of the person delivering them. The person delivering the goods is called the '**bailor**'. The person to whom they are delivered is called the '**bailee**'.
> Explanation.— If a person **already in possession** of the goods of another contracts to hold them as a bailee, he thereby becomes the bailee, and the owner becomes the bailor, of such goods although they may not have been delivered by way of bailment."

Everyday examples: giving a car for service, clothes to a dry-cleaner, a watch for repair, luggage to a cloak-room, goods to a carrier or warehouse, a book lent to a friend, jewellery pledged with a bank.

### Essentials

1. **Delivery of goods** — only **movable** property (goods), not land or money as currency.
2. **Delivery of possession** — not ownership. "The delivery to the bailee may be made by doing anything which has the effect of putting the goods in the possession of the intended bailee or of any person authorized to hold them on his behalf" (s. 149). Delivery may be **actual** (handing over) or **constructive** (e.g., handing over the keys of a godown; a person already in possession agreeing to hold as bailee — Explanation).
3. **For some purpose** — repair, safe custody, carriage, use, security.
4. **Upon a contract** — express or implied (the finder of goods is in a similar position by law — s. 71).
5. **Return** of the **same** goods (in their original or altered form) or their **disposal** according to the bailor's directions.

:::trap When it is not bailment
- **Deposit of money in a bank** — not bailment, because the bank need not return the **same** notes; it is a debtor–creditor relationship.
- **Sale** — ownership passes; no return of the same goods.
- **Servant** holding his master's goods — he has **custody**, not possession; no bailment.
- **Parking a car** in an open lot where the owner keeps the keys and no control passes to the lot operator — usually a **licence**, not bailment (it depends on whether possession and control were handed over).
- **Bank locker** — often treated as a **lease or licence** of space, since the bank has no possession of the contents; but where the bank controls access, courts have imposed a **duty of care**.
:::

:::case State of Gujarat v. Memon Mahomed Haji Hasam (AIR 1967 SC 1885)
__Held.__ When the State **seizes** goods under a statute (e.g., vehicles suspected of smuggling), it holds them as a **bailee** — the relationship arises by **operation of law**, without any contract — and is bound to take reasonable care and to **return** them if the seizure is set aside, or pay their value.
:::

## 3.2 Kinds of bailment

{w:30,70}
|Basis|Kinds|
|**Reward**|**Gratuitous** (no charge — lending a book free) and **non-gratuitous** / for reward (dry-cleaning, warehousing, hire of a car).|
|**Benefit**|For the **exclusive benefit of the bailor** (safe custody free of charge); of the **bailee** (free loan of a car to a friend); of **both** (hire, repair for a fee, pledge).|
|**Purpose**|Deposit for **safe custody**; for **work** (repair); for **carriage**; for **use**; as **security** (pledge).|

## 3.3 Duties of the bailor

1. **Disclose known faults — s. 150.** "The bailor is bound to **disclose** to the bailee faults in the goods bailed, of which the bailor is **aware**, and which **materially interfere with the use** of them, or **expose the bailee to extraordinary risks**; and if he does not make such disclosure, he is responsible for damage arising to the bailee directly from such faults. If the goods are **bailed for hire**, the bailor is responsible for such damage, **whether he was or was not aware** of the existence of such faults." *Illus. (a):* A lends a horse he knows to be **vicious**, without telling B; B is thrown — A is liable. *Illus. (b):* A **hires** a carriage from B; it is unsafe, though B does not know — B is liable.
2. **Repay necessary expenses in gratuitous bailment — s. 158.** Where the bailee is to receive **no remuneration**, "the bailor shall repay to the bailee the **necessary expenses** incurred by him for the purpose of the bailment".
3. **Extraordinary expenses** — in a bailment for reward, ordinary expenses are the bailee's, but extraordinary expenses (e.g., a vet's bill for a hired horse suddenly taken ill) may be the bailor's.
4. **Indemnify for defective title — s. 164.** "The bailor is responsible to the bailee for any loss which the bailee may sustain by reason that the bailor was **not entitled to make the bailment**, or to receive back the goods or to give directions, respecting them."
5. **Indemnify for early return of a gratuitous loan — s. 159** (below).
6. **Receive back the goods** when the purpose is accomplished.

## 3.4 Duties of the bailee

### (1) Duty of reasonable care — ss. 151, 152

> "151. In all cases of bailment the bailee is bound to take as much care of the goods bailed to him as **a man of ordinary prudence** would, under similar circumstances, take of **his own goods** of the same bulk, quality and value as the goods bailed."

> "152. The bailee, in the absence of any **special contract**, is **not responsible** for the loss, destruction or deterioration of the thing bailed, if he has taken the amount of care of it described in section 151."

**Points:**

1. **One standard for all bailments.** English law graded the duty (slight care for a gratuitous bailee; great care for a borrower — *Coggs v. Bernard* (1703) 2 Ld Raym 909). Section 151 applies **one** objective standard — ordinary prudence — "in **all** cases of bailment", gratuitous or for reward.
2. **Objective test** — the care a **prudent** man would take of his **own** goods of the same kind. A careless person who neglects his own goods is not excused because he treated the bailed goods the same way.
3. **Burden of proof** — once the bailor proves the bailment and the bailee's failure to return, the burden shifts to the **bailee** to show that he took reasonable care (the facts are within his knowledge).
4. **Special contract** (s. 152) — may **increase** the bailee's liability (e.g., to make him an insurer). Whether it may **reduce** liability below the s. 151 standard is doubtful; Indian courts have held that the bailee cannot contract out of the minimum duty of care.

:::case Lily White v. R. Munuswami (AIR 1966 Mad 13)
__Facts.__ A laundry receipt said that, for lost garments, the customer would get only **50 % of the market price**. A new saree was lost.

__Held.__ The clause was **invalid**. Under ss. 151–152, the bailee must take reasonable care; a clause limiting his liability in a way that encourages negligence is **opposed to public policy**. The customer recovered the full value.
:::

:::eg Applying ss. 151–152
- **Theft from a godown** — a warehouseman who keeps a watchman, locks and normal precautions is not liable for a burglary that happens despite those precautions (s. 152); one who leaves the godown unguarded is.
- **Riots and natural disasters** — a bailee is not liable if loss occurs despite reasonable care; but if he was negligent (e.g., left goods outside in the rain, or did not move them when danger was apparent), he is liable.
- **Common carriers** are governed by the **Carriers Act 1865**, and railways by the **Railways Act 1890**: their liability is stricter (a common carrier is in principle an insurer, subject to the statutory exceptions).
:::

### (2) Not to make unauthorised use — ss. 153, 154

- **s. 153** — "A contract of bailment is **voidable at the option of the bailor**, if the bailee does any act with regard to the goods bailed, **inconsistent with the conditions** of the bailment." *Illus.:* A lets a horse for B's **own riding**; B **drives it in his carriage** — A may terminate.
- **s. 154** — "If the bailee makes any use of the goods bailed, which is **not according to the conditions** of the bailment, he is liable to make compensation to the bailor for **any damage** arising to the goods **from or during** such use of them." *Illus. (a):* a horse lent for B's own riding is ridden by C, a family member, carefully; it falls accidentally — B is liable. *Illus. (b):* a horse hired in **Karachi** to go to **Hyderabad** is ridden to **Khairpur**; it falls accidentally — A is liable.

**Point.** Under s. 154 the bailee is liable **even without negligence** — the unauthorised use makes him an insurer during that use.

### (3) Not to mix the goods — ss. 155 to 157

{w:30,70}
|Situation|Result|
|Mixed **with consent** (s. 155)|Bailor and bailee have an interest **in proportion** to their shares in the mixture.|
|Mixed **without consent**, **separable** (s. 156)|Property remains with each; the bailee bears the **cost of separation** and any damage. *Illus.:* 100 marked cotton bales mixed with B's own bales — B must separate at his cost.|
|Mixed **without consent**, **not separable** (s. 157)|The bailee must **compensate** the bailor for the loss of the goods. *Illus.:* a barrel of Cape flour worth Rs. 45 mixed with country flour worth Rs. 25 — B must compensate A.|

### (4) To return the goods — ss. 160, 161

- **s. 160** — "It is the duty of the bailee to **return**, or deliver according to the bailor's directions, the goods bailed, **without demand**, as soon as the **time** for which they were bailed has expired, or the **purpose** … has been accomplished."
- **s. 161** — "If, by the **default of the bailee**, the goods are not returned, delivered or tendered at the proper time, he is responsible to the bailor for **any loss, destruction or deterioration** of the goods **from that time**" — even without negligence.

:::case Shaw & Co. v. Symmons & Sons ([1917] 1 KB 799)
__Held.__ A bookbinder who kept books beyond the agreed time for return, after which they were destroyed by an accidental fire, was **liable** for their loss: once the bailee is in default in returning the goods, he bears the risk of their loss.
:::

### (5) To return the increase or profit — s. 163

"In the absence of any contract to the contrary, the bailee is bound to deliver to the bailor … any **increase or profit** which may have accrued from the goods bailed." *Illus.:* A leaves a **cow** with B; the cow has a **calf** — B must deliver both. Dividends on shares pledged belong to the pawnor.

### (6) Not to set up an adverse title (estoppel)

A bailee cannot deny the bailor's title to the goods (Art. 115, Qanun-e-Shahadat — estoppel of tenant and licensee — applied by analogy). But where a **third person** claims the goods, he may apply to the court to stop delivery to the bailor and decide the title (s. 167). If the bailor has no title and the bailee, **in good faith**, returns the goods to him, the bailee is **not liable** to the true owner (s. 166). If several **joint owners** bail goods, the bailee may return them to **one** of them (s. 165).

## 3.5 Rights of the bailee

1. **Compensation** for undisclosed faults (s. 150) and for the bailor's defective title (s. 164).
2. **Reimbursement** of necessary expenses in gratuitous bailment (s. 158).
3. **Indemnity** on early recall of a gratuitous loan (s. 159).
4. **Lien** — particular (s. 170) or general (s. 171).
5. **Suit against wrongdoers** — "If a third person wrongfully deprives the bailee of the use or possession of the goods bailed, or does them any injury, the bailee is entitled to use such remedies as the owner might have used …; and **either the bailor or the bailee** may bring a suit against a third person" (s. 180). The compensation recovered is divided **according to their respective interests** (s. 181).
6. **Deliver to one joint owner** (s. 165) and **protection on good-faith return** to a bailor without title (s. 166).
7. **Interpleader / court determination** when a third person claims (s. 167; s. 88 and O. XXXV CPC — Topic 2).

## 3.6 Termination of bailment

1. **Expiry of the time** or **accomplishment of the purpose** (s. 160).
2. **Bailee's inconsistent act** — voidable at the bailor's option (s. 153).
3. **Gratuitous loan recalled** — "The lender of a thing for use may **at any time require its return**, if the loan was gratuitous, even though he lent it for a specified time or purpose. But, if, on the faith of such loan made for a specified time or purpose, the borrower has acted in such a manner that the return of the thing lent before the time agreed upon would cause him **loss exceeding the benefit** actually derived by him from the loan, the lender must, if he compels the return, **indemnify** the borrower for the amount in which the loss so occasioned exceeds the benefit so derived" (s. 159).
4. **Death** — "A **gratuitous** bailment is terminated by the death either of the bailor or of the bailee" (s. 162). A bailment for reward continues.
5. **Destruction** of the subject matter, or the bailment becoming unlawful (s. 56).

## 3.7 Bailee's lien — ss. 170, 171

A **lien** is a right to **retain possession** of goods until a claim is satisfied. It is a passive right — to keep, not (in general) to sell.

> "170. Where the bailee has, in accordance with the purpose of the bailment, **rendered any service involving the exercise of labour or skill** in respect of the goods bailed, he has, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, a right to **retain such goods** until he receives due remuneration for the services he has rendered in respect of them."

*Illus. (a):* a jeweller who cuts and polishes a rough diamond may retain it until paid. *Illus. (b):* a tailor who has agreed to deliver the coat on completion and give **three months' credit** has **no lien** (a lien is excluded by the credit agreement).

> "171. **Bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court and policy-brokers** may, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, retain, as a security for a **general balance of account**, any goods bailed to them; but no other persons have a right to retain, as a security for such balance, goods bailed to them, unless there is an **express contract** to that effect."

{w:22,39,39}
|Point|Particular lien (s. 170)|General lien (s. 171)|
|Covers|Only the **specific goods** on which labour or skill was spent|**Any goods** bailed, for the **general balance** of account|
|Who has it|Any bailee who improves the goods by labour or skill|Only bankers, factors, wharfingers, High Court attorneys and policy-brokers (or by express contract)|
|Basis|Service on the goods|Course of business / general account|
|Example|Tailor, repairer, jeweller|A bank retaining securities for an overdraft|

**Lien is lost** by **parting with possession** voluntarily, by **waiver**, by a contract inconsistent with it (credit), or by **tender** of the amount due. A lien is a **right of retention**, not of sale — except where statute allows (e.g., ss. 169, 176; the unpaid seller under SGA s. 54).

## 3.8 Finder of goods (ss. 71, 168, 169)

The finder of lost goods who takes them into his custody "is subject to the same responsibility as a bailee" (s. 71).

**Duties:** reasonable care (s. 151); no unauthorised use; reasonable efforts to find the **owner**; return the goods to him.

**Rights:**

1. **No suit for compensation** — "The finder of goods has **no right to sue** the owner for compensation for trouble and expense voluntarily incurred by him to preserve the goods and to find out the owner;"
2. **but he may retain** the goods "against the owner until he receives such compensation;" and
3. where the owner has offered a **specific reward**, he **may sue** for it and retain the goods until he receives it (s. 168).
4. **Right to sell — s. 169.** When a thing **commonly the subject of sale** is lost, and the owner cannot with reasonable diligence be found, or **refuses to pay the lawful charges**, the finder may sell it — (1) when the thing is in danger of **perishing** or of losing the greater part of its value; or (2) when the finder's **lawful charges amount to two-thirds of its value**.

:::case Hannah v. Peel ([1945] KB 509)
__Held.__ A soldier who found a brooch in a house requisitioned by the army, which the owner had never occupied, was entitled to it as against the owner of the house (who had no knowledge of it); but the finder's title is good against everyone **except the true owner** (*Armory v. Delamirie* (1722) 1 Str 505 — a chimney-sweep's boy who found a jewel could recover its value from a goldsmith who refused to return it).
:::

:::recap Chapter 3 in eight lines
- Bailment: delivery of goods for a purpose, on a contract to return or dispose of them (s. 148); possession not ownership; delivery actual or constructive (s. 149).
- Not bailment: bank deposit of money, sale, servant's custody; State seizure creates bailment (*Memon Mahomed*).
- Bailor: disclose known faults — hirer liable even if unaware (s. 150); expenses in gratuitous bailment (s. 158); defective title (s. 164); s. 159 indemnity on early recall.
- Bailee: ordinary prudence in all bailments (s. 151; no liability if that care taken — s. 152; *Lily White*); unauthorised use — voidable, strict liability (ss. 153–154); mixing (ss. 155–157); return without demand, risk after default (ss. 160–161; *Shaw v. Symmons*); increase (s. 163).
- Bailee's protections: s. 165 joint owners; s. 166 good-faith return; s. 167 third-party claim; s. 180 suit against wrongdoers, s. 181 apportionment.
- Termination: time / purpose; s. 153; gratuitous recall (s. 159); death in gratuitous bailment (s. 162).
- Lien: particular (s. 170 — labour or skill; credit excludes) vs general (s. 171 — bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, policy-brokers).
- Finder: bailee's duties (s. 71); retain for expenses, sue for offered reward (s. 168); sell if perishing or charges reach two-thirds (s. 169).
:::

# Chapter 4 · Pledge (ss. 172 to 179)

## 4.1 Meaning

> "172. The **bailment of goods as security** for payment of a debt or performance of a promise is called '**pledge**'. The bailor is in this case called the '**pawnor**'. The bailee is called the '**pawnee**'."

A pledge is a **special kind of bailment** — for **security**. Common examples: gold ornaments pledged with a bank for a gold loan; goods in a godown pledged to a bank, with the godown keys handed over; shares pledged to a broker.

### Essentials

1. **Delivery of possession** of goods (actual or constructive — e.g., delivery of documents of title, or the keys of the godown) to the pawnee.
2. **As security** for a debt or the performance of a promise.
3. The pawnor need not be the **debtor** (a third person may pledge his goods for another's debt).
4. **Ownership remains** with the pawnor; the pawnee gets a **special property** — the right to retain and, on default, to sell.

## 4.2 Pledge, hypothecation, mortgage and lien

{w:18,27,27,28}
|Point|Pledge|Hypothecation|Mortgage (of movables)|
|Possession|With the **creditor** (pawnee)|With the **debtor**; creditor has a charge|Ownership interest transferred to the creditor, possession may remain with the debtor|
|Law|Contract Act ss. 172–179|Contract / banking practice; recovery laws (e.g., Financial Institutions (Recovery of Finances) Ordinance 2001)|Transfer of Property Act (immovables); general law|
|Right on default|Sue and retain, or **sell on reasonable notice** (s. 176)|Take possession and sell under the agreement / through court|Foreclosure / sale|
|Example|Gold loan|Car finance; stock hypothecated to a bank|Chattel mortgage|

**Pledge vs lien:** a lien is only a right to **retain**; a pledge carries a **power of sale** on default (s. 176). A lien arises by **law** (ss. 170–171); a pledge by **contract**.

## 4.3 Rights of the pawnee

1. **Retain** the goods — "not only for payment of the debt or the performance of the promise, but for the **interest** of the debt, and all **necessary expenses** incurred by him in respect of the possession or for the preservation of the goods" (s. 173).
2. **Only for that debt** — "The pawnee shall not, in the absence of a contract to that effect retain the goods pledged for **any debt or promise other than** the debt or promise for which they are pledged; but such contract, in the absence of anything to the contrary, shall be **presumed in regard to subsequent advances** made by the pawnee" (s. 174).
3. **Extraordinary expenses** incurred for preservation — recoverable from the pawnor (s. 175) (but the pawnee has no lien for them; he must sue).
4. **On default — s. 176:**
   - **sue** the pawnor on the debt and **retain** the goods as collateral security; **or**
   - **sell** the goods on giving the pawnor **reasonable notice** of the sale;
   - if the proceeds are **less** than the amount due, the pawnor is liable for the **balance**; if **more**, the pawnee must pay over the **surplus**.

:::case Lallan Prasad v. Rahmat Ali (AIR 1967 SC 1322)
__Facts.__ The pawnee of aluminium sheets sued the pawnor for the debt, but could not return the goods, which he had already disposed of without notice.

__Held.__ A pawnee who sues for the debt must be **ready to return** the pledged goods on payment. If he has disposed of them **without** giving reasonable notice under s. 176, he cannot recover the debt without accounting for the goods; the pawnor may resist the suit or claim their value.
:::

**Reasonable notice** is mandatory. A sale without notice is a breach: the pawnee is liable for the difference between the market value and the debt.

## 4.4 Rights of the pawnor

1. **Redeem — s. 177.** "If a time is stipulated for the payment of the debt …, and the pawnor makes **default** …, he **may redeem** the goods pledged at any subsequent time **before the actual sale** of them; but he must, in that case, pay, in addition, any **expenses** which have arisen from his default."
2. **Notice before sale** (s. 176), and the **surplus** of sale proceeds.
3. **Reasonable care** of the goods by the pawnee (s. 151) and return of **increase** (s. 163 — e.g., bonus shares).
4. **Suit against wrongdoers** (s. 180).

## 4.5 Pledge by persons who are not owners

The general rule is that only the owner, or a person authorised by him, can pledge. The Act and the SGA make exceptions to protect a **pawnee in good faith**:

{w:24,76}
|Pawnor|Rule|
|**Mercantile agent — s. 178**|"Where a **mercantile agent** is, **with the consent of the owner**, in possession of goods or the documents of title to goods, any pledge made by him, when acting in the **ordinary course of business** of a mercantile agent, shall be as valid as if he were expressly authorized by the owner …; provided that the pawnee acts in **good faith** and has not at the time of the pledge **notice** that the pawnor has not authority to pledge."|
|**Person in possession under a voidable contract — s. 178A**|"When the pawnor has obtained possession of the goods pledged by him under a contract **voidable under section 19 or section 19A**, but the contract has **not been rescinded** at the time of the pledge, the pawnee acquires a **good title** to the goods, provided he acts in good faith and without notice of the pawnor's defect of title."|
|**Person with a limited interest — s. 179**|"Where a person pledges goods in which he has only a **limited interest**, the pledge is valid **to the extent of that interest**" (e.g., a pawnee who re-pledges, or a hirer).|
|**Seller or buyer in possession — SGA s. 30**|A seller who remains in possession, or a buyer who obtains possession with the seller's consent, can make a valid **pledge** to a person in good faith without notice (Chapter 9).|
|**Co-owner in sole possession — SGA s. 28** (by analogy)|For sales; pledges follow the same logic where the co-owner has sole possession with consent.|

:::recap Chapter 4 in six lines
- Pledge: bailment of goods as security (s. 172); pawnor and pawnee; possession passes, ownership stays.
- Distinguish hypothecation (possession with debtor), mortgage, and lien (no power of sale).
- Pawnee: retain for debt, interest, necessary expenses (s. 173); only that debt, but subsequent advances presumed (s. 174); extraordinary expenses (s. 175).
- On default: sue and retain, or sell on reasonable notice; balance / surplus (s. 176; *Lallan Prasad*).
- Pawnor: redeem before actual sale with expenses (s. 177).
- Non-owners: mercantile agent (s. 178), voidable-contract possessor (s. 178A), limited interest (s. 179), seller / buyer in possession (SGA s. 30).
:::
