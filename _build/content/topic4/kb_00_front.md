# How to use this knowledge base

## What Topic 4 covers

Topic 3 dealt with the **general principles** that govern every contract. Topic 4 deals with the **special contracts** that the Contract Act regulates in detail, and with the **Sale of Goods Act 1930**, which took the law of sale out of the Contract Act. This volume covers:

1. **indemnity** (ss. 124–125);
2. **guarantee** — kinds, the surety's liability, discharge of the surety, and the surety's rights (ss. 126–147);
3. **bailment** — duties of bailor and bailee, finders, and liens (ss. 148–171);
4. **pledge** — rights of pawnor and pawnee, and pledges by non-owners (ss. 172–181);
5. **agency** — creation, authority, ratification, sub-agents, duties and rights, liability to third parties, and termination (ss. 182–238);
6. the **contract of sale** — sale and agreement to sell, goods, price (SGA ss. 1–10);
7. **conditions and warranties** and **caveat emptor** (SGA ss. 11–17, including the Pakistani **s. 16A**);
8. **transfer of property** and **risk** (SGA ss. 18–26);
9. **transfer of title** — *nemo dat quod non habet* and its exceptions (SGA ss. 27–30);
10. **performance** — delivery, acceptance and rejection (SGA ss. 31–44);
11. the **unpaid seller** — lien, stoppage in transit and resale (SGA ss. 45–54); and
12. **suits for breach**, auctions and miscellaneous rules (SGA ss. 55–66).

Each chapter follows the same pattern as Topics 1 to 3: the idea in plain words, the law (quoted), its ingredients, the **rationale**, **case studies** with ratio decidendi, **maxims**, the statutory **illustrations**, exam tips and common mistakes, and a quick recap.

:::ict Pakistani features of Topic 4
The Pakistani texts differ in places from the Indian Acts on which many textbooks are based:
1. **SGA s. 16A** (inserted by Act XVIII of 1994): "the seller shall be under an **obligation to inform the buyer of any defect** in the goods sold at the time of the contract, except in a case where the defect is obviously known to the buyer" — unless the parties agree otherwise. This cuts deeply into **caveat emptor**.
2. **SGA s. 2(7)**: "goods" includes **electricity, water and gas** (Ordinance XLVII of 1962).
3. **SGA s. 13(2)**: the words treating acceptance of **specific goods whose property has passed** as a bar to rejection were **omitted** in 1962; in Pakistan only **acceptance** of goods in a non-severable contract converts a condition into a warranty.
4. **SGA s. 37(2)** (1994): where excess goods are delivered and separating the contract quantity is **difficult or time-consuming**, the buyer may reject the whole.
5. **SGA s. 64A**: increases or decreases of customs, excise or **sales tax** after the contract are added to or deducted from the price.
6. **Contract Act ss. 178, 178A**: pledges by a **mercantile agent** and by a person in possession under a **voidable contract** (s. 19 or **s. 19A**) are valid in favour of a pawnee in good faith.
7. The statutory illustrations are localised (Karachi, Quetta, Lahore, Rawalpindi, Islamabad) — quote them as they stand in the Pakistani text.
8. A guarantee "may be either oral or written" (s. 126), but **Art. 17 of the Qanun-e-Shahadat 1984** requires instruments of **financial obligations** to be attested by two men, or one man and two women, and Art. 79 governs their proof.
:::

:::tip Quote the illustrations
The sections on guarantee, bailment and agency carry dozens of **statutory illustrations** — many of them almost exact past-paper problems (e.g., s. 133 illustrations on variance; s. 139 on impairing the surety's remedy; s. 215 on the agent who buys the estate himself). In the exam, with the bare Act in hand, quote the illustration that fits: it is authority the examiner cannot argue with.
:::
