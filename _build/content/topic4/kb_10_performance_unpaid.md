
# Chapter 10 · Performance of the contract of sale — delivery and acceptance (SGA ss. 31 to 44)

## 10.1 Duties of seller and buyer — ss. 31, 32

- **s. 31** — "It is the duty of the **seller to deliver** the goods and of the **buyer to accept and pay** for them, in accordance with the terms of the contract of sale."
- **s. 32** — "Unless otherwise agreed, **delivery** of the goods and **payment** of the price are **concurrent conditions**, that is to say, the seller shall be ready and willing to give possession of the goods to the buyer in exchange for the price, and the buyer shall be ready and willing to pay the price in exchange for possession of the goods." (Compare Contract Act s. 51.)

## 10.2 Delivery — meaning and kinds

"**Delivery**" means "**voluntary transfer of possession** from one person to another" (s. 2(2)). "Delivery of goods sold may be made by doing **anything which the parties agree** shall be treated as delivery or which has the effect of **putting the goods in the possession** of the buyer or of any person authorised to hold them on his behalf" (s. 33).

Delivery transfers **possession**; transfer of **property** (ownership) is governed separately by ss. 18–26. The two may happen at different times.

![The three kinds of delivery](delivery_kinds.png)

{w:24,76}
|Kind|Meaning and examples|
|**Actual (physical)**|The goods are physically handed over to the buyer or his agent — handing a television to the buyer at the counter.|
|**Symbolic**|Something is handed over that gives **control** of the goods — the **keys** of a godown containing the goods; a **bill of lading**, railway receipt or other **document of title** (s. 2(4)); the registration book and keys of a car.|
|**Constructive (attornment)**|There is no physical change of hands, but the person in possession **acknowledges** that he now holds the goods **for another**: (i) the **seller** agrees to hold the goods as the **buyer's bailee**; (ii) the **buyer**, already in possession as bailee, begins to hold as owner; (iii) a **third person** (warehouseman) acknowledges to the buyer that he holds for him — "there is no delivery by seller to buyer **unless and until such third person acknowledges** to the buyer that he holds the goods on his behalf" (s. 36(3)).|

## 10.3 Rules as to delivery — ss. 33 to 40

|s.|Rule|
|34|**Part delivery** in progress of delivery of the whole has the same effect, for passing property, as delivery of the whole; but part delivery with an intention of **severing** it from the whole does not operate as delivery of the rest.|
|35|**Buyer to apply** — apart from express contract, the seller is not bound to deliver until the buyer applies for delivery.|
|36(1)|**Place** — as agreed; otherwise goods **sold** are delivered at the place where they are at the time of sale; goods **agreed to be sold** at the place where they are at the time of the agreement, or, if not then in existence, where they are manufactured or produced. Whether the buyer collects or the seller sends depends on the contract.|
|36(2)|**Time** — if the seller must send the goods and no time is fixed, within a **reasonable time**.|

|36(4)|**Reasonable hour** — demand or tender of delivery may be treated as ineffectual unless made at a reasonable hour.|
|36(5)|**Expenses** of putting the goods into a deliverable state — borne by the **seller**, unless otherwise agreed.|
|37(1)|**Short delivery** — the buyer may **reject**; if he accepts, he pays at the contract rate.|
|37(2)|**Excess delivery** — the buyer may accept the contract quantity and reject the rest, or — **if the goods delivered are such that it is difficult or time consuming to separate the quantity contracted for** (Pakistani addition, 1994) — reject the whole; if he accepts the whole, he pays at the contract rate.|
|37(3)|**Mixed goods** of a different description — accept those that conform and reject the rest, or reject the whole.|

## 10.4 Examination, acceptance and rejection — ss. 41 to 44

1. **Right to examine — s. 41.** A buyer to whom goods are delivered which he has **not previously examined** is **not deemed to have accepted** them until he has had a **reasonable opportunity of examining** them to see whether they conform to the contract; and the seller, on tendering delivery, must on request afford that opportunity.
2. **Acceptance — s. 42.** The buyer is deemed to have accepted the goods (i) when he **intimates** acceptance to the seller; (ii) when, after delivery, he does any **act inconsistent with the seller's ownership** (e.g., resells or uses them as his own); or (iii) when, after a **reasonable time**, he **retains** them without intimating rejection.
3. **No duty to return rejected goods — s. 43.** Unless otherwise agreed, a buyer who rightly refuses to accept need **not return** the goods; it is enough to **intimate** refusal to the seller (he holds them as a bailee meanwhile).
4. **Buyer's refusal or neglect to take delivery — s. 44.** Where the seller is ready and willing and requests delivery, and the buyer does not take it within a reasonable time, the buyer is liable for **any loss** caused and for a **reasonable charge for care and custody**; if his refusal amounts to **repudiation**, the seller has his further remedies.

**Effect of acceptance:** after acceptance in a non-severable contract, a breach of condition can only be treated as a breach of **warranty** (s. 13(2)) — damages, not rejection.

- Rules: part delivery (s. 34); buyer to apply (s. 35); place, time, reasonable hour, expenses (s. 36); wrong quantity (s. 37 — Pakistani 1994 addition); instalments (s. 38); carrier and sea transit (s. 39); distant place (s. 40).

# Chapter 11 · Rights of the unpaid seller against the goods (SGA ss. 45 to 54)

## 11.1 Who is an unpaid seller — s. 45

> "45. (1) The seller of goods is deemed to be an '**unpaid seller**' within the meaning of this Act— (a) when the **whole of the price has not been paid or tendered**; (b) when a **bill of exchange or other negotiable instrument** has been received as **conditional payment**, and the condition on which it was received has not been fulfilled by reason of the **dishonour** of the instrument or otherwise.
> (2) In this Chapter, the term 'seller' includes any person who is **in the position of a seller**, as, for instance, an agent of the seller to whom the bill of lading has been indorsed, or a consignor or agent who has himself paid, or is directly responsible for, the price."

A seller who has received **part** of the price is still an unpaid seller. A seller who has been **tendered** the full price, or who has accepted a negotiable instrument as **absolute** payment, is not.

## 11.2 The rights — s. 46

> "46. (1) Subject to the provisions of this Act and of any law for the time being in force, **notwithstanding that the property in the goods may have passed** to the buyer, the unpaid seller of goods, as such, has by implication of law— (a) a **lien** on the goods for the price while he is in possession of them; (b) in case of the **insolvency** of the buyer a right of **stopping the goods in transit** after he has parted with the possession of them; (c) a right of **re-sale** as limited by this Act.
> (2) Where the property in goods has **not passed** to the buyer, the unpaid seller has, in addition to his other remedies, a right of **withholding delivery** similar to and co-extensive with his rights of lien and stoppage in transit where the property has passed to the buyer."

![Rights of the unpaid seller](unpaid_seller.png)

Besides these rights **against the goods**, he has rights **against the buyer personally**: a suit for the **price** (s. 55), for **damages** for non-acceptance (s. 56), for **interest** (s. 61), and for damages for anticipatory repudiation (s. 60).

## 11.3 The lien — ss. 47 to 49

**When available — s. 47(1).** The unpaid seller **in possession** may retain them until payment or tender of the price —

1. where the goods have been sold **without any stipulation as to credit**;
2. where they have been sold **on credit**, but the **term of credit has expired**; or
3. where the **buyer becomes insolvent** (even during the credit period).

The seller may exercise his lien even though he is in possession **as agent or bailee for the buyer** (s. 47(2)) — e.g., where property has passed and he holds the goods for the buyer.

**Part delivery — s. 48.** After part delivery, the seller may exercise his lien on the **remainder**, unless the part delivery shows an agreement to waive the lien.

**Loss of lien — s. 49.** The unpaid seller loses his lien —

1. when he **delivers** the goods to a **carrier** or other bailee for transmission to the buyer **without reserving the right of disposal**;
2. when the **buyer or his agent lawfully obtains possession**; or
3. by **waiver**.

He does **not** lose his lien merely because he has obtained a **decree for the price** (s. 49(2)).

**Nature of the lien.** It is a **possessory** lien for the **price only** (not for storage charges or other debts); it does not by itself rescind the contract (s. 54(1)).

## 11.4 Stoppage in transit — ss. 50 to 52

> "50. Subject to the provisions of this Act, when the **buyer of goods becomes insolvent**, the unpaid seller who has **parted with the possession** of the goods has the right of **stopping them in transit**, that is to say, he may **resume possession** of the goods as long as they are **in the course of transit**, and may retain them until payment or tender of the price."

**Conditions:** (i) the seller is **unpaid**; (ii) he has **parted with possession**; (iii) the goods are **in transit**; (iv) the buyer is **insolvent** (s. 2(8) — ceased to pay debts in the ordinary course, or cannot pay as they fall due).

**Duration of transit — s. 51.**

1. Goods are in transit from delivery to a **carrier** or other bailee for transmission to the buyer **until the buyer or his agent takes delivery** from the carrier (s. 51(1)).
2. If the buyer obtains delivery **before arrival** at the destination, transit **ends** (s. 51(2)).
3. If, after arrival, the carrier **acknowledges** to the buyer that he holds the goods **on his behalf**, transit **ends** (s. 51(3)).
4. If the goods are **rejected** by the buyer and the carrier keeps them, transit **continues** (s. 51(4)).
5. Goods on a **ship chartered by the buyer** — whether the master holds as carrier or as the buyer's agent depends on the circumstances (s. 51(5)).
6. If the carrier **wrongfully refuses** to deliver to the buyer, transit **ends** (s. 51(6)).
7. After **part delivery**, the remainder may be stopped unless the part delivery shows an agreement to give up possession of the whole (s. 51(7)).

**How effected — s. 52.** By taking **actual possession**, or by **notice** to the carrier or bailee (or to his principal, in time to prevent delivery). The carrier must then **re-deliver** to the seller, at the seller's expense.

|Point|Lien|Stoppage in transit|

|Buyer's insolvency|Not necessary (credit expired or no credit is enough)|**Essential**|

## 11.5 Effect of sub-sale or pledge by the buyer — s. 53

The seller's lien or right of stoppage is **not affected** by any sale or other disposition by the buyer, **unless the seller has assented** (s. 53(1)). **But** where a **document of title** has been issued or lawfully transferred to the buyer, and he transfers it to a person taking **in good faith and for consideration**: if by way of **sale**, the seller's lien or stoppage is **defeated**; if by way of **pledge**, the seller's rights can be exercised only **subject to the pledgee's rights** (s. 53(1) proviso). In the case of a pledge, the seller may require the pledgee to satisfy his claim first, as far as possible, out of other goods or securities of the buyer in his hands (s. 53(2)) — the principle of **marshalling**.

## 11.6 Right of resale — s. 54

1. **No rescission by lien or stoppage alone** — "a contract of sale is not rescinded by the mere exercise by an unpaid seller of his right of lien or stoppage in transit" (s. 54(1)).
2. **Resale with notice** — where the goods are **perishable**, or the unpaid seller **gives notice** to the buyer of his intention to resell, and the buyer does not pay or tender the price within a **reasonable time**, the seller may **resell** within a reasonable time and recover **damages** for any loss from the original buyer; the buyer is **not** entitled to any **profit** on the resale (s. 54(2)).
3. **Resale without notice** — the seller **cannot** recover damages, and the buyer is **entitled to any profit** on the resale (s. 54(2)).
4. **Title of the second buyer** — "the buyer acquires a **good title** thereto as against the original buyer, notwithstanding that no notice of the re-sale has been given to the original buyer" (s. 54(3)).
5. **Express reservation** — where the seller **expressly reserves** a right of resale on the buyer's default and resells, the original contract is **rescinded**, without prejudice to his claim for damages (s. 54(4)).

:::eg The unpaid seller in practice
Sadiq Textiles, Faisalabad, sells 500 bales of cloth to Rehman Traders, Karachi, on 30 days' credit, and sends them by goods train, the railway receipt made out in Rehman's name. While the goods are on their way, Rehman stops paying its debts. Sadiq is an **unpaid seller** (s. 45); it has **parted with possession** and so has no lien (s. 49(1)(a)); but the buyer is **insolvent** and the goods are **in transit** (ss. 50–51) — Sadiq may **stop them** by notice to the railway (s. 52). If Rehman had already **endorsed** the railway receipt to a bank as **pledge** for a loan, Sadiq's right is subject to the bank's rights (s. 53). Having stopped the goods, Sadiq should give Rehman **notice of resale**, then resell and claim any shortfall as damages (s. 54(2)).
:::

:::recap Chapter 11 in six lines
- Unpaid seller: whole price not paid or tendered, or negotiable instrument dishonoured (s. 45); includes persons in the seller's position.
- Rights against goods: lien, stoppage in transit, resale; withholding delivery where property has not passed (s. 46).
- Lien: no credit / credit expired / buyer insolvent (s. 47); on remainder after part delivery (s. 48); lost by delivery to carrier without reservation, buyer's lawful possession, waiver — not by decree (s. 49).
- Stoppage: buyer insolvent, goods in transit (ss. 50–51 — seven rules); by possession or notice (s. 52).
- Sub-sale / pledge by buyer: no effect unless seller assents, except transfer of a document of title to a bona fide transferee for value (s. 53).
- Resale: not rescission by lien alone; perishable or after notice — damages, no profit to buyer; without notice — no damages, buyer gets profit; second buyer's good title; express reservation rescinds (s. 54).
:::

# Chapter 12 · Suits for breach and miscellaneous rules (SGA ss. 55 to 66)

## 12.1 The seller's remedies against the buyer

1. **Suit for the price — s. 55.** (1) Where **property has passed** and the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to pay; or (2) where the price is payable **on a day certain irrespective of delivery**, even though property has not passed and the goods have not been appropriated.
2. **Damages for non-acceptance — s. 56.** Where the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to **accept and pay**. The measure, under Contract Act s. 73, is ordinarily the difference between the **contract price** and the **market price** at the time and place fixed for acceptance (illus. (c) to s. 73).
3. **Repudiation before the due date — s. 60.** The seller may treat the contract as **subsisting** and wait, or treat it as **rescinded** and sue for damages at once (anticipatory breach — *Hochster v. De la Tour*).
4. **Interest — s. 61.** In the absence of a contrary contract, the court may award interest on the price from the date of **tender of the goods** or the date the price was payable (s. 61(2)(a)) (see the riba note in Topic 3, Chapter 12).
5. **Charges for care and custody** where the buyer delays taking delivery (s. 44).

## 12.2 The buyer's remedies against the seller

1. **Damages for non-delivery — s. 57.** Ordinarily the difference between the **market price** at the time and place of delivery and the **contract price** (illus. (a) to s. 73).
2. **Specific performance — s. 58.** "Subject to the provisions of **Chapter II of the Specific Relief Act, 1877**, in any suit for breach of contract to deliver **specific or ascertained goods**, the Court may, if it thinks fit, on the application of the plaintiff, by its decree direct that the contract shall be performed specifically, **without giving the defendant the option of retaining the goods on payment of damages**." Granted where damages are not adequate — e.g., **unique** goods (an antique, a rare manuscript, a special machine).
3. **Breach of warranty — s. 59.** The buyer cannot reject for breach of warranty, but may (a) **set it up in diminution or extinction of the price**, or (b) **sue for damages**; and setting up the breach in diminution does not prevent him from suing for **further** damage.
4. **Repudiation before due date — s. 60.**
5. **Refund and interest — s. 61.** Recovery of money paid where the **consideration has failed** (e.g., *Rowland v. Divall*), with interest from the date of payment (s. 61(2)(b)); **special damages** where recoverable by law (s. 61(1); Contract Act s. 73 second limb).
6. **Rejection** of goods for breach of **condition** (ss. 12–17, 37), and a suit for recovery of the price paid.

## 12.3 Auction sales — s. 64

{w:8,92}
|s. 64|Rule|
|(1)|Goods put up in **lots** — each lot is prima facie a **separate contract**.|
|(2)|The sale is **complete** on the **fall of the hammer** or other customary announcement; until then, **any bidder may retract** his bid (the bid is an offer — Topic 3, Chapter 2).|
|(3)|A right to bid may be **reserved expressly** by or on behalf of the seller; then the seller or **one person** on his behalf may bid.|
|(4)|If not notified as subject to a right to bid, the seller may **not bid** himself or employ anyone to bid, nor may the auctioneer knowingly take such bids; a sale contravening this may be treated as **fraudulent** by the buyer.|
|(5)|The sale may be notified to be subject to a **reserved or upset price**.|
|(6)|If the seller uses **pretended bidding** ("puffing") to raise the price, the sale is **voidable** at the buyer's option.|

## 12.4 Other rules

1. **Increase or decrease of duty or tax — s. 64A.** If customs or excise duty, or **sales tax** (under the Sales Tax Act 1951, as the Explanation states), is imposed, increased, decreased or remitted after the contract (and the contract is silent), the seller may **add** the increase to the price, and the buyer may **deduct** the decrease.
2. **Reasonable time** is a question of **fact** (s. 63).
3. **Exclusion** of implied terms by agreement, course of dealing or usage (s. 62).
4. **Savings — s. 66.** Existing rights, insolvency rules and non-inconsistent rules of law are saved; and "the provisions of this Act relating to contracts of sale **do not apply** to any transaction in the form of a contract of sale which is intended to operate by way of **mortgage, pledge, charge or other security**" (s. 66(3)).

- Seller: price (s. 55 — property passed, or price due on a day certain); damages for non-acceptance (s. 56); anticipatory breach (s. 60); interest (s. 61); custody charges (s. 44).
- Buyer: damages for non-delivery (s. 57); specific performance of specific / ascertained goods (s. 58); warranty — diminution of price or damages (s. 59); refund on failure of consideration and interest (s. 61); rejection for breach of condition.
